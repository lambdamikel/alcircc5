\title{A Repaired Split-Forest Automata Proof of Decidability for $\mathcal{ALCI}_{\RCC}$\\
\large Patchwork Bags, Exact Occurrence Identity, and Two-Way Parity Checking}
\author{Prepared as a consolidated proof manuscript}
\date{May 31, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note gives a repaired proof scheme for decidability of concept satisfiability for
$\mathcal{ALCI}$ whose roles are the five atomic RCC5 relations
$\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO,\DR$, interpreted over abstract complete atomic RCC5 frames with
strong equality.  The proof keeps the split-forest idea as the semantic normal form, but
it replaces the earlier transformer-based finite abstraction by a patchwork-bag
abstraction.  The key change is that global RCC5 coherence is no longer derived from
route-dependent pair and triple transformers.  Instead, each bag carries a finite complete
RCC5 network, adjacent bags agree on overlaps, and global consistency follows from the
patchwork property of RCC5.  Equality is treated as exact occurrence identity in the tree
decomposition, not as a quotient of repeated blocking profiles.  Universal restrictions
are checked by relation-set shadows, and infinite vertical eventualities are handled by a
two-way alternating parity tree automaton.  This is a proof for abstract composition-table
RCC5 frames, not for topological RCC5 semantics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and relation to the original split-forest idea}

The original split-forest idea remains the essential model-theoretic normal form.  Its
purpose is not to compute the Hasse diagram of an arbitrary semantic RCC5 model.  Rather,
it says that after witness thinning one may work with a generated containment
presentation: selected $\PP/\PPI$ witnesses are represented vertically, selected
$\DR/\PO$ witnesses are represented in finite side contexts, and multiple incomparable
proper-superpart witnesses are handled by split occurrences connected through explicit
identity ports.

The part that has changed is the finite abstraction layer.  Earlier drafts attempted to
recover the global relation $\rho(x,y)$ by pushing pair and triple states through
interface transformers.  That creates a coherence problem: computing a pair from its
least common interface and computing the same pair as a projection of a higher triple
need not agree unless a nontrivial confluence theorem is proved.  The present proof avoids
that issue.  The finite object is a tree decomposition by \emph{patchwork bags}.  Each bag
is a finite complete RCC5 network.  The only required compatibility between bags is
agreement on shared occurrences.  Global RCC5 consistency then follows from the RCC5
patchwork theorem.  Thus the split forest supplies the normal form, while the automaton
checks existence of a regular labelled tree of finite patchwork bags.

This gives the following theorem spine.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.7cm,style=nextline]
\item[Theorem A.] Every satisfiable concept has a witness-generated saturated
split-forest presentation.
\item[Theorem B.] Saturated split forests are exactly represented, up to the closure of the
input concept, by finite patchwork-bag abstractions.
\item[Theorem C.] A finite two-way alternating parity tree automaton accepts exactly the
patchwork-bag abstractions satisfying the input concept.
\end{description}
The decidability theorem follows from the decidability of emptiness for finite two-way
alternating parity tree automata.

\section{RCC5 frames and the two external finite-model facts}

\subsection{The abstract frame semantics}

Let
\[
\Rel=\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO,\DR\}.
\]
An \emph{abstract complete atomic RCC5 frame} is a nonempty set $\Delta$ together with a
map
\[
\rho:\Delta^2\to \Rel
\]
such that for all $x,y,z\in\Delta$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item $\rho(x,y)=\EQ$ iff $x=y$;
\item $\rho(y,x)=\rho(x,y)^{-1}$, where $\PP^{-1}=\PPI$, $\PPI^{-1}=\PP$, and
$\EQ,\PO,\DR$ are self-converse;
\item $\rho(x,z)\in\Comp(\rho(x,y),\rho(y,z))$, where $\Comp$ is the RCC5 composition
operation.
\end{enumerate}
We use the standard RCC5 composition table:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\Comp & \EQ & \PP & \PPI & \PO & \DR\\\hline
\EQ & \{\EQ\} & \{\PP\} & \{\PPI\} & \{\PO\} & \{\DR\}\\
\PP & \{\PP\} & \{\PP\} & \Rel & \{\PP,\PO,\DR\} & \{\DR\}\\
\PPI & \{\PPI\} & \{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO\} & \{\PPI\} & \{\PPI,\PO\} & \{\PPI,\PO,\DR\}\\
\PO & \{\PO\} & \{\PP,\PO\} & \{\PPI,\PO,\DR\} & \Rel & \{\PPI,\PO,\DR\}\\
\DR & \{\DR\} & \{\PP,\PO,\DR\} & \{\DR\} & \{\PP,\PO,\DR\} & \Rel
\end{array}
\]
An interpretation of $\mathcal{ALCI}_{\RCC}$ consists of such a frame and an assignment of
concept names to subsets of $\Delta$.  The five RCC5 relations are ordinary roles:
\[
\calI,x\models \exists R.C\quad\text{iff}\quad
\text{there is }y\in\Delta\text{ with }\rho(x,y)=R\text{ and }\calI,y\models C,
\]
and similarly for universal restrictions.  Since $\EQ$ is identity, $\exists\EQ.C$ and
$\forall\EQ.C$ are equivalent to $C$; below we normalize them away.

\subsection{Patchwork for complete RCC5 networks}

The proof uses one algebraic fact about RCC5.

\begin{theorem}[RCC5 patchwork property]\label{thm:patchwork}
Let $N_1,N_2$ be finite complete atomic RCC5 networks over variable sets $V_1,V_2$.
Assume that each $N_i$ is satisfiable as an abstract RCC5 network and that $N_1$ and
$N_2$ agree on the common variables $V_1\cap V_2$.  Then the union network on
$V_1\cup V_2$ is satisfiable.  The same holds for any finite tree-shaped family of
finite complete RCC5 networks agreeing on pairwise overlaps.  Moreover, any countable
tree-shaped family has a countable abstract completion all of whose finite restrictions
are patchwork unions of finitely many bags.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof indication]
The two-network statement is the usual patchwork property of atomic RCC5.  The finite
family statement follows by induction over the tree of bags, repeatedly amalgamating one
leaf bag with the already amalgamated remainder.  For a countable tree of bags, every
finite set of variables is contained in the union of finitely many bags after adding the
finite connecting subtree.  That finite restriction is satisfiable by the finite
patchwork property.  Since the relation alphabet is finite, a standard compactness/Konig
argument yields a labelling of all pairs in the countable union whose every finite
restriction is one of these satisfiable finite patchworks.  This is an abstract
composition-table argument; it does not claim topological representability.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:patchwork} is precisely what replaces the earlier transformer-coherence
lemma.  No relation in the global model is computed by following an interface route.  It
is supplied by a patchwork completion of locally consistent complete bag networks.

\subsection{Two-way alternating parity tree automata}

We also use the standard fact that emptiness for finite two-way alternating parity tree
automata over finite ranked alphabets is decidable.  Two-way motion is convenient because
it allows the automaton to revisit ancestor and sibling interfaces instead of relying on
lossless one-way shadows.  The use of two-way motion is only in the decision layer; the
semantic normal form remains a split-forest presentation.

\section{Syntax, closure, types, and witness thinning}

Put all concepts in negation normal form.  For each NNF concept $D$, write $\overline D$
for its NNF complement.  Let $\Cl(C_0)$ be the least finite set containing $C_0$, closed
under subformulas, under $D\mapsto\overline D$, and with $\exists\EQ.D,\forall\EQ.D$
replaced by $D$.

\begin{definition}[Hintikka type]
A type over $\Cl(C_0)$ is a set $t\subseteq\Cl(C_0)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(T\arabic*)]
\item exactly one of $D,\overline D$ is in $t$ for each $D\in\Cl(C_0)$;
\item $D\sqcap E\in t$ iff $D,E\in t$, and $D\sqcup E\in t$ iff $D\in t$ or $E\in t$;
\item the EQ-normalized clauses $\exists\EQ.D\leftrightarrow D$ and
$\forall\EQ.D\leftrightarrow D$ are respected when such formulas occur before
normalization.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\mathsf{Typ}$ be the finite set of all such types.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Witness-generated submodel]\label{lem:thin}
If $\calI,a_0\models C_0$, then there is a subinterpretation $\calI\upharpoonright W$ with
$a_0\in W$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $W$ is generated from $a_0$ by choosing, for every $a\in W$ and every
$\exists R.D\in\Cl(C_0)$ true at $a$, one witness $b$ with $\rho(a,b)=R$ and
$\calI,b\models D$;
\item for every $a\in W$ and every $D\in\Cl(C_0)$,
\[
\calI,a\models D\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \calI\upharpoonright W,a\models D .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $W$ be the least subset containing $a_0$ and all selected witnesses for existential
closure formulas at already generated elements.  The proof is by induction on $D\in
\Cl(C_0)$.  Atomic and Boolean cases are immediate.  For $D=\exists R.E$, a witness in
$\calI$ is retained by construction.  Conversely, any witness in the restricted model is
also a witness in $\calI$.  For $D=\forall R.E$, the forward direction is inherited by
restriction.  For the reverse direction, suppose $a\not\models_\calI\forall R.E$.
Then $a\models_\calI\exists R.\overline E$.  Since $\Cl(C_0)$ is closed under NNF
complement, the selected witness for $\exists R.\overline E$ lies in $W$, and by the
induction hypothesis it still satisfies $\overline E$ in the restricted model.  Hence the
universal fails there as well.
\end{proof}

\section{Saturated split-forest presentations}

The split-forest presentation is a normal form for witness-generated models.  It is not a
Hasse diagram of an arbitrary original model.  It is a generated containment presentation.

\begin{definition}[Generated containment skeleton]
A generated containment skeleton is a forest-like directed structure whose vertical edges
are selected $\PPI$-cover edges downward, equivalently selected $\PP$-cover edges upward.
The semantic $\PP$ and $\PPI$ relations along a branch are the strict ancestor and strict
descendant closures of these selected cover edges.  The skeleton may have infinite rays.
Blocking repetitions are presentation repetitions, not semantic equality.
\end{definition}

A node may need several incomparable selected $\PP$-superpart witnesses.  Rather than
assigning several parents to one tree node, the presentation uses a tree decomposition of
finite bags.  The same semantic occurrence may be present in several bags through shared
ports; the occurrence is one vertex of the decomposition, not two vertices related by a
nontrivial $\EQ$ edge.

\begin{definition}[Patchwork bag]
A patchwork bag is a finite object
\[
B=(P_B,N_B,\tau_B,\iota_B,W_B,U_B,E_B)
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(B\arabic*)]
\item $P_B$ is a finite set of local port positions, drawn from a fixed finite set
$\{1,\ldots,K\}$;
\item $N_B:P_B^2\to\Rel$ is a complete atomic RCC5 network satisfying the composition
table on all triples in $P_B$ and with $N_B(p,q)=\EQ$ iff $p=q$;
\item $\tau_B(p)\in\mathsf{Typ}$ is the concept type of $p$;
\item $\iota_B$ records overlap maps to the parent and children of the bag; overlap maps
are partial injections preserving $N_B$ and $\tau_B$;
\item $W_B$ records local witnesses for existential restrictions, either as a port in the
same bag or as a declared vertical request to be fulfilled by parity;
\item $U_B$ records relation-set universal obligations carried by live occurrences and
shadows;
\item $E_B$ records exact occurrence identity through overlaps.  There is no nontrivial
semantic $\EQ$ between distinct ports; identity means that two port occurrences are the
same vertex of the tree decomposition by the equivalence relation generated by overlap
maps.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Saturated side context]
A side context for a source occurrence $u$ is saturated if it is the least finite context
closed under the following operations, up to the rank bound described below.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*)]
\item Insert every selected $\DR$ or $\PO$ witness of $u$.
\item Insert the vertical boundary of $u$ needed to evaluate relations to its parent,
children, selected superparts, selected subparts, and shared identity ports.
\item For every side/tree or side/side pair whose inherited relation is $\PP$, $\PPI$, or
$\EQ$, replace the pair by an explicit local vertical stratum or shared identity port.
Thus such a pair is never left as an unstructured residual frontier pair.
\item Apply the same operation recursively to side witnesses, with strictly smaller rank
measured by the remaining modal depth below the triggering existential formula.
\item Only after this saturation may a residual frontier pair be classified as $\DR$ or
$\PO$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Finite saturation bound]\label{lem:finite-saturation}
For fixed $C_0$ there is a computable number $K(C_0)$ such that every rank-$r$ saturated
side context needed for $C_0$ has a representative using at most $K(C_0)$ local ports,
up to equality of types, overlap pattern, finite RCC5 bag network, witness declarations,
and universal-obligation summaries.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $n=|\Cl(C_0)|$ and let $T=|\mathsf{Typ}|\le 2^n$.  For a local context of width at
most $k$, the number of complete atomic RCC5 networks on its ports is at most
$5^{k^2}$, the number of type assignments is at most $T^k$, and the number of witness
and obligation summaries is bounded by a power set of a set of size polynomial in
$k,n,5$.  Hence, for every fixed $k$, there are finitely many context shapes.

Define $K_r$ inductively as the number of equivalence classes of rank-$r$ context shapes,
where rank $0$ contains only the source, its finite vertical boundary, and direct
witnesses whose targets have no further modal obligations.  For rank $r+1$, a context
shape is determined by: the source type; a finite bag network on a live frontier; one
direct witness declaration for each existential formula in $\Cl(C_0)$; one threshold
verticalization position for each pair of live or side ports whose composition forces
$\PP,\PPI$, or $\EQ$; and one recursive rank-$r$ subcontext class for each recursive
trigger.  Since each of these choices ranges over a finite set determined by $K_r$ and
$n$, there are finitely many classes.  Let $K(C_0)$ be the maximum size of a chosen
representative for all ranks $r\le n$.  This is an effective bound because all classes are
enumerable: enumerate all finite labelled bags up to size $m$, check local RCC5
consistency, saturation closure, overlap compatibility, and increase $m$ until every
newly generated rank-$r$ class has a representative.  The induction guarantees
termination at each rank.  No numerical recurrence is used; only effective finiteness of
the quotient is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A: split-forest normal form]\label{thm:A}
If $C_0$ is satisfiable over abstract complete atomic RCC5 frames, then $C_0$ is
satisfiable in a witness-generated saturated split-forest patchwork presentation of width
at most $K(C_0)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Start with a satisfying model and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:thin}.  Use the selected
$\PP/\PPI$ witnesses to form generated vertical cover links.  When one semantic element
requires several incomparable selected superparts, introduce several bags sharing the
same occurrence through overlap ports rather than identifying distinct copies by a
semantic $\EQ$ edge.  Insert all selected $\DR/\PO$ witnesses into side contexts and
saturate them by (S1)--(S5).  The construction is rank-decreasing on recursive side
contexts, hence terminates in one of the finitely many context shapes of
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-saturation}.  Each finite bag is labelled by the restriction of the
original complete RCC5 network, so every bag is locally satisfiable and adjacent bags
agree on their shared occurrences.  The resulting presentation is a tree decomposition by
finite complete networks, with exact occurrence identity represented by connected overlap
classes.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite patchwork abstraction}

The finite abstraction deliberately avoids pair and triple transformers.  Its alphabet
contains finite complete bag networks and overlap maps.  Global consistency is a patchwork
consequence.

\subsection{Shadows and universal obligations}

A universal restriction at a source occurrence must apply to every target occurrence in
the final global network.  The automaton can check this with two-way motion, but the bag
labels still record finite summaries called shadows.

\begin{definition}[Shadow profile]
For a bag $B$, a shadow profile is a tuple
\[
\sigma=(t,\vec r,\Omega)
\]
where $t\in\mathsf{Typ}$ is the type of the source represented by the shadow, $\vec r$ is
the vector of RCC5 relations from that source to the live ports of $B$, and
\[\Omega\subseteq \Rel\times\Cl(C_0)\]
is the set of universal obligations carried by the source: $(R,D)\in\Omega$ means that
any target currently known to be in relation $R$ to the source must have $D$ in its type.
Two shadows with the same triple impose exactly the same constraints on every extension
of the current bag.  The finite set of shadow profiles over width $K$ has cardinality at
most
\[
|\mathsf{Typ}|\cdot 5^K\cdot 2^{5|\Cl(C_0)|}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Relation-set update]
When moving from a bag $B$ to an adjacent bag $B'$ across overlap $O$, a shadow
$(t,\vec r,\Omega)$ may be updated to $(t,\vec r',\Omega)$ if the combined finite network
on $O\cup P_{B'}$ assigns $\vec r'$ to the new live ports and agrees with $\vec r$ on
$O$.  For every new live port $p$ of $B'$ and every $(R,D)\in\Omega$, if $\vec r'(p)=R$
then $D\in\tau_{B'}(p)$ is required.  If the source type $t$ contains $\forall R.D$, then
$(R,D)$ is included in $\Omega$.  No obligation is discarded because an intermediate
relation temporarily leaves $R$; the exact relation vector at every visited bag is
carried explicitly.
\end{definition}

This repairs the earlier may-analysis.  An obligation is never pruned merely because some
composition entry is multivalued; it is evaluated against the relation actually recorded
in the current bag network or shadow vector.

\subsection{Patchwork abstraction}

\begin{definition}[Valid patchwork abstraction]\label{def:valid-abstraction}
A valid patchwork abstraction for $C_0$ is a finitely branching tree whose nodes are
labelled by patchwork bags of width at most $K(C_0)$ and satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
\item each bag network is a finite complete atomic RCC5 network with strong equality;
\item adjacent bags agree exactly on their overlap positions, including types,
relations, witness declarations that mention overlap positions, and shadow vectors;
\item the equivalence classes generated by overlap maps are connected subtrees; these
classes are the semantic occurrences of the final model;
\item all existential restrictions in a port type have either a local witness in the same
bag or a declared vertical parity request;
\item all universal obligations are present as shadows and are checked against every live
port introduced in every adjacent bag reached by shadow propagation;
\item saturated side-context closure holds: any side/tree or side/side pair whose local
network label is $\PP$, $\PPI$, or $\EQ$ is represented by a vertical stratum or shared
identity port before the pair may be forgotten as residual frontier;
\item residual frontier pairs are labelled only by $\DR$ or $\PO$;
\item vertical eventuality requests are attached to a concrete support branch and, if the
source occurrence is carried through an overlap, to the same overlap equivalence class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[B: finite abstraction adequacy]\label{thm:B}
For every $C_0$, saturated split-forest presentations of $C_0$ of width $K(C_0)$ are sound
and complete for valid patchwork abstractions in the following sense.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Every saturated split-forest presentation induces a valid patchwork abstraction.
\item Every valid patchwork abstraction has a global abstract complete RCC5 interpretation
that realizes all bag types, witnesses, universal restrictions, and vertical requests.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (a), label each decomposition bag by the restriction of the presentation's global RCC5
network, by the type of each occurrence, and by the selected witness/support declarations.
Since the presentation is saturated, side/tree and side/side containment or identity
pairs have already been verticalized or shared; residual frontier pairs are therefore
$\DR$ or $\PO$.  Universal shadows are obtained by taking, for every source occurrence
whose universal restrictions remain relevant outside the current bag, its type, its
relation vector to the live ports, and the set of obligations induced by its universal
formulas.  Since these objects range over the finite set specified above, equal shadows
can be merged.  The merge is a congruence: the update into an adjacent bag depends only on
$(t,\vec r,\Omega)$ and the finite adjacent bag network, not on the identity of the
forgotten source.

For (b), form the set $V$ of semantic occurrences as equivalence classes of local port
occurrences under the overlap maps.  For each bag $B$, the labelled network $N_B$ is a
finite complete satisfiable RCC5 network on the occurrences represented in that bag.
Adjacent bags agree on overlaps by (A2), and the overlap classes are connected by (A3).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:patchwork}, the tree of bag networks has a countable abstract RCC5
completion $\rho$ on $V$ whose restriction to every bag is exactly $N_B$.

Concept names are interpreted according to the bag types; this is well-defined because
bag types agree on overlaps.  Boolean clauses follow from Hintikka consistency.
Existential clauses are satisfied either by a same-bag witness, whose relation is read in
$N_B$, or by a vertical parity witness whose support branch is realized in the tree and
whose endpoint type contains the required concept.  Universal clauses hold by the shadow
condition: for a source occurrence $x$ with $\forall R.D$, the obligation $(R,D)$ is
carried in every shadow/profile representing $x$ outside its home bag.  Whenever a target
occurrence is introduced, the exact recorded relation from the source shadow to that
target is checked; if it is $R$, then $D$ is forced into the target type.  Since every
occurrence of $V$ is introduced in some bag and every source's shadow is propagated along
the unique connecting path in the decomposition tree, all targets are checked.  Finally,
strong equality is automatic: $\EQ$ occurs in a bag only on identical ports, and global
identity is generated by overlaps, not by quotienting distinct vertices.  Hence the
interpretation realizes the abstraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this repairs transformer coherence]
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} never defines $\rho(x,y)$ by an interface route.  It
therefore has no least-common-ancestor versus higher-triple coherence problem.  Pair and
triple consistency is supplied by the finite bag networks and the RCC5 patchwork theorem.
The diagnostic concept
\[
\exists\PP.(A\sqcap\neg B\sqcap\forall\PP.\neg B\sqcap\forall\PPI.\neg B\sqcap
\forall\PO.\neg B)\sqcap\exists\PP.B
\]
is rejected because any patchwork bag or amalgam containing the shared split child and
the two superpart witnesses must satisfy
$\Comp(\PPI,\PP)=\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO\}$, so $\DR$ is not available, and the remaining four
relations are exactly those forbidden by the first witness type.
\end{remark}

\section{The automaton}

Let $\Sigma_{C_0}$ be the finite set of all patchwork bags of width at most $K(C_0)$:
finite port sets, finite complete RCC5 networks, type assignments, overlap maps, witness
declarations, shadows, and request declarations.  Finiteness follows from finiteness of
$\Rel$, $\mathsf{Typ}$, $\Cl(C_0)$, and the bound $K(C_0)$.

\begin{definition}[Two-way alternating parity automaton $\APTA_{C_0}$]
The automaton $\APTA_{C_0}$ runs on ranked trees over $\Sigma_{C_0}$.  Its states are of
six kinds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*)]
\item local-bag states checking finite RCC5 consistency, type consistency, overlap
agreement, and saturation closure;
\item existential-witness states checking local witnesses and spawning vertical request
states for nonlocal $\PP/\PPI$ requests;
\item universal-shadow states carrying a shadow profile and moving two-way through the
decomposition tree to check every introduced target;
\item equality/identity states checking that overlap maps generate connected occurrence
classes and that no distinct local ports are labelled $\EQ$;
\item patchwork states checking that adjacent bag networks agree on their overlaps;
\item parity-request states for vertical eventualities.
\end{enumerate}
The transition relation is finite because every state mentions only a bounded port number,
a type in $\mathsf{Typ}$, a relation in $\Rel$, a formula in $\Cl(C_0)$, or a shadow
profile from the finite shadow set.
\end{definition}

The parity condition is a standard generalized Buchi condition converted to parity.  For
each vertical eventuality kind $e=(R,D)$ with $R\in\{\PP,\PPI\}$, the automaton creates a
request state when it sees $\exists R.D$ without a same-bag witness.  The request follows
the declared support branch.  It is fulfilled at the first occurrence whose recorded
relation to the source is $R$ and whose type contains $D$.  If the request crosses an
overlap, the source occurrence is the same overlap-equivalence class; there is no
possibility of deferring forever by bouncing between semantic equality mates, because
there are no nontrivial equality mates.  Generalized Buchi acceptance requires every
request generated infinitely often to be fulfilled infinitely often along the support
run; a standard indexing of the finitely many request kinds gives a finite parity index.

\begin{lemma}[Automaton soundness]\label{lem:auto-sound}
If $\APTA_{C_0}$ accepts an input tree, then the input tree is a valid patchwork
abstraction for $C_0$ with some root occurrence whose type contains $C_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The local-bag, overlap, identity, and patchwork states enforce (A1)--(A3).  Existential
states enforce (A4).  Universal-shadow states enforce (A5): the automaton carries the
full relation vector of the source shadow and checks the target type whenever the recorded
relation is one of the source's universal guards.  Saturation states enforce (A6)--(A7).
Parity-request states enforce (A8).  The initial condition requires a root port with
$C_0$ in its type.  Hence all clauses of Definition~\ref{def:valid-abstraction} hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Automaton completeness]\label{lem:auto-complete}
Every valid patchwork abstraction for $C_0$ is accepted by $\APTA_{C_0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose the accepting run that follows the witnesses, shadows, overlaps, and support
branches recorded in the abstraction.  All local checks succeed by validity.  Universal
shadow runs succeed because the abstraction's shadow updates carry the exact relation
vector into every adjacent bag.  Every vertical eventuality request has a declared support
branch reaching a witness of the required type, so the generalized Buchi condition is
satisfied.  Thus the parity run is accepting.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[C: automaton correctness]\label{thm:C}
For every concept $C_0$,
\[
L(\APTA_{C_0})\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
C_0\text{ has a valid patchwork abstraction.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemmas~\ref{lem:auto-sound} and~\ref{lem:auto-complete}.
\end{proof}

\section{Decidability}

\begin{theorem}[Decidability over abstract RCC5 frames]\label{thm:dec}
Concept satisfiability for $\mathcal{ALCI}_{\RCC}$ over abstract complete atomic RCC5
frames with strong equality is decidable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Given $C_0$, compute $\Cl(C_0)$, the finite type set $\mathsf{Typ}$, the finite width
bound $K(C_0)$, and hence the finite alphabet $\Sigma_{C_0}$ of patchwork bags.  Construct
$\APTA_{C_0}$.  By Theorems~\ref{thm:A},~\ref{thm:B}, and~\ref{thm:C}, $C_0$ is
satisfiable iff $L(\APTA_{C_0})$ is nonempty.  Emptiness for finite two-way alternating
parity tree automata is decidable.  Therefore satisfiability is decidable.
\end{proof}


\section{Expanded construction details}

This section spells out the finite objects used above.  The point is to make explicit
where the previous route-dependent transformer machinery has been removed.

\subsection{From a generated model to bags}

Given a witness-generated model $\calI\upharpoonright W$, choose for each generated
occurrence $x$ a home bag $B_x$.  The home bag contains $x$, all selected same-step
witnesses for existential formulas in $\tp(x)$ that are not handled by a vertical
request, and the bounded vertical boundary needed by the saturated side-context
construction.  If a selected witness $y$ lies in a side context of $x$ and some boundary
point $b$ has $\rho(y,b)\in\{\PP,\PPI,\EQ\}$, then the bag is enlarged by the threshold
position that witnesses that containment or identity interaction.  If the threshold
itself creates new side obligations, the rank decreases because it belongs to a proper
subformula of the formula that generated $y$.  Hence this recursive enlargement stops.

For multiple selected superparts, the same semantic occurrence is not duplicated as two
RCC5-distinct points.  Instead, it is represented by local ports in several bags joined
by overlap maps.  The equivalence class of those ports is one occurrence in the final
tree decomposition.  The connectedness condition on overlaps is the replacement for the
old typed-EQ quotient.  Thus the statement
\[
  p\text{ in }B \text{ and }q\text{ in }B'\text{ represent the same element}
\]
means that $p$ and $q$ are in the same overlap-connected component; it does not mean that
there are two distinct elements with an $\EQ$ edge between them.

\subsection{The finite alphabet}

Fix $K=K(C_0)$.  The alphabet $\Sigma_{C_0}$ is obtained by enumerating all tuples
\[
  (P,N,\tau,\mu,W,U,\Gamma)
\]
with $P\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}$, $N:P^2\to\Rel$, $\tau:P\to\mathsf{Typ}$, $\mu$ a finite
family of partial injective overlap maps to the parent and to the $K$ possible child
slots, $W$ a set of witness declarations, $U$ a set of shadow profiles, and $\Gamma$ a
set of vertical request declarations.  A tuple is retained in the alphabet only if the
following finite tests pass.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(L\arabic*)]
\item $N(p,p)=\EQ$ and $N(p,q)\ne\EQ$ for $p\ne q$.
\item $N(q,p)=N(p,q)^{-1}$.
\item For all $p,q,r\in P$, $N(p,r)\in\Comp(N(p,q),N(q,r))$.
\item The type assignment $\tau$ is Hintikka-consistent.
\item Every local existential witness declaration for $\exists R.D\in\tau(p)$ names a
port $q\in P$ with $N(p,q)=R$ and $D\in\tau(q)$.
\item Every nonlocal vertical request for $\exists R.D$ has $R\in\{\PP,\PPI\}$ and records
which support branch it follows.
\item For every universal formula $\forall R.D\in\tau(p)$, the shadow set contains the
source obligation $(R,D)$ for the occurrence represented by $p$ whenever $p$ is not kept
live in the adjacent bag being checked.
\item Saturation holds: if a side-context pair has label $\PP,\PPI$, or $\EQ$, the tuple
also contains the corresponding vertical stratum or overlap identification; otherwise it
is not admitted as a residual frontier tuple.
\end{enumerate}
The alphabet is finite because all components range over finite sets.  Importantly, the
alphabet does not contain a function that attempts to compute a far-away pair relation by
iteration.  It contains only finite complete networks on finite bags and finite summaries
of obligations.

\subsection{Overlap and running-intersection checks}

For an edge between a parent bag $B$ and a child bag $C$, the input label supplies a
partial injection
\[
  \mu_{B,C}:P_B\rightharpoonup P_C.
\]
The local transition checks:
\begin{align*}
  \tau_C(\mu(p)) &= \tau_B(p),\\
  N_C(\mu(p),\mu(q)) &= N_B(p,q)
\end{align*}
for all $p,q$ in the domain of $\mu$.  The automaton also checks that each local port is
introduced once and forgotten along a connected subtree.  This can be expressed by three
finite status bits for every port: introduced here, continued to a child, or forgotten
here.  Since the underlying input is a tree, these local bits enforce the
running-intersection property for each overlap-equivalence class.

This is the exact point at which equality-port congruence is repaired.  The old proof
needed to show that two distinct copies had the same relation to every third occurrence.
Here there are no two distinct copies.  There is one global occurrence whose local
manifestations form a connected subtree.  All bag networks containing that occurrence
agree on its relations to all other ports in the same bag by the overlap checks and
patchwork agreement.

\subsection{The patchwork construction in detail}

Let $T$ be an accepted labelled input tree.  Let $V_T$ be the quotient of all local port
occurrences $(t,p)$ by the equivalence relation generated by the overlap maps.  For every
node $t$, the bag label gives a finite complete network $N_t$ on the finite set
$P_t/{\sim}$ of global occurrences represented in that bag.  If two bags share an
occurrence, their networks agree on all relations inside the overlap.

For a finite set $F\subseteq V_T$, choose finitely many bags whose union contains $F$ and
close this set of bags under paths in the input tree.  The result is a finite subtree of
bags.  Repeatedly apply the two-bag patchwork theorem from the leaves inward.  This gives
a satisfiable complete RCC5 network on the union of all occurrences in those bags, hence
on $F$.  Therefore every finite subset of $V_T$ has at least one complete RCC5 labelling
extending all bag networks.  Since $\Rel$ is finite, the product space
$\Rel^{V_T\times V_T}$ is compact in the product topology.  The closed set of labellings
satisfying each finite bag constraint and each finite composition constraint has the
finite intersection property, hence is nonempty.  Any element of this intersection is the
global relation $\rho_T$.

This construction proves three facts that were previously only asserted:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*)]
\item $\rho_T$ is well-defined globally, because it is chosen as one complete labelling of
all pairs, not computed by a path.
\item Every global triple satisfies the RCC5 composition table, because each finite
triple is contained in some finite patchwork union and the compactness construction
preserves all finite composition constraints.
\item If a diagnostic composition is forced, such as $\PP;\DR=\DR$ or $\PPI;\PP\subseteq
\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO\}$, then every global completion respects that forced value or forced
exclusion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Universal propagation by exact relation vectors}

A universal source $x$ may be forgotten from a bag before a later target $y$ is
introduced.  To ensure that $\forall R.D$ at $x$ is not lost, the input carries a shadow
profile for $x$.  At a bag $B$, the shadow records the exact vector
\[
  (\rho_T(x,p))_{p\in P_B}
\]
whenever $x$ is not live.  In an accepted input this vector is not inferred from a single
composition step.  It is part of the finite label and is checked for consistency with the
parent and child bag networks.  When a child bag introduces a new port $q$, the child
label records $\rho_T(x,q)$ in the updated vector.  If this relation is $R$ and
$\forall R.D\in\tp(x)$, then $D$ is required in the type of $q$.

This is stronger than may-analysis.  A may-analysis would carry a set of possible
relations and might drop an obligation if an intermediate step leaves $R$.  Here the
actual recorded relation at the target is checked.  In an existential branch of the
automaton the run guesses the vectors; soundness follows because the guessed vectors are
part of the patchwork network and must be globally extendible.  Completeness follows by
choosing the vectors from an actual satisfying presentation.

\subsection{Vertical eventualities and equality transfer}

For $R\in\{\PP,\PPI\}$, a witness for $\exists R.D$ may lie arbitrarily far along a
vertical branch.  The automaton handles this by a parity request.  A request state
contains:
\[
  (R,D,p,\ell),
\]
where $p$ is the current manifestation of the source occurrence and $\ell$ is the support
branch declaration.  If the source occurrence passes through an overlap, the state updates
$p$ to the corresponding port under the overlap injection.  If the source is forgotten,
its shadow is used to keep the relation vector.  A request is fulfilled only when the run
visits a port $q$ with recorded relation $R$ from the source and $D\in\tau(q)$.

Because occurrence identity is overlap identity, a request cannot be deferred by jumping
between different equality mates.  There are no equality mates outside the connected
manifestations of the same global occurrence.  The parity condition is therefore the
standard one: for the finite set of request kinds $(R,D)$, require that each request kind
that is pending infinitely often is fulfilled infinitely often.  Converting the resulting
generalized Buchi condition to a parity condition gives index at most
$2\cdot |\{(R,D):R\in\{\PP,\PPI\},D\in\Cl(C_0)\}|+1$.

\section{Limitations and scope}

The theorem is for abstract complete atomic RCC5 frames satisfying the composition table.
It does not assert decidability for topological RCC5 role semantics.  The external
algebraic input is the RCC5 patchwork property.  If one wants a fully self-contained
paper, Theorem~\ref{thm:patchwork} should either be proved in an appendix from a standard
homogeneous representation of RCC5 or cited with a precise reference.
