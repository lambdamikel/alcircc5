
You are an expert referee in description logics, modal and temporal
logics, and qualitative spatial reasoning (RCC5/RCC8), asked to
adversarially review a decidability proof for correctness. You have no
prior involvement with this work.

THE MANUSCRIPTS. Attached is a revision stack of three documents proving
that concept satisfiability for ALCI_RCC5 -- the description logic ALCI
whose five roles are the RCC5 base relations DR, PO, EQ, PP, PPI,
interpreted over abstract complete atomic RCC5 frames (every pair of
domain elements carries exactly one base relation, closed under the RCC5
composition table), with strong equality (EQ = identity) -- is decidable.

  (1) The BASE manuscript: split-forest normal form (Theorem A), a finite
      patchwork-bag abstraction (Theorem B), and a two-way alternating
      parity tree automaton (Theorem C), deciding via emptiness.
  (2) A first DELTA that amends the abstraction layer of (1): it
      restricts the "shadow" mechanism to horizontal relations and proves
      an amalgamation lemma for that restricted language.
  (3) A second DELTA that replaces most of (2): it withdraws the
      horizontal restriction and instead treats every universal source as
      a virtual active occurrence at every bag ("active virtual bags",
      AV1-AV6), with finite-restriction closure conditions, and
      re-proves amalgamation, coverage, truth, and extraction in that
      form.

The EFFECTIVE candidate proof is: (1), as amended by the surviving parts
of (2) (in particular its width-accounting section), as amended by (3).
Where (2) and (3) conflict, (3) governs; (3) states explicitly which
clauses of (2) it withdraws.

YOUR TASK. Determine whether the effective candidate proof is correct.
Two separate questions:
  1. Is the decidability THEOREM true?
  2. Does the effective stack actually PROVE it -- or are there gaps,
     unjustified steps, or claims that do not follow?
A theorem can be true while its proof is broken; address both.

HOW TO WORK -- concrete and adversarial:
  - First verify the RCC5 composition table (base manuscript, Section 2)
    independently, then take it as ground truth.
  - Identify the load-bearing lemmas of (3) and check each: proved, or
    asserted? Pay particular attention to:
      * every place where an infinite object (an infinite active set, an
        infinite tree, an infinite family of constraints) is handled by
        finite conditions -- check the finite conditions actually entail
        the infinite property used later;
      * the interaction of the three layers: does (3) really discharge
        everything it withdraws from (2), and does anything in (1) or (2)
        silently depend on a withdrawn clause?
      * the automaton layer: the abstraction of (3) carries data that is
        not part of any finite bag label; check that the described
        finite-state machinery (alternating run copies / monitors) can
        actually enforce all validity conditions, including for objects
        first introduced far apart in the input tree, and that the
        alphabet and state space are genuinely finite;
      * all width/finiteness accounting: if a quantity is claimed
        bounded, verify the bound argument; try to make it unbounded
        with a fixed satisfiable concept;
      * the eventuality/parity layer: can an accepted run defer a
        required witness forever?
  - Scrutinise BOTH directions of the finite-abstraction theorem:
    realization (valid abstraction => model) AND extraction (satisfiable
    concept => valid abstraction of bounded width). Historically,
    repairs that strengthen one direction shift the failure to the other.
  - Try to construct explicit counterexamples: an unsatisfiable concept
    the procedure would accept; a satisfiable concept no valid
    abstraction of the claimed width can represent; or an abstract
    configuration meeting all stated validity clauses that falsifies a
    lemma's conclusion. Small, fixed concepts are best.
  - Check strong equality end-to-end: nothing may force EQ between
    distinct occurrences, and blocking/repetition must not be conflated
    with identity.

REPORT FORMAT: referee-style. (a) Verdict (defect found / gap / could not
break it), with confidence. (b) Severity-graded findings, each with exact
location, why it does not follow, and -- wherever possible -- a concrete
witness (concept or configuration). (c) Steps checked and found sound.
Be explicit about what you verified by computation-in-principle versus
read-through. Do not defer to the authors' assertions: if a step is
"clear" or "immediate" in the text but not to you, that is a finding.
