PRIMARY PROMPT  (paste verbatim)
================================================================

You are an expert referee in description logics, qualitative spatial reasoning
(RCC5/RCC8), and automata on infinite trees, asked to adversarially review a
proof for correctness.  You have no prior involvement with this work.

THE MANUSCRIPT.  Attached is a proof (with a companion paper organising it into
three theorems A/B/C) that concept satisfiability in the description logic
ALCI_RCC5 -- ALCI extended with the five RCC5 base relations DR, PO, EQ, PP, PPI
as roles, under abstract complete atomic RCC5 composition-table semantics with
strong equality -- is decidable.  The strategy: thin any satisfying model to a
witness-generated split forest (Theorem A); abstract that split forest by a
finite system of frontier profiles, pair/triple product-state representatives,
and equality ports (Theorem B); and decide satisfiability as non-emptiness of a
finite alternating parity tree automaton that accepts exactly the valid finite
abstractions (Theorem C).

YOUR TASK.  Determine whether the proof is correct.  Distinguish two questions:
  1. Is the decidability THEOREM true over this abstract RCC5 semantics?
  2. Does the manuscript actually PROVE it -- or are there load-bearing claims
     that are asserted, sketched, or do not in fact follow?

HOW TO WORK -- be concrete and adversarial:
  - First recompute the RCC5 composition table yourself by subset enumeration
    (a few lines of code) and check the manuscript's table; composition is
    load-bearing.
  - The heart of any such proof is the FINITE ABSTRACTION step (Theorem B
    here).  Interrogate it hard.  In the weak occurrence model EVERY pair of
    occurrences has an RCC5 relation, and many forced relations are *indirect*
    -- a witness of a witness, or an equality-split mate, can be a
    composition-forced target of a far-away ancestor.  Check precisely:
      * Is there a finite, inductively-defined representative for EVERY pair and
        EVERY triple of occurrences in the (generally infinite) unfolded tree --
        not just for selected witnesses or "nearby" occurrences?  Or does a key
        claim quietly quantify over all global triples while calling itself a
        finite check?
      * Universal restrictions: if forall R.D is in the type of u and the global
        relation between u and some v is R, is D *forced* into the type of v --
        for EVERY such v, including ones reached only by composition through
        intermediate witnesses?  Is that proved, or only checked on selected
        targets?
      * Equality: is rho(u,v) = EQ enforced if and only if u and v are in the
        same equality class, with the congruence proved against ALL third
        occurrences (not only local ones)?
      * Is the automaton a genuinely finite object -- a total transition
        function over a finite alphabet/state set with a stated parity/priority
        condition -- and does every non-idle child recursively inherit all
        well-formedness obligations?  (If it makes parent moves, is the
        two-way alternating tree-automaton emptiness theorem invoked?)
      * Is the claimed finite bound on profile/frontier size actually proved, or
        is it a counting heuristic?
  - Try to BREAK it with concrete witnesses.  Construct small ALCI_RCC5 concepts
    and trace whether the abstraction/automaton gives the right answer.  The
    most convincing defect is a small UNSATISFIABLE concept that the
    construction would ACCEPT because a composition-forced relation or universal
    target is not represented.  Worth trying, among others:
        exists PP.(exists DR.A) and forall DR.not A          (unsat?)
        exists PP.(A and not B and forall PP.not B and forall PPI.not B
                   and forall PO.not B) and exists PP.B        (unsat?)
        exists DR.(A and exists DR.B)                         (recursive child obligations)
        exists PP.top and forall PP.exists PP.top             (infinite PP-chain; no EQ collapse)
    For each, show the composition arithmetic and say exactly where in the
    construction the forced relation / universal / recursive obligation is (or
    is not) represented and checked.
  - Do not manufacture defects.  Confirming a step is sound is as valuable as
    finding it unsound.  A clean bill of health -- "I tried hard to break the
    finite-abstraction theorem and could not, here is what I checked" -- is a
    fully acceptable and valuable outcome.

DELIVERABLE -- a referee report:
  (a) one-paragraph verdict (correct / correct-modulo-fixable-gaps / genuine gap
      / unsound) with your confidence;
  (b) a severity-graded findings list (critical / major / minor); for each: the
      precise location, a self-contained statement of what is claimed, why it
      does not follow (or a counterexample concept with the composition
      calculation), and a candidate repair if you see one;
  (c) a short list of the steps you checked and found sound.

Show your composition calculations and witness constructions explicitly.

OPTIONAL FOCUSED PASS  (run ONLY if the primary pass came back clean)
================================================================

Follow-up, same manuscript: focus exclusively on the finite-abstraction theorem
(Theorem B / the pair-and-triple product-state representation).  State precisely
the inductive definition of the pair representative rep2(u,v) and the triple
representative rep3(u,v,w) from the nearest common interface, and check: (i)
every pair and every triple of occurrences in the infinite unfolded split tree
has a representative; (ii) the representative's labels equal the actual global
RCC5 labels; (iii) finiteness of the representative alphabet is proved from a
precise invariant, not assumed; and (iv) local checks of these finite
representatives genuinely imply global RCC5 composition AND universal coverage
to all composition-forced targets.  Try to construct a concept whose unfolding
forces a triple whose three endpoints share no bounded common interface, and
see whether it still has a finite representative.
