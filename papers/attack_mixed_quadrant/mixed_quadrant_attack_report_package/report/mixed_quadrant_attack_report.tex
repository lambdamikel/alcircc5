\begin{abstract}
The supplied attack packet isolates three open extraction problems for the mixed
$\exists\PO$/$\exists\PP$ quadrant of the $\forall\PO$-free fragment of
$\mathcal{ALCI}_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$. This report makes progress on all three. First, it gives a
finite simultaneous-rectangle theorem for finitely many interacting $\PP/\PPI$ towers.
Under the segment hypotheses stated in the packet, this proves the mathematical core
of Target B at paper level. Second, it proves that the proposed Target A label is always satisfiable but
constructs an eight-point ordered-disjoint countermodel showing that a common fresh
class-top need not be insertable. Blocking by model type alone is therefore insufficient;
a finite lower-context spectrum is a plausible replacement. Third, it refutes the natural
tail-stabilize/add-witness fixed point for Target C and factors the remaining problem into
postselection of uniform anchors followed by an ordered-disjoint PO-completion. The report
also records several defects in the supplied Python probes. All conclusions are scoped to
the abstract RCC5 composition-table semantics and the stated $\forall\PO$-free fragment.
No complete mixed-quadrant extraction or decidability theorem is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, assumptions, and result map}

We retain the packet's standing assumptions without alteration:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Models are total, converse-closed, composition-closed atomic RCC5 networks under
        abstract composition-table semantics; they need not be concrete region models.
  \item Strong equality is used: $\EQ$ is identity and occurs only on the diagonal.
  \item $x\,\PP\,y$ means that $x$ is a proper part of $y$; $\PPI$ is its converse.
  \item No formula $\forall\PO.C$ occurs in the closure. Thus a declared $\PO$ edge carries
        no universal obligation.
  \item The singleton cells used repeatedly are
        \begin{align*}
          \comp(\PP,\PP)&=\{\PP\}, &
          \comp(\PP,\DR)&=\{\DR\},\\
          \comp(\DR,\PPI)&=\{\DR\}, &
          \comp(\PPI,\PPI)&=\{\PPI\}.
        \end{align*}
  \item Ordered-disjoint frames are used as the RCC5 normal form: a strict partial order,
        a symmetric irreflexive downward-closed disjointness relation, and $\PO$ as the
        residual relation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evidence levels used in this report}

To avoid conflating proof, certification, and measurement, conclusions are labelled by
their actual evidence level:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Paper-level theorem or counterexample:} a general mathematical argument is
        supplied here, but it has not yet been translated into Lean.
  \item \textbf{Finite executable check:} a dependency-free Python artifact checks a
        concrete frame, composition-table calculation, or bounded family. It is not a
        proof of the corresponding unbounded statement.
  \item \textbf{Reported certified background:} the input packet says a result is
        Lean-certified. The Lean development itself was not included in this package and
        was not independently recompiled for this report.
  \item \textbf{Candidate reduction:} a bounded construction or invariant has a local
        argument but still lacks one or more global certificate obligations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Target B: finite simultaneous cross-kernel rectangles}

\statusbox{darkgreen}{Result: under the packet's fixed-family and recurrence hypotheses,
the finite off-diagonal phase rectangles needed by the certificate have a paper-level
proof. Infinite tail-by-tail stabilization is false and unnecessary.}

\subsection{The finite rectangle theorem}

Let $K=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ be finite. For each $k\in K$, let
\[
  c_k:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow M
\]
be a strict tower in one atomic RCC5 model. Its direction $d_k$ is either $\PP$ or $\PPI$:
\[
  R(c_k(n),c_k(n+1))=d_k.
\]
Assume, as in the packet's periodic extraction, that every tail contains two positions of
the same full model type.

\textbf{Theorem 2.1 (finite simultaneous rectangles).}
There are indices $s_k<t_k$ and atoms $q_{k\ell}$ for $k\neq\ell$ such that
\[
  \mty(c_k(s_k))=\mty(c_k(t_k))
\]
and
\[
  R(c_k(s_k+a),c_\ell(s_\ell+b))=q_{k\ell}
\]
for every $a<t_k-s_k$, every $b<t_\ell-s_\ell$, and every $k\neq\ell$.
The intervals may be chosen beyond any prescribed finite collection of lower bounds.

Thus contiguous recurrence segments suffice; no arbitrary subsequence selection is used.

\subsection{The transition ranks}

Fix a point $y$ and move $x_n$ along a strict tower. If
$R(x_n,x_{n+1})=d$, composition requires
\[
  R(x_n,y)\in\comp(d,R(x_{n+1},y)).
\]
For $d=\PP$, the allowed transition graph has the rank partition
\[
  \{\DR,\PP\}\;<\;\{\PO,\EQ\}\;<\;\{\PPI\};
\]
for $d=\PPI$, it has
\[
  \{\PPI\}\;<\;\{\PO,\EQ\}\;<\;\{\DR,\PP\}.
\]
Direct inspection of the RCC5 table shows that every allowed non-loop transition strictly
increases the displayed rank. There are no transitions between distinct atoms at the same
rank. Hence every relation sequence against a fixed point eventually stabilizes, with at
most two genuine atom changes. The analogous target-coordinate statement follows by
converse, or by using the corresponding right-composition transition relation.

\subsection{Two towers: stabilization of the row limits}

For two towers $c$ and $d$, set
\[
  R_{ij}=R(c(i),d(j)).
\]
One-sided stabilization gives, for each $i$, an eventual row value
\[
  \lambda(i)=\lim_{j\to\infty}R_{ij}.
\]
Choose a target index beyond the stabilization thresholds for both rows $i$ and $i+1$.
Applying composition to the triple $c(i),c(i+1),d(j)$ shows that
$\lambda(i),\lambda(i+1)$ obey the same transition graph as a relation to a fixed point.
The rank argument therefore yields
\[
  \exists I,q\;\forall i\geq I:\quad \lambda(i)=q.
\]

Choose any finite recurrence segment of $c$ beyond $I$. Each of its finitely many rows has
eventual value $q$, so moving $d$ beyond the maximum of their individual thresholds makes
the entire rectangle constant. A same-type recurrence interval for $d$ can then be chosen
inside that tail.

\subsection{Finite fusion}

Order the finitely many towers. When selecting tower $k$, place its complete recurrence
segment beyond:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item all outer row-limit stabilization bounds against towers not yet selected;
  \item all row thresholds imposed by the finitely many phases already selected;
  \item all stabilization thresholds imposed by the finite external set already fixed.
\end{enumerate}

Finite type recurrence supplies equal-type endpoints after this maximum. Later towers are
only restricted to farther tails, so previously obtained rectangles cannot be destroyed.
Induction over the finite tower list proves Theorem 2.1.

\subsection{Discharging the certificate obligations}

Define
\[
  \operatorname{phase}(k,a)=\mty(c_k(s_k+a)),\qquad
  Q(k,\ell)=q_{k\ell}.
\]
If $\forall r.C$ belongs to a source phase and $Q(k,\ell)=r$, rectangular constancy gives
relation $r$ from that source point to every target phase point. Model semantics therefore
places $C$ in every target phase, exactly as required by \texttt{kq\_all}.

Choose one representative from each selected segment. Because every macro relation is the
actual relation between representatives, the finite macro-network is an induced subnet of
the source model. Converse and composition closure, and hence the cross-kernel part of
\texttt{frame\_q}, follow immediately.

For distinct kernels the common rectangle atom cannot be $\EQ$ if recurrence gaps are
chosen at least two: each segment then contains two distinct points of a strict tower, while
a constant $\EQ$ rectangle would identify every point in both segments. Such recurrence
gaps are available because some model type occurs infinitely often in every tail. This
preserves strong equality at the macro level; coincident kernel traces may alternatively be
merged before defining $Q$.

Rectangular constancy is sufficient, not logically necessary. Labels may satisfy
\texttt{kq\_all} accidentally, and $Q(k,\ell)=\PO$ makes the implication vacuous in this
fragment.

\subsection{Why infinite rectangular stabilization is false}

Let
\[
  M=\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1\},\qquad
  (n,u)<(m,v)\Longleftrightarrow n<m,
\]
with empty disjointness. The ordered-disjoint network has
\[
R((i,0),(j,1))=
\begin{cases}
\PP,&i<j,\\
\PO,&i=j,\\
\PPI,&i>j.
\end{cases}
\]
Both coordinate sequences are ascending towers. Each individual row and column stabilizes,
but every product of two infinite cofinal subsequences contains both $\PP$ and $\PPI$.
Finite constant rectangles occur arbitrarily late: select one finite segment completely
above or below the other. This separates the true finite theorem from an invalid tail
theorem.

\subsection{Diagonal qualification}

The displayed definition of \texttt{kq\_all} in the packet lacks an explicit $k\neq k'$
guard. If this is literal, \texttt{frame\_q} gives $Q(k,k)=\EQ$, and \texttt{kq\_all} would
propagate the body of every $\forall\EQ.C$ from one phase to every phase. That is stronger
than equality semantics and stronger than the separately listed \texttt{kk\_eq}
obligation. The Lean definition should therefore be checked before the finite rectangle
theorem is installed: either the guard is present, or the diagonal obligation must be
changed explicitly.

\section{Target A: the class-top proposal fails}

\statusbox{darkred}{Result: the advertised class-top label is always satisfiable, but this
does not permit one fresh top to serve all same-type occurrences. A finite
ordered-disjoint counterexample refutes that inference.}

\subsection{The local satisfiability lemma}

Let $T=\mty_M(a)$, let $\exists\PP.D\in T$, and define
\[
  L_T=D\sqcap\bigwedge\{E:\forall\PP.E\in T\}.
\]
Choose any $w$ with $a\,\PP\,w$ and $M,w\models D$. Every
$\forall\PP.E\in T$ is true at $a$, so $M,w\models E$. Consequently
\[
  M,w\models L_T.
\]
This argument is valid in arbitrary relational semantics; the RCC5 table and the
$\forall\PO$-free restriction are not needed. The proposed ``is the label satisfiable?''
question is therefore settled positively but is too weak to solve the group problem.

\subsection{An eight-point counterexample}

Use concept names $P,Q,R,S$ and define
\[
  U_P=\forall\DR.P,\qquad U_Q=\forall\DR.Q,
\]
\[
  A=U_P\sqcup U_Q,\qquad D=\exists\DR.A.
\]
Let $C_0$ be the conjunction of
\[
  \exists\PP.D,\quad
  \exists\PO.R,\quad
  \exists\PO.S,\quad
  \exists\PPI.\top,\quad
  P\sqcup\neg P,\quad
  Q\sqcup\neg Q.
\]
This concept is in the mixed quadrant and contains no $\forall\PO$ formula.

Take the eight points
\[
  x_1,v_1,y_1,z_1,x_2,v_2,y_2,z_2.
\]
The strict order is exactly the two chains $x_i<v_i<y_i$, including their transitive
edges. Disjointness holds exactly between $z_i$ and the three points
$x_i,v_i,y_i$. Every other distinct incomparable, non-disjoint pair is $\PO$.
This is an ordered-disjoint frame and therefore induces a composition-closed RCC5
network.

Use the following valuation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll@{\qquad}ll}
\toprule
Point & True atoms & Point & True atoms \\
\midrule
$x_1$ & $P$       & $x_2$ & $Q,S$ \\
$v_1$ & $P,Q,R$   & $v_2$ & $P,Q,R$ \\
$y_1$ & $P,Q$     & $y_2$ & $P,Q$ \\
$z_1$ & $S$       & $z_2$ & none \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $\DR$-neighbours of $z_1$ are exactly $x_1,v_1,y_1$, all of which satisfy $P$;
thus $z_1\models U_P$. Since $x_1\not\models Q$, $z_1\not\models U_Q$. Symmetrically,
$z_2\models U_Q$ and $z_2\not\models U_P$. Hence $y_i\models D$, witnessed by $z_i$,
and $v_i\models\exists\PP.D$, witnessed by $y_i$.

A direct evaluation of all 22 syntactic subformulas of $C_0$ gives
\[
  \mty(v_1)=\mty(v_2)=T.
\]
The modal conjuncts at root $v_1$ also force the relevant lower contexts into the demand
closure:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Demand at $v_1$ & Unique witness \\
\midrule
$\exists\PP.D$ & $y_1$ \\
$\exists\PO.R$ & $v_2$ \\
$\exists\PO.S$ & $x_2$ \\
$\exists\PPI.\top$ & $x_1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus $v_2$ is a genuine blocked same-type occurrence, and $x_1,x_2$ are not irrelevant
ambient points. There is no positive $\forall\PP$ formula in $T$, so $L_T=D$, already
realized separately at $y_1$ and $y_2$.

\subsection{No common fresh top}

Assume that an extension contains a fresh $t$ with
\[
  v_1\,\PP\,t,\qquad v_2\,\PP\,t,\qquad t\models D.
\]
Choose $z$ such that $z\,\DR\,t$ and $z\models A$. Since $x_i\,\PP\,v_i\,\PP\,t$,
transitivity gives $t\,\PPI\,x_i$. The singleton cell
\[
  \comp(\DR,\PPI)=\{\DR\}
\]
therefore forces $z\,\DR\,x_1$ and $z\,\DR\,x_2$.

Boolean coherence at $z$ chooses one disjunct of $A$:

\begin{itemize}
  \item If $z\models\forall\DR.P$, then $P(x_2)$ is forced, contradicting
        $x_2\models\neg P$.
  \item If $z\models\forall\DR.Q$, then $Q(x_1)$ is forced, contradicting
        $x_1\models\neg Q$.
\end{itemize}

No common $D$-top exists in any label-preserving model or certificate extension. The same
argument applies if the $\DR$ witness for $t$ is represented by a kernel phase. Choosing
the full type of one existing witness does not help: the obstruction is exposed while
serving the nested demand $D$.

\subsection{A bounded context refinement}

For a source point $v$, define its strict lower type spectrum
\[
  \Delta_{<}(v)=\{\mty(x):x\,\PP\,v\}
\]
and block by
\[
  q(v)=(\mty(v),\Delta_{<}(v)).
\]
If $N$ is the number of full model types, at most $N2^N$ such contexts exist. With at most
$m\leq|\cl(C_0)|$ demands per context, the gate-child budget is at most $mN2^N$,
independent of occurrence count and model depth.

Equality of lower spectra gives a local gluing lemma. Let a group have common context
$(T,\Delta)$, choose a representative PP witness, and take a disjoint copy of a
witness-closed source gadget rooted there. Attach the copied upper cone above every group
down-cone, propagate copied disjointness through the forced $\PP\circ\DR$ cell, and assign
$\PO$ to all residual cross pairs. Every lower point has a type already present below the
representative, so all forced $\PP$ and $\DR$ universal-compatibility rectangles already
hold in the source model. Residual $\PO$ pairs carry no universal obligations.

This proves local frame preservation, cross-interface universal propagation, and internal
existential fulfilment when the copied gadget is witness-closed. It does not yet give a
finite certificate: the copied gadget may be infinite, and recursive context transitions
can cycle. A plausible next step is to compile acyclic context states as externals and
strict-PP strongly connected components as periodic kernels. The remaining proof must
establish uniform $\PPI/\DR$ witness rows and the cross-kernel obligations. Target A should
therefore be recorded as a bounded reduction, not as solved.

\section{Target C: the selection circularity is distinct}

\statusbox{darkamber}{Result: the natural tail-stabilize/add-witness iteration can diverge
even with one kernel. The certificate only needs constancy on selected phases, permitting
postselection of uniform witnesses and an anchored PO-completion.}

\subsection{A counterexample to the finite fixed point}

Use the concrete set-induced model
\[
  A_n=\{0,\ldots,n\},\qquad B_m=\{m+1\},\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{N}.
\]
Then $A_n\,\PP\,A_{n+1}$ and
\[
R(A_n,B_m)=
\begin{cases}
\DR,&n\leq m,\\
\PPI,&n\geq m+1.
\end{cases}
\]
Let
\[
  H=(\exists\PP.\top)\sqcap(\exists\DR.\top),\qquad
  C_0=H\sqcap\forall\PP.H.
\]
Every $A_n$ satisfies $C_0$. A $\DR$ witness for $A_n$ is a $B_m$ with $m\geq n$.
For fixed $B_m$, however, its row along the tower stabilizes only at $m+1$, after changing
from $\DR$ to $\PPI$. Hence no $n,m$ satisfy both
\[
  n\geq m+1\qquad\text{and}\qquad m\geq n.
\]

The iteration ``select beyond current external thresholds, add the selected segment's
witnesses, and reselect'' therefore chases
\[
  n<m<n'<m'<\cdots
\]
and has no finite fixed point. This is not a counterexample to finite certificates. A
postselected $B_m$ can be constant on a one-phase segment and can be assigned a constant
abstract row in the unfolding. The counterexample only refutes the unnecessarily strong
eventual-tail extraction discipline.

\subsection{Postselection and anchored PO-completion}

Suppose a kernel segment $p_0,\ldots,p_{q-1}$ has already been selected. If a certified
uniform witness $w$ satisfies
\[
  R(p_a,w)=r\quad\text{for every }a<q,
\]
then $w$ may be inserted after segment selection: this finite equality is exactly what the
certificate function $K$ requires. No stabilization against the unused tail is needed.

The following anchored PO-completion is a candidate local reduction. Partition uniform
anchors by their required kernel relation into $U_0$ ($\PP$), $L_0$ ($\PPI$), and $D_0$
($\DR$). In the external frame put
\[
  U=\uparrow U_0,\qquad L=\downarrow L_0,
\]
\[
  D=\downarrow D_0\;\cup\;\{x:\exists u\in U,\;x\,\DR\,u\}.
\]
Assign the macro-kernel relation $\PP$ on $U$, $\PPI$ on $L$, $\DR$ on $D$, and $\PO$ on
the residual points. Joint realization by any selected phase makes these sets compatible.
The four singleton cells
\[
  \PP\circ\PP,\quad \PPI\circ\PPI,\quad
  \PP\circ\DR,\quad \DR\circ\PPI
\]
show that every forced non-$\PO$ assignment agrees with the source model across all phases,
provided the anchors and their closure remain row-conservative. Full model-type labels then discharge the corresponding \texttt{ke\_all} and
\texttt{ek\_all} implications; residual $\PO$ rows are universally inert.

The unresolved issue is demand closure of the new anchors. If that closure remains
model-agreeing, the anchored completion applies recursively. Borrowed, non-model-agreeing
edges from Target A may violate the row-conservative invariant. The residue of C is thus a
finite closure problem closely related to A, rather than another simultaneous tower
stabilization theorem.

\subsection{Dependence on Target B}

Target B does not imply Target C: the preceding counterexample has one kernel, so the
cross-kernel rectangle problem is vacuous. Conversely, postselection of external witnesses
says nothing about $Q$ or \texttt{kq\_all}. A clean extraction order is therefore:
\begin{align*}
  &\boxed{\text{select a finite kernel macro-frame using Target B}}\\[-0.15em]
  &\hspace{0.48\linewidth}{+}\\[-0.15em]
  &\boxed{\text{postselect uniform anchors and PO-complete using Target C}}.
\end{align*}
The remaining common dependency is a bounded, row-conservative demand closure.

\section{Audit of the supplied probes}

The fixed-seed outputs were reproduced with Python 3. The empirical Q2 total in
\texttt{wp131} is
\[
  1562/1702=91.77\%,
\]
leaving 140 failing groups. The negative group phenomenon is robust, but several reported
diagnostics or verdicts are incorrect.

\subsection{The Q3 upper diagnostic is tautological}

Q2 asks whether some source-model point $y$ is above the gate and every blocked member and
also satisfies $D$. Q3's ``has upper'' predicate repeats the same condition after Q2 has
failed. Its reported $0/140$ is therefore forced by program structure. If the $D(y)$ test is
removed, 44 of the 140 failures have a common order-upper and 96 do not. The README's
unqualified statement that none has ``any common upper at all'' is false.

Only 12 of the 140 failures have a nonempty set of positive $\forall\PP$ bodies, whereas
102 have a modal filler $D$. The sample primarily stresses completion of the new top's own
demands, not aggregation of immediate $\PP$-universal bodies.

\subsection{The witness-selection cycle counts are unsound}

Parts C and F of \texttt{wp131} reselect a target separately for each blocked borrower
after gate expansion. Some selected targets are not members of \texttt{nodes};
\texttt{declared\_cycle} silently ignores those edges. Some $(a,D)$ groups also receive
multiple targets even though the construction has one actual gate child. Redirecting all
borrowers to that actual child yields eight cyclic instances under both repeat-free and
``agree'' selection, rather than five and zero. The five originally reported repeat-free
survivors themselves are genuine; the claim that the agree rule removes all cycles is not.

\subsection{The \texttt{wp94} acceptance verdict is invalid}

The checker suppresses \texttt{ee\_all} failures when a target filler is omitted by its
budget truncation. Removing this exemption produces 33 violations in 29 of 3833 accepted
instances. Moreover, nodes are pairs \texttt{(region,budget)} while the relation function
ignores the budget. Distinct occurrences of the same region therefore receive off-diagonal
$\EQ$ in 493 of 3833 instances, including occurrences with different labels. This violates
strong equality. The result is consistent with the packet's later acknowledgement that the
budget-truncated route was refuted, but \texttt{run\_probes.sh} still prints a misleading
``ALL PASS'' verdict.

\subsection{The \texttt{wp93} proof-plan check is too weak}

Part C returns success merely when its set of used cells is nonempty. Nine non-singleton
composition cells are restricted rather than universal and still require arguments. Part B
exhausts ordered-disjoint structures only through four points; it is useful finite evidence,
not by itself a universal proof. The general result should rest on the existing formal
ordered-disjoint normal-form proof, not on this finite enumeration.

\section{Revised work program}

\section{Conclusion}

The packet's recommended Target A lead is not the solution: its local satisfiability claim
is immediate, and the inference to a shareable class-top is false. The counterexample shows
exactly what a type-only key forgets: modal demands of the new top can inspect the union of
the lower contexts through forced $\DR$ rectangles.

Target B, by contrast, has a short structural paper proof once the requested property is
matched to the finite certificate. RCC5's finite transition ranks support a second
stabilization over row limits, and a triangular finite fusion selects constant phase
rectangles for all kernels simultaneously. Subject to the stated segment assumptions and
the diagonal guard check, this removes the mathematical obstruction behind
\texttt{kq\_all}.

Target C is not another instance of B. Eventual stabilization against newly generated
witnesses can diverge, but the certificate does not require it. Postselection of already
uniform finite anchors, followed by ordered-disjoint PO-completion, isolates the remaining
problem as row-conservative finite demand closure. That residue aligns naturally with the
down-spectrum refinement suggested by Target A.

Accordingly, the overall mixed-quadrant extraction and the associated fragment
decidability result remain open. The present report closes one finite combinatorial
subproblem, refutes two proposed extraction disciplines, and narrows the remaining work.
