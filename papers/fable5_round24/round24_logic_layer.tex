\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Rounds 19--23 build a composition-closed, converse-coherent, EQ-free
atomic RCC5 frame from a catalogue, entirely in the kernel.  Round-24
puts the \emph{description logic} on top.
\code{formal/Round19Transport.lean} (4{,}295 lines, still
\textbf{zero} \code{sorry}) now defines $\ALCIRCC$ concepts in negation
normal form (\code{Concept}), their semantics over a frame
(\code{sat}), Hintikka systems (\code{Hintikka} --- locally coherent
type labellings with one-step $\exists$-fulfilment), and proves the
\textbf{truth lemma}: every concept in an occurrence's type is
satisfied there, under the canonical interpretation.  Two results tie
this to the pipeline: \code{certInterp\_rcc5} proves the frame built by
rounds 19--23 is a legitimate RCC5 interpretation frame (reflexive EQ,
strong-EQ = identity, R2, R3 --- read directly off
\code{pairVal\_conv}/\code{pairVal\_closed}/\code{pairVal\_proper}); and
the capstone \code{sat\_from\_hintikka} turns any wellformed,
S-conditioned certificate carrying a root-anchored Hintikka labelling
into an RCC5 \emph{model} of the target concept.  A concrete witness
(\code{sample\_satisfiable}) exhibits a satisfiable concept with its
model.  What round-25 must add is the other direction: that the
catalogue generator \emph{produces} such a labelling for a satisfiable
input.  Status: \emph{strongly supported, not certified}.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was proved}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \code{Concept}: $\top$, $\bot$, atoms and negated atoms (NNF
literals), $\sqcap$, $\sqcup$, and $\exists r.C$ / $\forall r.C$ with
$r$ an RCC5 atom (inverse roles absorbed per the project convention).
\item \code{sat}: satisfaction over an interpretation (domain
predicate, the total single-valued relation \code{rho}, atomic
extension), structural on the concept.  Roles read the relation
directly ($\code{rho}\,x\,y = r$), which is why a proper atomic RCC5
network --- exactly what the pipeline delivers --- is the right frame.
\item \code{Hintikka}: clash-freeness and $\bot$-freeness of literals,
$\sqcap$/$\sqcup$ decomposition, $\forall$-propagation to
$r$-neighbours, and $\exists$-fulfilment by an actual $r$-neighbour.
One-step fulfilment means no promissory witnesses, hence no
eventuality/parity condition --- the structural payoff of the
split-forest normal form.
\item \code{truth\_lemma}: by structural induction on the concept,
$C \in \tau(x) \Rightarrow x \models C$.  Each case is one Hintikka
clause plus the induction hypotheses; the literal cases use
clash-freeness.
\item \code{RCC5Interp} and \code{certInterp\_rcc5}: the frame
conditions R1 (reflexive EQ, strong-EQ = identity), R2 (converse), R3
(composition), and the theorem that the pipeline's induced
interpretation satisfies them --- a direct reading of the round-21/22
closure theorems, with the diagonal handled by the EQ convention.
\item \code{sat\_from\_hintikka} (ROUND-24 MAIN THEOREM): wellformed +
S-conditioned + a root-anchored Hintikka labelling $\Rightarrow$ the
target concept is RCC5-satisfiable.  With \code{catalogue\_soundness},
\code{SCat} + \code{Faithful} supply the S-condition.
\item \code{sample\_satisfiable}: $A_0 \sqcap \forall\mathrm{DR}.\bot$
is satisfiable in a one-point model --- non-vacuity of every
definition above.
\end{itemize}

\section{What remains}

The soundness pipeline is now complete from catalogue syntax to a
\emph{model of the logic}: a certificate that is wellformed,
faithful, S-conditioned, and Hintikka-labelled is an RCC5 model of its
root concept, every step kernel-checked.  The one hypothesis not yet
produced by construction is the Hintikka labelling itself --- this is
the round-25 CONSTRUCTION: the catalogue generator, driven by the
input concept's $\exists$-requirements, must emit a plan whose built
certificate carries a fulfilling Hintikka labelling (with the
$K(C_0)$ width accounting and the limit passage absorbing the RCC5
patchwork property).  That is the completeness/construction direction,
where the standing open items F6 (width budgets) and W2$'$
(uniformization) live.  Ledger: thirteen reviews --- twelve defects,
one no-counterexample; rounds 19--24 unreviewed.  The decidability
theorem for $\ALCIRCC$ remains \textbf{strongly supported, not
certified}.
