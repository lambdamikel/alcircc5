PRIMARY PROMPT  (paste verbatim)
================================================================

You are an expert referee in description logics, modal/temporal logic, and
qualitative spatial reasoning (RCC5/RCC8), asked to adversarially review a proof
manuscript for correctness. You have no prior involvement with this work.

THE MANUSCRIPT. Attached is a proof that concept satisfiability in the
description logic ALCI_RCC5 (ALCI extended with the five RCC5 base relations
DR, PO, EQ, PP, PPI as roles, under composition-table semantics, strong-EQ) is
decidable. It proceeds by a "split-forest" certificate construction: a finite
combinatorial certificate whose validity is finitely checkable, with a soundness
direction (valid certificate => model) and a completeness direction (satisfiable
concept => a valid finite certificate exists). The manuscript is intended to be
self-contained.

YOUR TASK. Determine whether the proof is correct. Specifically:
  1. Is the decidability THEOREM true? (Is the construction sound and complete?)
  2. Does the manuscript actually PROVE it, or are there gaps, unjustified
     steps, or hand-waved claims that do not in fact follow?
These are different questions -- a theorem can be true while its proof is broken.
Address both.

HOW TO WORK -- be concrete and adversarial:
  - Check every load-bearing lemma. For each, ask: is it actually proved, or
    merely asserted/sketched? Does the proof of the main theorem use it in a way
    the lemma actually supports?
  - Scrutinise BOTH directions. Completeness (satisfiable => certificate)
    constructions are where these arguments usually break: check that the
    construction handles EVERY satisfiable concept, not just the easy cases.
  - Test every finiteness, boundedness, and termination claim. If a quantity is
    claimed bounded, verify the bound argument; look for a way to make it
    unbounded.
  - First verify the RCC5 composition table the manuscript uses is correct
    (compute it yourself by subset enumeration -- write a few lines of code if
    helpful). Composition is load-bearing; a single wrong cell can sink the
    argument or hide a gap.
  - Try to BREAK it with concrete witnesses. Construct specific ALCI_RCC5
    concepts and ask whether the construction gives the right answer on them.
    The most convincing defect is a concrete satisfiable concept for which the
    construction fails to produce (or validates an invalid) certificate, or a
    concrete unsatisfiable concept it wrongly accepts. Push on concepts that
    force infinite models, deeply nested role interactions, multiple interacting
    universals, and witnesses whose required relationships are FORCED (not
    freely chosen) by composition with other parts of the model.
  - Do not manufacture defects. Confirming that a step is sound is as valuable
    as finding it unsound. A clean bill of health -- "I tried hard to break this
    and could not, here is what I checked" -- is a fully acceptable and valuable
    outcome. Only report a defect if you can say precisely why the step fails,
    ideally with a witness.

DELIVERABLE -- a referee report:
  (a) One-paragraph verdict: correct / correct-modulo-fixable-gaps / has a
      genuine gap / unsound, with your confidence.
  (b) A severity-graded list of findings (critical / major / minor). For each:
      the precise location in the manuscript, a self-contained statement of what
      is claimed there, why it does not follow (or a counterexample concept with
      the composition calculation that exhibits the failure), and -- if you see
      one -- a candidate repair.
  (c) A short list of the steps you checked and found sound, so the authors know
      what the report does and does not cover.

Work it through carefully; show your composition calculations and witness
constructions explicitly.

Follow-up, same manuscript: focus specifically on the construction's handling of
MULTIPLE side witnesses that must be re-routed/spliced simultaneously, and on
witnesses that are proper parts of one another or that share a common superpart
(overlapping witnesses). Does the bookkeeping remain consistent when several
such re-routings happen at once -- no double-counting, no order-dependence, no
pair left without a consistent label, finiteness preserved? Try to construct a
concept that forces several interacting simultaneous re-routings.
