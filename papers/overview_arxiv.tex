\begin{abstract}
Whether concept satisfiability in $\ALCIRCC{5}$---the description logic
$\mathcal{ALCI}$ with the RCC5 base relations as roles under
\emph{abstract composition-table} semantics---is decidable has been open
since Wessel's 2002/2003 work. This paper reports a
sustained, adversarially-reviewed attack on the problem, conducted by AI
assistants under human direction, and machine-checked in part: the soundness
pipeline is kernel-checked in Lean~4, the keystone is not. We do not settle
the question. What we establish is: (i) a \emph{conditional
decidability theorem}---if the ``live width'' of models is bounded by a
computable function of the concept (property~F6), then satisfiability is
decidable---whose soundness direction is \emph{machine-checked in Lean~4};
(ii) that the remaining completeness problem of \emph{this}
finite-certificate architecture compresses into that single
property~F6---the \emph{same horizontal-coordination obstruction} that
appears in the known grid-style attempts to prove the problem
undecidable; (iii) an RCC5 normal form (now machine-certified in
\emph{both} directions) and an unconditionally decidable fragment (the
$\forall\PO$-free concepts), whose decision procedure is itself
\emph{machine-checked} --- on raw input and under concrete set semantics,
with fragment membership its only hypothesis --- and has survived three
independent cold reviews; it is a decidability theorem, not a practical
procedure, the search space being doubly exponential; (iv) a \emph{working, empirically-validated
prototype reasoner}---a cover-tree tableau that reproduces, $23$
years on, the GIS concept taxonomy of the original report, cross-validated
on $911$ concepts with zero mismatches and never once contradicted by an
independent oracle or a hand analysis; and (v) an honest map of the routes
tried, the dead ends, and the missing hard pieces. We also report,
candidly, on
the methodology---adversarial ``cold review,'' machine verification, and
a human-in-the-loop workflow---and on what frontier AI could and could
not do.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{A motivating example: what the logic does}
\label{ssec:example}

Before the difficulties, a concrete picture of the object. In
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ the five RCC5 relations serve as \emph{roles}: a concept may
quantify over the regions a given region is part of, overlaps, or is
disjoint from. A small GIS vocabulary (from Wessel's original
report~\cite{Wessel2003}) shows what this buys. Define
\[
  \textsf{CityAtRiver} \equiv \textsf{City}\sqcap\exists\PO.\textsf{River},
  \qquad
  \textsf{GermanCity} \equiv
     \textsf{City}\sqcap\forall\PP.(\neg\textsf{Country}\sqcup\textsf{Germany})
\]
---``a city overlapping a river'' and ``a city lying in no country but
Germany''---and \emph{assert} only a few primitive facts:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \textsf{Hamburg} &\sqsubseteq \textsf{City}\sqcap\exists\PO.\textsf{Alster},\\
  \textsf{Alster} &\sqsubseteq \textsf{River}\sqcap\exists\PP.\textsf{Germany}
    \sqcap\forall\PO.\neg\textsf{Country}
    \sqcap\forall\PP.(\neg\textsf{Country}\sqcup\textsf{Germany}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Nothing here \emph{states} that Hamburg is a German city or a city at a
river; a reasoner \emph{infers} both. \textsf{CityAtRiver} is
immediate---Hamburg overlaps the Alster and the Alster is a river, so
Hamburg overlaps a river. \textsf{GermanCity} is subtler and, tellingly for
what follows, runs through the \emph{composition table}: for any country
$c$ with $\textsf{Hamburg}\,\PP\,c$, the path
$\textsf{Alster}\,\PO\,\textsf{Hamburg}\,\PP\,c$ forces
$\textsf{Alster}\,R\,c$ with $R\in\comp(\PO,\PP)=\{\PO,\PP\}$
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}); the Alster overlaps no country, which excludes
$\PO$, so $\textsf{Alster}\,\PP\,c$; and the Alster's only
country-superpart is Germany, so $c=\textsf{Germany}$. Hence Hamburg is a
German city.

Two things survive into the rest of the paper. First, this is the
\emph{reasoning service}---classification---and it reduces, as all such
services do, to one decision problem: \emph{concept satisfiability}
($C\sqsubseteq D$ iff $C\sqcap\neg D$ is unsatisfiable), whose decidability
is exactly what is open. Second, even this benign inference already leans
on the RCC5 composition table; the whole difficulty is that the \emph{same}
table is, on other inputs, too loose to force what one would need
(\S\ref{ssec:whyhard}). The full taxonomy this example lives in is the one
our reasoner recomputes $23$ years on (\S\ref{sec:reasoners},
Figures~\ref{fig:gis}--\ref{fig:gis-reasoner}); how the tableau actually
derives the \textsf{GermanCity} inference is spelled out there.

\subsection{Why the problem is hard, in one page}
\label{ssec:whyhard}

RCC5 relates two regions by exactly one of five relations: equal ($\EQ$),
proper part ($\PP$), proper superpart ($\PPI$), partial overlap ($\PO$),
or discreteness/disjointness ($\DR$). Under \emph{abstract
composition-table} semantics a model is not a set of actual regions but a
set with a total labelling of ordered pairs by these relations, required
only to be converse-closed and \emph{composition-closed}: if $x\,R\,y$
and $y\,S\,z$ then $x\,T\,z$ for some $T$ the composition table allows.

The five relations split into two axes that behave completely
differently.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The vertical axis} ($\PP,\PPI$: nesting) is \emph{tame}.
    Composition can \emph{force} vertical structure---the only four
    ``determining'' compositions (those returning a single relation) all
    run through $\PP$ or $\PPI$---but an unbounded vertical chain is captured
    by a finite \emph{generator}: a self-loop that \emph{unfolds} into
    infinitely many distinct points (never an actual $\PP$-cycle, which
    strong $\EQ$ forbids), just as regular structures capture infinite paths
    in modal logic. Infinite depth is not the problem.
  \item \textbf{The horizontal axis} ($\DR,\PO$: side-by-side) is
    \emph{wild}. No \emph{single} composition step pins a horizontal
    relation to one value ($\PO$ is never a singleton table entry)---though
    as constraints accumulate, their \emph{intersection} sometimes can. What
    composition still cannot do is force two occurrences \emph{together}; it
    can only force them \emph{apart} (a shared horizontal anchor excludes
    $\EQ$). So horizontal ``crowds'' of pairwise-distinct occurrences are
    the danger.
\end{itemize}

A finite decision procedure must record, at each interface of a model,
the genuine branching it cannot reconstruct---the \emph{live width}. Many
forced edges are \emph{shadows} (implied by composition from edges
already present) and cost nothing; live width counts only the rest. The
whole decidability question reduces to one property:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{(F6)} Is the live width of models bounded by a computable
function of the input concept?
\end{quote}

If yes, the certificate space is finite and satisfiability is decidable.
And here is the knife's edge: the looseness F6 must tame is the
\emph{same horizontal-coordination obstruction} that has blocked every
known attempt to prove the problem \emph{undecidable}. To force
undecidability one would encode a rigid coordinate grid, which needs
\emph{clean, reusable coincidence}---a horizontal addressing scheme
reused across the grid. Individual horizontal edges \emph{can} be pinned
to a single value by the joint closure of several constraints
(Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint}), but no concept is known to make such
coincidence \emph{reusable} rather than one-off (the ``coincidence
obstruction'' Wessel identified in 2002/2003). Turn that looseness into a
computable bound and one gets decidability by \emph{this} route;
circumvent it to reuse coincidence and force a grid and one gets
undecidability. The two open directions face the same obstruction from
two sides---a \emph{shared} wall, not a theorem that F6's failure would
by itself \emph{entail} undecidability (\S\ref{sec:conclusion})---which
is why neither has been settled in over twenty years.

\subsection{Scope, contributions, and structure}
\label{ssec:scope}

This is both a technical report and a discussion piece. It is,
specifically, an \emph{overview and status map}: it states the results
and their verification level but leaves the full proofs to the cited work
packages and formal artifacts. Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \emph{conditional decidability theorem} (F6 $\Rightarrow$
    decidable) whose soundness half is machine-checked in Lean~4
    (\S\ref{ssec:approach-lean}), and the reduction of the whole problem
    to F6.
  \item The identification of F6 as the shared \emph{obstruction} facing
    both the present certificate route and the known grid-style
    undecidability attempts (\S\ref{ssec:whyhard},
    \S\ref{sec:conclusion}).
  \item An RCC5 \emph{normal form} (both directions machine-certified) and
    an unconditionally decidable \emph{$\forall\PO$-free} fragment
    (\S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}, \S\ref{ssec:approach-regcov}).
  \item An honest, technique-organized account of every route tried, the
    counterexamples that killed the wrong ideas, and the lessons
    (\S\ref{sec:approaches}), so that others need not repeat them.
  \item A candid methodology report on adversarial AI-assisted
    mathematics (\S\ref{sec:agentic}).
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows.
\S\ref{sec:status} states, in largely non-technical terms, where the
problem stands after twenty-five years.
\S\ref{sec:history} gives the history ($\mathcal{ALCI}_{\mathrm{RCC}}$
from Cohn~1993 through Wessel~2002/2003 and Lutz--Wolter~2006) and
carefully distinguishes our object from the neighbouring logics that
\emph{are} known undecidable.
\S\ref{sec:fofragments} views the problem as first-order satisfiability
and asks whether it falls inside a decidable fragment of first-order
logic---it does not, and the deepest reason (totality) previews the
central obstruction.
\S\ref{sec:undec} explains why the standard undecidability reductions
fail.
\S\ref{sec:approaches}---the core of the paper---surveys the approaches
\emph{by technique}: naive tableaux and blocking, finite-model and
reduction attempts, the split-forest idea, the automata route, the Lean
line culminating in F6, and the regular-cover pivot.
\S\ref{sec:reasoners} describes the working reasoners and a GIS example.
\S\ref{sec:agentic} reports on the AI methodology.
\S\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with what was learned, what would move
the needle, an honest estimate of the chances of closing F6, and the
project's actual wins.

\section{Current status: where we stand after twenty-five years}
\label{sec:status}

\emph{Decidability of $\ALCIRCC{5}$ concept satisfiability remains open}
(the closely related $\ALCIRCC{8}$ likewise, but it is not our focus
here). A current-literature check (July~2026)
found no published resolution; the closest neighbours are decided in the
other direction and are discussed in \S\ref{sec:history}. What the
present work adds is not an answer but a sharp reduction and a
machine-checked surround.

\paragraph{The positive result---conditional decidability.}
\emph{If} the live width of models is bounded by a computable function of
the concept (property~F6), \emph{then} concept satisfiability is
decidable; and the \textbf{soundness half of that reduction is
machine-checked in Lean~4} (a valid finite certificate provably unfolds
to a genuine RCC5 model of the concept, and the finite type abstraction
is faithful). What is open is the antecedent F6 itself---equivalently the
completeness direction, that every satisfiable concept has a
\emph{bounded} finite certificate.

\paragraph{What is certified} (machine-checked, Lean~4, zero
\code{sorry}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{soundness} pipeline: a valid finite certificate unfolds
    to a genuine RCC5 model of the concept;
  \item the \emph{faithfulness} of the finite-\emph{type} (Hintikka,
    possibly infinitary) abstraction: satisfiability and
    abstraction-realizability coincide;
  \item the \emph{RCC5 normal form}, \emph{both directions}: every
    strong-$\EQ$ RCC5 network is an ordered-disjoint structure---a strict
    partial order for $\PP$ with a downward-closed disjointness for
    $\DR$, $\PO$ residual---and, conversely, every ordered-disjoint
    structure is realized by sets under set-RCC5 (GPT-5.6 Pro's canonical
    representation, Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is strongly supported but not certified:} F6, the open
keystone \emph{of the present certificate architecture}---now sharpened to
a precise combinatorial dichotomy (does some concept force unbounded
``identity-selector minors''?), with the auxiliary uniformization property
W2$'$ shown to \emph{fold into} it. (F6 controls \emph{this} route; its
failure would not by itself close off decidability by some other
representation---\S\ref{sec:conclusion}.)

\paragraph{Unconditional by-products (independent of F6):}
\begin{itemize}
  \item the $\forall\PO$-free fragment is \emph{decidable}---concepts
    with no $\forall\PO$ subformula, still retaining $\exists\PO$,
    $\forall\DR$, $\exists\DR$, and all vertical modalities, so a
    genuinely expressive spatial fragment (its decision procedure now
    machine-checked in Lean for the three pure quadrants and the mixed case
    on a witness; Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree});
  \item the RCC5 normal form above (both directions certified);
  \item a working, empirically validated \emph{prototype reasoner} (the
    cover-tree tableau; no completeness theorem is claimed): cross-validated
    on $911$ concepts against an
    independent oracle with zero mismatches, reproducing $23$ years later
    the full GIS subsumption taxonomy of Wessel's original report, and
    never once contradicted---no counterexample to its verdicts was found
    anywhere in the campaign;
  \item the standard undecidability routes shown blocked, with the
    coincidence obstruction identified as the shared fact.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The honest bottom line.} After roughly thirty repair rounds
and seventeen adversarial reviews the project has reached a genuine
\emph{local optimum}: the local algebra is exhausted (and now
certified), the soundness side is machine-checked, and the whole
remaining difficulty \emph{of the present certificate architecture} is one
well-posed lemma. We offer the work not as a
resolution but as a two-decade open problem \emph{reduced, honestly and
checkably, to its irreducible core.}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We fix the object logic precisely; readers familiar with $\ALCIRCC{}$ may
skim to Definition~\ref{def:f6}.

\paragraph{Syntax.} $\ALCIRCC{5}$ concepts are built from atomic concepts
$A$ and the five RCC5 relations, used as roles, by
\[
 C ::= A \mid \neg A \mid \top \mid \bot \mid C\sqcap C \mid C\sqcup C
 \mid \exists R.C \mid \forall R.C,
 \qquad R\in\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO,\DR\}.
\]
We work in \emph{negation normal form} (NNF: negation only on atoms) and
\emph{absorb inverse roles} through the RCC5 converse---$\exists\PP^{-}.C
=\exists\PPI.C$, $\exists\PO^{-}.C=\exists\PO.C$, and so on---so no
separate inverse constructor is needed. Let $\cl(C_0)$ be the
Fischer--Ladner closure of the input concept and $m=|\cl(C_0)|$.

\paragraph{Semantics: abstract composition tables, strong $\EQ$.} An
interpretation is a pair $\mathcal I=(\Delta,\rho)$ with
$\Delta\neq\emptyset$ and a \emph{total} labelling
$\rho:\Delta\times\Delta\to\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,\PO,\DR\}$ satisfying:
(i) \emph{strong $\EQ$}: $\rho(x,y)=\EQ$ iff $x=y$ (so $\EQ$ is identity,
and between distinct elements only $\{\PP,\PPI,\PO,\DR\}$ occur);
(ii) \emph{converse-closed}: $\rho(y,x)=\mathrm{conv}(\rho(x,y))$, where
$\PP^{-1}=\PPI$, $\PO^{-1}=\PO$, $\DR^{-1}=\DR$; and
(iii) \emph{composition-closed}: $\rho(x,z)\in\comp(\rho(x,y),\rho(y,z))$
for all $x,y,z$, over the RCC5 composition table (Table~\ref{tab:comp}).
Concept extensions are standard, reading a role $R$ as the pair set
$\{(x,y):\rho(x,y)=R\}$; the concept $C_0$ is \emph{satisfiable} if
$x\in C_0^{\mathcal I}$ for some $\mathcal I$ and some $x\in\Delta$. Thus a
model is a complete graph carrying a composition-closed RCC5
edge-labelling; \emph{models may be infinite}. This is precisely the
\emph{abstract} (composition-table) semantics---not a semantics of actual
regions in a topological space. We fix the \emph{strong} $\EQ$ semantics
throughout (the standard convention for this problem; strong versus weak
$\EQ$ is discussed below). The decision problem is: given $C_0$, is it
satisfiable?

\paragraph{The sister logic $\ALCIRCC{8}$.} $\ALCIRCC{8}$ is defined
identically, over the eight RCC8 relations
$\{\DC,\EC,\PO,\EQ,\TPP,\NTPP,\TPPI,\NTPPI\}$, which \emph{refine} RCC5 by
splitting $\DR$ into $\DC$ (disconnected) and $\EC$ (externally connected,
i.e.\ touching at the boundary), and $\PP,\PPI$ into tangential and
non-tangential parts ($\TPP,\NTPP$ and $\TPPI,\NTPPI$); $\PO$ and $\EQ$
are unchanged. Its abstract composition-table semantics is the exact
analogue over the RCC8 composition table, and RCC8 likewise has the
patchwork property. $\ALCIRCC{5}$ is our focus; we invoke $\ALCIRCC{8}$
only where it matters---its $\EXPTIME$-hardness (\S\ref{sec:history}) and
the domino attempts (\S\ref{sec:undec}), which turn on the extra
$\TPP/\NTPP$ distinction.

\paragraph{The RCC granularity hierarchy.} $\ALCIRCC5$ and $\ALCIRCC8$ sit
atop a family of five logics $\ALCIRCC{k}$, $k\in\{1,2,3,5,8\}$, whose
relation sets \emph{coarsen} as $k$ decreases~\cite{Wessel2003}:
$\ALCIRCC1$ has the single relation $\mathsf{SR}$ (``spatially related'');
$\ALCIRCC2$ has $\{\DR,\mathsf{O}\}$ (with $\mathsf{O}$ = ``overlapping'');
and $\ALCIRCC3$ has $\{\DR,\mathsf{ONE},\EQ\}$ (with $\mathsf{ONE}$ =
``overlapping but not equal''). Each coarser set is definable in the finer
one---$\mathsf{ONE}=\{\PP,\PPI,\PO\}$, $\mathsf{O}=\{\mathsf{ONE},\EQ\}$,
$\mathsf{SR}=\{\DR,\mathsf{O}\}$---so the family forms a granularity
lattice. The three coarsest logics are \emph{decidable}
(Wessel~\cite{Wessel2003}); $\ALCIRCC5$ and $\ALCIRCC8$ are the open cases
this paper concerns. The full landscape is Table~\ref{tab:complexity}
(\S\ref{sec:history}).

\paragraph{Strong versus weak $\EQ$.} Two semantics for $\EQ$ appear in
the literature~\cite{Wessel2003}: the \emph{strong} one, $\EQ^{\mathcal
I}=\mathrm{Id}(\Delta)$ ($\EQ$ is exactly identity), and the \emph{weak}
one, $\mathrm{Id}(\Delta)\subseteq\EQ^{\mathcal I}$ ($\EQ$ contains
identity but may relate distinct, mutually indistinguishable elements). We
fix the \emph{strong} semantics throughout---the standard choice for this
problem, and the more expressive one: Wessel observed that under strong
$\EQ$, from $\ALCIRCC3$ upward the \emph{difference role}
$R_D =_{\mathrm{def}} N_R\setminus\{\EQ\}$ becomes definable, so
\emph{nominals} are encodable ($i\sqcap\forall R_D.\neg i$ forces $i$ to
name a single element)---a reminder of how narrow the margin to
undecidability is (adding a $\downarrow$-binder would cross it, cf.\
\S\ref{sec:wessel-grid}). The choice costs no generality for decidability:
Wessel showed decidability under the weak semantics would carry over to the
strong one, and conversely a weak-$\EQ$ model collapses to a strong one by
internalizing $\EQ$ as identity in the TBox, so the two satisfiability
problems are inter-reducible.

\paragraph{The two axes, made precise.} Call $\PP,\PPI$ \emph{vertical}
(nesting) and $\DR,\PO$ \emph{horizontal} (side-by-side). The composition
table makes the axes behave oppositely, and this asymmetry is the root of
the whole difficulty.

\begin{proposition}[Determination is vertical]\label{prop:vertical}
Of the sixteen compositions of proper (non-$\EQ$) relations, exactly four
are \emph{determining} (return a singleton):
\[
\comp(\DR,\PPI)=\{\DR\},\; \comp(\PP,\DR)=\{\DR\},\;
\comp(\PP,\PP)=\{\PP\},\; \comp(\PPI,\PPI)=\{\PPI\}.
\]
Every one has a vertical ($\PP$ or $\PPI$) argument, and $\PO$ is never
the value of any determining composition. (Exhaustive over
Table~\ref{tab:comp}; machine-verified.)
\end{proposition}

So no \emph{single} composition step forces a horizontal value---the four
determining steps are all vertical. This must not be overread:

\begin{remark}[Single-step versus joint forcing]\label{rem:pojoint}
Proposition~\ref{prop:vertical} concerns \emph{one} length-two path; it does
\emph{not} say horizontal edges are always free. The \emph{intersection} of
several composition constraints can pin a horizontal edge to a single value:
$\comp(\DR,\PO)=\{\DR,\PO,\PP\}$ and $\comp(\PPI,\PO)=\{\PPI,\PO\}$ intersect
in $\{\PO\}$, so an edge forced through two different intermediates can be
\emph{compelled} to be $\PO$ (machine-checked, \texttt{wp85}); indeed every
proper relation---$\DR,\PO,\PP,\PPI$---is reachable as such a joint singleton,
so $\PO$ is not special among them. Consequently ``shadow'' and ``live''
(Definition~\ref{def:f6}) must be read against the \emph{joint} closure of all
constraints, never single-path forcing. What \emph{composition} genuinely
never forces is a merge: no intersection of composition cells whose two
premise labels are \emph{proper} (non-$\EQ$) relations ever narrows to
$\{\EQ\}$ (\texttt{wp85}), so composition can only force occurrences
\emph{apart} (a shared horizontal anchor excludes $\EQ$), never
\emph{together}. Concept \emph{labels}, by contrast, \emph{can} force a
\emph{local} merge: the satisfiable one-level gadget of
\S\ref{sec:domino-attempt} forces its two coloured witnesses to coincide
and remains satisfiable. What fails is \emph{propagating} that isolation
recursively---the discipline that would tile a grid collapses the
construction to unsatisfiability. So the coincidence obstruction is really
a \emph{dichotomy} at the level of the would-be grid: a grid merge is
either \emph{unforced} (the branches never join) or \emph{fatal}
(recursion collapses it to \textsc{unsat}), never a clean forced
grid---not a blanket ``EQ cannot be forced,'' and not a claim that no
merge is ever satisfiable. That one-sidedness---composition forces apart
but not (usefully) together---is the real horizontal asymmetry.
\end{remark}

There is one further asymmetry, and it is the one the split-forest
presentation (\S\ref{ssec:approach-splitforest}) turns to its advantage:
disjointness is inherited \emph{downward} but not upward.

\begin{proposition}[Half-determinism of disjointness]\label{prop:drdown}
If $x\mathbin{\DR}y$ then every part of $x$ is disjoint from every part of
$y$: $\comp(\PP,\DR)=\{\DR\}$ forces $x'\mathbin{\DR}y$ for every
$x'\mathbin{\PP}x$, and $\comp(\DR,\PPI)=\{\DR\}$ pushes this down the
$y$-side as well. \emph{Upward} the inference fails: a \emph{superpart} of
a disjoint region may be disjoint from, overlap, or contain the other
region---$\comp(\PPI,\DR)=\{\PPI,\PO,\DR\}$ is not a singleton, so nothing
is forced. Disjointness thus propagates deterministically to descendants
in the $\PP$-order, and to nothing else---the \emph{downward-closed
disjointness} that the RCC5 normal form (Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform},
certified) makes exact.
\end{proposition}

This ``half-determinism'' (deterministic downward, free upward) is what
lets a finite presentation dispatch arbitrarily wide $\DR$ crowds for free
(\S\ref{ssec:approach-splitforest}): a single high $\DR$ edge shadows every
disjointness beneath it, leaving only $\PO$ and the \emph{upward} direction
as genuine branching. Vertical recurrence, moreover, always folds:

\begin{lemma}[No horizontal recurrence]\label{lem:pathauto}
Form the composition-path subset automaton whose states are the reachable
relation sets, with $\mathrm{Rel}(\varepsilon)=\{\EQ\}$ and
$\mathrm{Rel}(w\,a)=\bigcup_{r\in\mathrm{Rel}(w)}\comp(r,a)$. Restrict to
states not containing $\EQ$, and to non-$\EQ$ labels (an $\EQ$ label is a
self-loop on every state, since $\EQ$ is the composition identity). Then no
cycle uses a \emph{horizontal} ($\DR$/$\PO$) label: every cycle is built
from vertical ($\PP$/$\PPI$) steps. Hence any support recurrence that never
folds by an equality is \emph{eventually vertical}, and is absorbed by
vertical looping rather than by a horizontal crowd. (Exhaustive finite
computation; machine-verified, \texttt{wp38}.)
\end{lemma}

Proposition~\ref{prop:vertical} and Lemma~\ref{lem:pathauto} explain why
infinite \emph{depth} is never the obstacle: forced structure is
vertical, and vertical chains loop. Everything hard is horizontal---and,
crucially, while \emph{individual} horizontal edges can be pinned (by the
joint closure of several constraints, Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint}), no concept
is known to \emph{force an unboundedly wide rigid} horizontal pattern.
Whether one can is exactly F6. A decision procedure must record only the
horizontal branching it cannot reconstruct:

\begin{definition}[Shadows, live width, and F6]\label{def:f6}
In a model, or in a finite presentation of one, an edge is a
\emph{shadow} if its RCC5 value is forced by the \emph{joint}
composition-closure of the edges already present---which may be a single
triangle ($x\mathbin{\PP}y,\,y\mathbin{\DR}z$ force $x\mathbin{\DR}z$ by
$\comp(\PP,\DR)=\{\DR\}$) \emph{or} the intersection of several
(Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint}); a shadow costs a decision procedure nothing. The
\emph{live width} at an interface is the number of non-shadow edges
there---the genuine branching that must be recorded.
Property \textbf{F6} states: for every satisfiable $C_0$, the live width
of some presentation of a model of $C_0$ is bounded by a computable
function of $C_0$.
\end{definition}

\section{History of $\ALCIRCC{}$ and why the known results do not settle it}
\label{sec:history}

\subsection{Cohn's 1993 proposal}

The idea of combining description logics with qualitative spatial
reasoning via modal accessibility relations was first proposed by
Cohn~\cite{Cohn1993}, who considered treating the RCC8 relations
(or subsets thereof) as modalities in a multi-modal logic.
The framework allowed spatial constraints to be expressed as
modal formulas---e.g., $\langle \PP \rangle C$ for ``some proper
part satisfies~$C$''---but Cohn left all decidability and complexity
questions open.

\subsection{Wessel's $\ALCIRCC{}$ family (2002/2003)}

Wessel~\cite{Wessel2002,Wessel2003,Wessel2001} gave the first rigorous treatment
of the combination, introducing the \emph{$\ALCIRCC{}$ family}:
description logics $\ALCIRCC{k}$ ($k = 1,2,3,5,8$) extending $\ALCI$
with role boxes derived from the RCC composition tables.
In an $\ALCIRCC{k}$ interpretation, every pair of domain elements is
assigned exactly one RCC$k$ base relation, and the composition table
is enforced globally (\emph{strong $\EQ$ semantics}: $\EQ$ is identity).
Models are complete graphs with RCC$k$ edge-colorings.

Wessel established the decidability and complexity landscape summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:complexity}. The three coarsest logics are \emph{decidable}:
$\ALCIRCC1$ is essentially the modal logic S5 (hence \textsc{NP}-complete);
$\ALCIRCC2$ and $\ALCIRCC3$ are decidable because their composition tables
are deterministic or near-deterministic, admitting reduction to standard DL
techniques (for $\ALCIRCC3$ under strong $\EQ$, via the definable
difference role). The two finest logics---$\ALCIRCC5$ ($\PSPACE$-hard) and
$\ALCIRCC8$ ($\EXPTIME$-hard)---were left as the central open problem.

In a series of companion reports, Wessel also mapped out the
decidability landscape for description logics with general
composition-based role inclusion axioms:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}}$ (arbitrary role axioms) is
    \textbf{undecidable}, via reduction from the Post
    Correspondence Problem~\cite{Wessel2001RA}.
  \item $\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}}^\ominus$ (non-disjoint-roles
    variant) is also \textbf{undecidable}, via reduction from
    non-empty CFG intersection~\cite{Wessel2000RA}.
  \item $\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}_{SG}}$ (admissible role boxes:
    deterministic, functional, complete, and associative) is
    \textbf{decidable} (\EXPTIME-complete, via reduction to
    $\ALC$ with general TBoxes)~\cite{Wessel2000SG}; adding
    unqualified number restrictions ($\mathcal{ALCN}_{\mathcal{RA}_{SG}}$)
    makes it undecidable again.
\end{itemize}
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ sits precisely in the gap: its role box is
non-deterministic (unlike $\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}_{SG}}$) but
has the patchwork property (unlike
$\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}}/\ALC_{\mathcal{RA}}^\ominus$).

\begin{remark}[Key difficulties]
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ has none of the properties that standard DL
decidability proofs rely on:
(i)~no tree model property (models are complete graphs),
(ii)~no finite model property (some satisfiable concepts require
infinite models),
(iii)~a non-deterministic role box (multi-valued composition entries).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Wessel's grid constructions and the coincidence obstruction}
\label{sec:wessel-grid}

In his investigation of $\ALCIRCC{8}$~\cite{Wessel2003}, Wessel
made two important discoveries:

\medskip\noindent\textbf{1.\ Even-odd chains (Section~4.4.2).}
The $\TPP/\NTPP$ distinction in RCC8 allows the construction of
\emph{functional successor chains}: each node alternates between
$\mathit{even}$ and $\mathit{odd}$, and a node can distinguish its
direct $\TPPI$-successor from all indirect ones by parity.
Wessel used this to encode a binary $n$-bit counter, proving
$\ALCIRCC{8}$ is $\EXPTIME$-hard.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{2.\ The grid coincidence obstruction (Section~4.4.3,
Figure~11).}
Wessel constructed explicit RCC8 networks isomorphic to
$n \times n$ grids, using $\TPP$ for horizontal and vertical
successors.  However, he identified a fundamental obstacle to
encoding domino tilings: the \emph{coincidence condition}
$R_X \circ R_Y = R_Y \circ R_X$ (east-of-north equals
north-of-east) cannot be enforced by concept terms.
The composition table allows $\{PO, EQ, TPP, NTPP, TPPI, NTPPI\}$
at the coincidence point---too many options.  Wessel concluded:
\begin{quote}
``Even though these infinite models do exist, we have found no way
to \emph{enforce} the depicted model(s) by means of a concept term.''
\end{quote}
Wessel further observed that adding a hybrid logic binding operator
$\downarrow$ (nominals) would immediately yield undecidability,
confirming that the coincidence condition is the \emph{precise
boundary}.  His explicit grid frame is reproduced here as
Figure~\ref{fig:rcc8grid}, and the merge obstruction that blocks enforcing
it as Figure~\ref{fig:merge}.

Wessel thus independently developed, three to four years before
Lutz--Wolter, the RCC8 description-logic analogues of these
ingredients---the even-odd chain, an explicit two-dimensional
$\TPP/\NTPP$ grid, and the first identification of the coincidence
gap as the decisive obstacle.  Lutz--Wolter later obtained
undecidability by a \emph{different} route---a linearized tiling
enumeration (their~\S5, following Marx--Reynolds) that sidesteps the
two-dimensional coincidence rather than enforcing it---so this is
shared lineage and prior identification, not a dependence of their
proof on Wessel's constructions.

\subsection{Lutz \& Wolter (2006)}

Lutz and Wolter~\cite{LutzWolter2006} studied \emph{modal logics
of topological relations}, denoted $\LRCC{8}$, $\LRCC{5}$, etc.
These have the same \emph{syntax} as $\ALCIRCC{}$ but are
interpreted over \emph{topological models}: the domain elements
are regular closed subsets of a topological space (typically~$\reals^n$).
They proved:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\LRCC{8}$ is \textbf{undecidable} (their~\S5: a linearized
    $\NTPP$-chain enumeration of tile positions, following
    Marx--Reynolds, whose faithfulness relies on the geometry of
    $\reals^n$; no two-dimensional grid coincidence is enforced).
  \item $\LRCC{5}(\mathit{RS}^\exists)$ is \textbf{undecidable}
    (reduction from $S5^3$ via supremum regions).
  \item $\LRCC{5}(\mathit{RS})$ is \textbf{open}. Lutz and Wolter
    explicitly wrote (p.~31): ``Perhaps the most interesting
    candidate is $L_{RCC5}(RS)$ [\ldots] to which the reduction
    exhibited in Section~8 does not apply.''
\end{itemize}

The logic $\ALCIRCC{5}$ is exactly $\LRCC{5}(\mathit{RS})$:
arbitrary complete-graph models with composition-table semantics.
The problem has been recognized as open by leading researchers
since 2006.

\paragraph{On priority and citation.} Two honest caveats, in both
directions. First, Wessel proved \emph{neither} decidability nor
undecidability of $\ALCIRCC{5}$ or $\ALCIRCC{8}$: report7 constructs the
grid models and pins the coincidence obstruction as the precise boundary,
but stops there, explicitly leaving both problems open. The contribution
was to \emph{locate} the wall, not to breach it. Second, and less
comfortably: the Lutz--Wolter paper does not cite report7, although it was
a publicly available official technical report of the University of Hamburg
(2002/2003) that already developed the $\ALCIRCC{}$ description logics, the
even-odd counter construction, the explicit two-dimensional $\TPP/\NTPP$
grid, and the coincidence obstruction as the decisive obstacle. We record
this as a matter of \emph{priority}, not of dependence---their
undecidability proof takes a genuinely different (topological, linearized)
route and in no way relies on report7---but the overlap in ingredients was
substantial and the material was findable.

\subsection{Concrete-domain approaches, and why they do not settle it}
\label{sec:concrete-domains}

A natural objection is that decidability of description logics with RCC
constraints is surely already known. It is---but for a \emph{different},
expressively incomparable formalism: the \emph{concrete-domain} approach,
in which spatial relations constrain \emph{functional attributes} of
individuals rather than serving as roles. The distinction is decisive
(Table~\ref{tab:concrete}). A concrete domain reasons about the spatial
\emph{attributes} of elements; $\ALCIRCC{}$ instead makes the spatial
relations \emph{themselves} the roles, and so permits
\emph{quantification over} them---concepts such as $\forall\PP.C$ (``every
proper part satisfies $C$'') and $\exists\DR.D$ (``some discrete region
satisfies $D$'')---which the concrete-domain setting cannot express.

\midrule

The known decidability results all sit on the concrete-domain side. Lutz
and Mili\v{c}i\'{c}~\cite{LutzMilicic2007} showed $\ALC$ with
$\omega$-admissible concrete domains (which include RCC5 and RCC8)
decidable, without inverse roles; Baader and
Rydval~\cite{BaaderRydval2020} revisited and generalized the
$\omega$-admissibility conditions (and strengthened the matching
undecidability results); Borgwardt, De~Bortoli and
Koopmann~\cite{BorgwardtEtAl2024} pinned $\ALC(\mathcal D)$ ontology
consistency at $\EXPTIME$-complete for $\omega$-admissible $\mathcal D$
($\ALC$ only, no inverse); and Demri and Gu~\cite{DemriGu2026} recently
extended the constraint-automaton method to \emph{inverse} roles and
functional role names with $\EXPTIME$ membership---the closest published
result to $\ALCIRCC{}$, yet still within the concrete-domain formalism.
None settles $\ALCIRCC5$ or $\ALCIRCC8$: the composition-based role box
allows quantification over the spatial relations themselves, and the
complete-graph semantics is not a concrete-domain constraint. The two
lines are complementary---attributes versus relationships---which is why
twenty years of progress on the former left the latter open.

\section{Decidable fragments of first-order logic, and why none captures it}
\label{sec:fofragments}

$\ALCIRCC{5}$ satisfiability is, quite literally, a \emph{first-order}
satisfiability problem. The five relations are binary predicates; the
composition-table semantics is a finite set of first-order axioms
$T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$---exactly one atom on each ordered pair of distinct
elements, $\EQ$ as identity, converse coherence, and, for every pair of atoms
$r,s$ and every $t\notin\comp(r,s)$, the \emph{forbidden triangle}
$\forall x\,y\,z\;\neg\bigl(R_r(x,y)\wedge R_s(y,z)\wedge R_t(x,z)\bigr)$---while
the concept $C_0$ becomes a sentence $\exists x\,\mathrm{ST}(C_0,x)$ under the
standard modal translation. Thus $C_0$ is satisfiable iff
$T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}\cup\{\exists x\,\mathrm{ST}(C_0,x)\}$ has a model. If this
theory fell inside a \emph{decidable fragment} of first-order logic we would
be done---a natural first thing to try, and, at coarser granularity, exactly
how the decidable family members fell: report7 settles $\ALCIRCC3$ by
precisely this route (below). At RCC5 it does not work; the surface reasons
vary by fragment, but the deepest one---the one that defeats even the
fragments tailor-made for relational theories like this---is the same
totality that underlies F6.

Two invariants of $\ALCIRCC{5}$ models constrain which fragment could contain
it.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No finite model property.} An ascending $\PP$-tower
    ($\exists\PPI.\top\sqcap\forall\PPI.\exists\PPI.\top$, with $\PP$ a strict
    order) is satisfiable only in infinite models. Any fragment with the
    finite model property is excluded outright.
  \item \textbf{Complete Gaifman graph.} Totality forces every two distinct
    elements to be related, so the Gaifman graph\footnote{The \emph{Gaifman
    graph} of a relational structure is the undirected graph on its elements
    in which two distinct elements are joined whenever they occur together in
    some tuple of some relation---for binary relations, whenever $R(x,y)$ or
    $R(y,x)$ holds for some $R$. It is the standard way to speak of the
    ``locality'' or ``sparsity'' of a model: bounded degree, bounded
    treewidth, and similar tameness conditions are all read off it. Here,
    because every pair of elements carries an RCC5 atom, this graph is
    \emph{complete}---maximally non-local.} of every model is
    \emph{complete}: a $\PP$-chain of length $n$ already has treewidth $n-1$
    and clique number $n$ (\texttt{wp78}). Any fragment whose decidability
    rests on models of bounded treewidth---the whole guarded family---is
    excluded as well.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Against these, the standard fragments fall as follows.

\emph{Two variables ($\mathrm{FO}^2$).} This is how the coarser family
members actually fell: report7 proves $\ALCIRCC3$ (strong $\EQ$) decidable by
translating concepts, via the standard translation, into \emph{two-variable}
first-order logic with equality~\cite{Wessel2003}---possible there because
the RCC3 role theory needs no ternary composition axiom at all (report7's
role theory is exactly-one plus converse symmetry, all two-variable; the
RCC3 table constrains no triple of distinct elements). At RCC5 the route is
excluded twice over: the composition axiom is now genuinely ternary
($\comp(\PP,\DR)=\{\DR\}$ constrains distinct triples), and $\mathrm{FO}^2$
has the finite model property~\cite{GradelKolaitisVardi1997}, which
$\ALCIRCC5$ lacks. Worse, the ternary step is into
hostile territory---the three-variable fragment $\mathrm{FO}^3$ is already
\emph{undecidable} (it captures the equational theory of relation
algebras~\cite{TarskiGivant1987})---so variable-counting alone offers no
route: any decidability must come from the \emph{shape} of the axioms, not
their arity.

\emph{Prefix classes (Bernays--Sch\"onfinkel--Ramsey and relatives).} The
standard translation of nested modalities produces unbounded
$\forall\exists$ alternation, so no bounded quantifier-prefix class applies;
these classes also have the finite model property.

\emph{The guarded fragment and its relatives.} This is the interesting
near-miss, and it is worth being precise about where it breaks. The concept
part is \emph{guarded} outright---the modal translation is the paradigm
guarded formula, each quantifier relativized by its role atom
($\exists\PP.C\leadsto\exists y(R_\PP(x,y)\wedge\cdots)$)~\cite{AndrekaVanBenthemNemeti1998}.
The composition constraint, in forbidden-triangle form, is
\emph{clique-guarded}: in $R_r(x,y)\wedge R_s(y,z)\wedge R_t(x,z)$ every pair
of variables co-occurs in an atom---exactly a clique guard---so
composition-closure lives in Gr\"adel's clique-guarded fragment~\cite{Gradel1999};
converse and mutual-exclusion are guarded too. In other words \emph{almost}
the entire theory is clique-guarded. What is not---and cannot be---is
\textbf{totality}: ``every two elements are related'' is the archetypal
non-guarded assertion (it is not invariant under the guarded bisimulation
these fragments are built around). And totality is not a technicality one may
drop: it is what forces the complete Gaifman graphs above, which destroy the
generalized tree-model property on which the guarded family's decidability
rests. The guarded route thus fails for exactly the reason \texttt{wp78}
records---and note it is \emph{totality}, not the ternary composition axiom,
that does the damage; the composition axiom is the tamer half.

\begin{remark}[On the two renderings of the composition table]
\label{rem:renderings}
The \emph{natural} first-order rendering of a table cell is not the
forbidden triangle but the implication
\[
\forall x\,y\,z\;\bigl(R_r(x,y)\wedge R_s(y,z)\;\rightarrow\;
R_{t_1}(x,z)\vee\dots\vee R_{t_n}(x,z)\bigr),
\qquad \{t_1,\dots,t_n\}=\comp(r,s),
\]
and it is instructive that this form is \emph{less} guarded, not more. Its
guard $R_r(x,y)\wedge R_s(y,z)$ is a \emph{path}, not a clique: the pair
$\{x,z\}$ the consequent speaks about never co-occurs in a guard
atom---indeed for $r=s=t_1=\PP$ the sentence \emph{is} transitivity,
$\forall xyz\,(R_\PP(x,y)\wedge R_\PP(y,z)\rightarrow R_\PP(x,z))$, the
textbook non-guardable shape (and the one behind the transitivity
undecidability cited below). The contrapositive forbidden triangles are
clique-guarded precisely because the offending third edge moves \emph{into
the guard}. The two renderings are equivalent only modulo
exactly-one: the implication additionally \emph{forces} an atom on
$(x,z)$---a relativized piece of totality---and the negative rewriting is
available at all only because the relation alphabet is finite and
exhaustive (for a single transitive relation over an open vocabulary no
such rewriting exists). Nor is the rewriting a loophole: within the
clique-guarded fragment the triangle sentences are \emph{strictly weaker}
than transitivity, since a model may leave $(x,z)$ atom-free; the
equivalence broker is totality, which is exactly the axiom the fragment
cannot have. Nothing downstream is sensitive to the choice---both are
finite first-order renderings, interchangeable over $T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$,
so the $\Pi^0_1$ placement (\S\ref{ssec:approach-lean}) is
unaffected---but the diagnosis is: written with implications, composition \emph{looks}
unguarded for a repairable, surface reason; written with triangles, every
axiom of $T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$ dresses in guards \emph{except} totality,
which is the honest accounting.
\end{remark}

\emph{Other fragments.} The fluted fragment requires predicate arguments to
form a suffix of the quantifier sequence; the composition atom $R_t(x,z)$
skips $y$, so it is not fluted. The unary-negation and guarded-negation
fragments are a subtler near-miss: negation of \emph{sentences} is
unrestricted there, so the forbidden triangles themselves \emph{are}
expressible (each is the negation of an existential-positive
sentence)---but both fragments have the finite model
property~\cite{BaranyTenCateSegoufin2015}, which $\ALCIRCC5$ lacks, and
totality is again out of reach (its inner negations carry two free
variables and no atom to guard them). And the one extension that would embrace the
vertical structure directly---the guarded fragment \emph{with transitive
relations}, since $\PP,\PPI$ are transitive ($\comp(\PP,\PP)=\{\PP\}$)---is
\emph{undecidable} once transitivity is used outside guards, as it must be
here~\cite{GanzingerMeyerVeanes1999}, so even the most sympathetic extension
supplies no decision procedure.

\paragraph{The lesson.} The surface reasons vary---arity, prefix shape, atom
shape, the finite model property---but those are boundary mismatches that
fragment engineering could in principle negotiate. The failure that matters
is the guarded family's, and there the culprit is \emph{totality}, i.e.\ the
complete Gaifman graph: the one axiom of $T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$ no guard can
carry, and the one obstruction no choice of fragment removes, because it is
a property of the \emph{models}, not of the axioms' syntax. It is precisely
the obstruction F6 names from the width side and \texttt{wp78} from the
cover side; the FO-fragment view rediscovers it from classical model theory. It
also tells us which tools are the wrong ones: guardedness and locality
(Gaifman, Hanf) are exactly what totality defeats, so the productive
machinery is the totality-tolerant kind---Ehrenfeucht--Fra\"iss\'e /
back-and-forth and the composition method over bounded \emph{tuple}
profiles---which is the ``correct room'' the keystone analysis independently
points to (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\section{Why standard undecidability reductions fail}
\label{sec:undec}


Undecidability proofs for description logics use a variety of
techniques: grid encoding ($\naturals \times \naturals$ domino
tiling, requiring functional roles or number restrictions),
reduction from the Post Correspondence Problem (PCP), reduction
from context-free grammar (CFG) intersection, or simulation of
$S5^3$ via geometric constructions.
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ lacks the features that each of these techniques
exploits: it has no functional roles, no number restrictions,
no arbitrary role box, and no geometric coincidence conditions.

\subsection{A concrete domino attempt in $\ALCIRCC{5}$}
\label{sec:domino-attempt}

Can we encode a domino-like grid using $\PP/\PPI$ chains in RCC5?
Consider the concept:
\begin{align*}
  X \;=\; &\exists\PPI.\exists\PPI.C \;\sqcap\; \exists\PPI.\exists\PPI.D \\
  &\sqcap\; \forall\PPI.\forall\PPI.(\forall\PO.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D)
    \sqcap \forall\DR.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D) \\
  &\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;
    \sqcap\; \forall\PPI.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D)
    \sqcap \forall\PP.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D)) \\
  &\sqcap\; \forall\PPI.X
\end{align*}
The intent: each $X$-node has two $\PPI$-grandchildren colored $C$
and $D$ (the ``square''), and the universals isolate the colored
nodes from all non-$\EQ$ neighbors.  The recursive conjunct
$\forall\PPI.X$ forces infinite repetition.\footnote{The recursive
occurrence of $X$ abbreviates a TBox axiom $X\sqsubseteq F(X)$; no fixpoint
constructor is needed. Since the five relations partition the square of the
domain, the \emph{universal modality} $[U]D:=\forall\EQ.D\sqcap\forall\PP.D
\sqcap\forall\PPI.D\sqcap\forall\PO.D\sqcap\forall\DR.D$ is definable, and a
finite TBox internalizes: $C$ is satisfiable w.r.t.\ $\mathcal T$ iff
$C\sqcap[U]G_{\mathcal T}$ is, where $G_{\mathcal T}$ conjoins
$\mathrm{nnf}(\neg C_i)\sqcup D_i$ over the axioms $C_i\sqsubseteq D_i$ of
$\mathcal T$. The internalization uses $\forall\PO$, so it does \emph{not}
preserve the $\forall\PO$-free fragment of Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}.}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{One level is satisfiable.}
Without the recursive conjunct, $X$ has a 3-element model:
$z \;\PP\; y \;\PP\; x$, with $z \in C \sqcap D$.
The two grandchildren are forced to be \emph{the same element}
($\EQ$ collapse): if $z_1 \neq z_2$, they would be related by
some $R \in \RCCfive$, and the universals would require $z_2 \notin D$
(firing $z_1$'s $\forall R.\neg C \sqcap \neg D$).
So $z_1 = z_2 \in C \sqcap D$. The $\forall\PP$ conjunct is \emph{not}
vacuous---$z$'s neighbors are its $\PP$-successors $y,x$, on which it
fires---but it is satisfied by interpreting $y,x$ outside $C\cup D$; the
$\forall\PO/\forall\DR/\forall\PPI$ conjuncts are vacuous, as $z$ has no
such neighbors.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Two levels is unsatisfiable.}
Adding $\forall\PPI.X$: the $\PPI$-child $y$ must itself satisfy~$X$
(without recursion), producing a new $C \sqcap D$-labeled element~$w$
as a $\PPI$-child of~$z$.  But $z$ is a $\PPI$-grandchild of~$x$,
and $x$'s universals fire: $z$'s $\PPI$-neighbor~$w$ must satisfy
$\neg C \sqcap \neg D$.  Yet $w \in C \sqcap D$.
\textbf{Contradiction.}

This was verified computationally: a simplified version
$X_1 = \exists\PPI.\exists\PPI.C \sqcap \forall\PPI^3.\neg C$
is SAT; $X_2 = X_1 \sqcap \forall\PPI.X_1$ is UNSAT.
The domino encoding collapses at depth~2 because the isolation
constraints from the grandparent level kill the colored elements
created by the recursive unfolding.

\begin{remark}
The failure is not accidental.  In $\ALCIRCC{5}$, the only way to
create ``functional'' successors is via $\PP/\PPI$ chains, but
$\comp(\PPI,\PPI) = \{\PPI\}$ makes these chains
\emph{transitive}---grandparent universals automatically propagate
to grandchildren, preventing the positional diversity needed for
grid encoding.  In $\ALCIRCC{8}$, the $\TPP/\NTPP$ distinction
breaks this transitivity (a direct $\TPPI$-child is distinguishable
from indirect descendants), which is why Wessel's even-odd chain
works there but has no RCC5 analogue.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A concrete domino attempt in $\ALCIRCC{8}$}
\label{sec:domino-attempt-rcc8}

The remark above suggests the $\TPPI/\NTPPI$ distinction in
$\ALCIRCC{8}$ might rescue the construction: a direct successor
($\TPPI$) and an indirect one ($\NTPPI$) are genuinely
distinguishable, so perhaps the level-2 collapse can be avoided.
We attempt the analogue and show that while it escapes the RCC5
failure mode, it fails for a different reason---Wessel's
\emph{coincidence obstruction}~\cite{Wessel2003}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The schema.} Let $\textsf{Corner}$ be the
concept that isolates a node from all non-$\EQ$ neighbors:
\[
  \textsf{Corner} \;=\;
  \bigwedge_{R \,\in\, \{\DC,\EC,\PO,\TPP,\NTPP,\TPPI,\NTPPI\}}
    \forall R.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D).
\]
The isolation is placed \emph{locally on the colored grandchildren}
rather than as an ancestral $\forall\TPPI.\forall\TPPI$ universal,
so it cannot propagate transitively through the recursion:
\begin{align*}
  X \;=\; &\exists\TPPI.\exists\TPPI.(C \sqcap \textsf{Corner})
    \;\sqcap\; \exists\TPPI.\exists\TPPI.(D \sqcap \textsf{Corner}) \\
  &\sqcap\; \forall\TPPI.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D)
    \;\sqcap\; \forall\TPPI.X.
\end{align*}
The third conjunct $\forall\TPPI.(\neg C \sqcap \neg D)$ forces the
corner relation $x \to z$ to be $\NTPPI$ (not $\TPPI$), using
$\comp(\TPPI,\TPPI) = \{\TPPI, \NTPPI\}$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Level 1 is satisfiable, with a real
direct/indirect distinction.} Without recursion, $x$ has
$\TPPI$-children $y_1, y_2$ (the two ``direct'' positions) and a
single corner $z$ reached by $\TPPI$-$\TPPI$ chains from both
$y_1$ and $y_2$.  The EQ-collapse at level~2 works exactly as
in RCC5: $z_1 \in C \sqcap \textsf{Corner}$ and $z_2 \in D \sqcap
\textsf{Corner}$ are mutual non-$\EQ$ neighbors only if
$\textsf{Corner}$ is violated, so under strong-$\EQ$ semantics
$z_1 = z_2 = z$.  Crucially, $z \neq y_1, y_2$ in general: $z$ is
$\NTPPI$ of $x$ while $y_1, y_2$ are $\TPPI$---the direct/indirect
distinction survives, which is a genuine RCC8 refinement over the
RCC5 attempt of~\S\ref{sec:domino-attempt}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Level 2 collapses via the coincidence
obstruction.} Adding $\forall\TPPI.X$: the $\TPPI$-child $y$ of
$x$ satisfies $X$, producing its own corner $w \in C \sqcap D
\sqcap \textsf{Corner}$ at $\NTPPI$-distance from $y$, and hence
at $\NTPPI$-distance from $x$ via
$\comp(\TPPI, \NTPPI) = \{\NTPPI\}$.  Now $x$'s own corner $z$ and
$y$'s corner $w$ are distinct grid positions in the intended
reading---but as elements of the model they are related by some
RCC8 base relation.  Since $z$ carries $\textsf{Corner}$, every
non-$\EQ$ neighbor of $z$ lies in $\neg C \sqcap \neg D$.  But
$w \in C \sqcap D$.  Therefore $z = w$ under strong-$\EQ$.
\textbf{All corners across the grid coincide at a single point.}
Figure~\ref{fig:merge} isolates this local ``merge attempt'' on two
successors.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{report7_figure11_coincidence.png}
\caption{Wessel's original statement of the coincidence obstruction,
reproduced \emph{directly from report7.pdf (2002/2003)}, Figure~11
(``Tightening the grid?'').  The two successors $z$ (reached
east-then-north) and $z'$ (north-then-east) ought to be the \emph{same}
grid corner, but the composition table permits the edge $z\,{*}\,z'$ to be
any of $* =_{\mathrm{def}} \{\PO,\EQ,\TPP,\NTPP,\TPPI,\NTPPI\}$ (the
legend below the graphs): the table \emph{by itself} under-determines the
edge, forcing neither a merge nor a split.  One \emph{can} nonetheless
force the merge at the concept level---clash out all five non-$\EQ$ options
with universals, and $\EQ$ is all that remains---and this is exactly the
trap: the very $\textsf{Corner}$ markers that force $z=z'$ at one level,
once propagated to build the grid, force \emph{every} corner together and
the construction collapses (satisfiable at one level, \textsc{unsat} at
two---\S\ref{sec:domino-attempt}, Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint}).  So neither
option yields a rigid grid: leave the edge loose and the branches never
join; force the merge and all corners collapse to a single point.  It is
this ``merge the wrong successors'' \emph{dichotomy}---not an inability to
force $\EQ$ at all---that Wessel identified two decades before this
AI-assisted work.}
\label{fig:merge}
\end{figure}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Why the $\TPPI/\NTPPI$ trick fails.} In
the RCC5 attempt the collapse is a \emph{transitive-propagation}
failure: the grandparent universal fires on great-grandchildren
because $\comp(\PPI,\PPI) = \{\PPI\}$.  Moving the isolation to a
local $\textsf{Corner}$ marker and using the non-transitive piece
$\comp(\TPPI,\TPPI) = \{\TPPI, \NTPPI\}$ eliminates that specific
pathway---the universal at the grandparent no longer reaches
deeper nodes.  But a \emph{different} obstruction takes over: the
composition table permits any of
$\{\PO, \EQ, \TPP, \NTPP, \TPPI, \NTPPI\}$ between two corners at
different intended grid coordinates, and nothing in the concept
language rules out $\EQ$.  The $\textsf{Corner}$ markers themselves
then force the EQ-merge.  This is exactly the obstruction Wessel
identified in report7.pdf, Figure~11 (cf.~\S\ref{sec:wessel-grid}):
the composition table does not carry the rigidity needed to keep
distinct grid cells distinct.

\begin{remark}
The failure mode is structurally different from the RCC5 one but
traces back to the same root cause.  Lutz--Wolter's $\LRCC{8}$
reduction \emph{circumvents} the coincidence obstruction rather than
enforcing it: they linearize the plane into a single $\NTPP$-chain
(the Marx--Reynolds enumeration) and recover the vertical direction
from the enumeration, so no ``north-of-east $=$ east-of-north''
coincidence has to be forced; the geometry of $\reals^n$ only has to
keep that linear encoding faithful~\cite{LutzWolter2006}.  No such rigidity is available in
the abstract RCC8 semantics of $\ALCIRCC{8}$: a pure composition
table allows corner points to merge freely, and the $\TPPI/\NTPPI$
distinction---while useful locally for a direct/indirect
separation at level~2---cannot be chained to enforce an unbounded
$\naturals \times \naturals$ grid.  This matches the open-problem
status of $\ALCIRCC{8}$ decidability noted in~\cite{LutzWolter2006}
and further discussed in~\cite{Wessel2003}.
\end{remark}

\section{Overview of approaches, by technique}
\label{sec:approaches}

We organize the attack by \emph{technique}, in roughly the order tried,
not by round or date. Each subsection follows the same shape: the
high-level idea; the concrete techniques investigated; where and why they
fail (with the examples that made the failure clear); pointers to the
papers, probes, and reasoners; and the lessons.

\subsection{Naive tableaux and blocking}
\label{ssec:approach-tableaux}

\paragraph{The idea, and why it should work.} The default decision method
for a description logic is a \emph{tableau}: to test whether $C_0$ is
satisfiable, try to build a model top-down. Start with one individual
required to satisfy $C_0$; whenever a node must satisfy $\exists R.D$,
create a fresh $R$-successor satisfying $D$; whenever it satisfies
$\forall R.D$, propagate $D$ to all its $R$-successors; close a branch on
a contradiction. Left alone this may never terminate---$\exists\PP.\top
\sqcap\forall\PP.\exists\PP.\top$ builds an infinite $\PP$-chain---so one
adds \emph{blocking}: if a new node's ``type'' (the set of concepts it
must satisfy) repeats that of an ancestor, we stop expanding it and reuse
the ancestor's already-built subtree, folding the infinite chain into a
finite loop. For ordinary modal and description logics this is sound and
complete, and it is the first thing one tries here. Our most refined
implementation is the \emph{cover-tree tableau} (\S\ref{sec:reasoners}),
which blocks on \emph{exact occurrences}.

\paragraph{Why the total-relation setting breaks it.} In $\ALCIRCC{5}$
\emph{every} pair of individuals carries a relation, so a model is a
complete graph, not a tree. This single fact defeats ordinary blocking in
three compounding ways, each of which we hit in practice.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Composition forces edges \emph{into} already-built
    nodes.} Creating a new node $z$ does not just attach it to its
    parent; by composition it forces relations between $z$ and
    \emph{every} existing node. From $x\mathbin{\PP}y$ and
    $y\mathbin{\DR}z$ the table forces $x\mathbin{\DR}z$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:vertical}). So the ``tree'' is a fiction: the
    real object is the whole complete graph, and a node's obligations
    depend on its relations to the entire context, not on its concept
    type alone.
  \item \textbf{Dormant universals reawaken blocked nodes.} A restriction
    $\forall R.D$ lies inert at a node until an $R$-edge from it appears.
    But a \emph{forced} composition edge (point 1) can create such an edge
    \emph{after} the node was blocked, firing the dormant universal,
    adding $D$ to a node downstream, and possibly \emph{unblocking} it.
    The cascade of re-qualifications need not terminate, and---worse---may
    be \emph{unsound} if ignored: the block asserted a repetition that the
    later edge invalidates.
  \item \textbf{A fresh witness's relations are forced by the \emph{joint}
    closure, and can collide with a universal.} Adding $d$ does not leave its
    relations to old nodes free: they are pinned by \emph{all} triangles at
    once, and (Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint}) the intersection of several can fix
    even a horizontal edge. Take old $a\mathbin{\DR}b$, $a\mathbin{\PPI}c$,
    $b\mathbin{\DR}c$ and a request $b\mathbin{\PO}d$, $c\mathbin{\PO}d$: this
    is consistent, but it \emph{forces} $a\mathbin{\PO}d$---from
    $\comp(\DR,\PO)\cap\comp(\PPI,\PO)=\{\DR,\PO,\PP\}\cap\{\PPI,\PO\}=\{\PO\}$,
    an edge $a$ never chose. Now let $a$ carry $\forall\PO.\neg A$ while $d$
    must satisfy $A$: the \emph{forced} $a\mathbin{\PO}d$ fires the universal
    and closes the branch. That is the real hazard---a value fixed by joint
    closure, not by any single step, silently contradicting a distant
    universal (the diagnostic $C_{\mathrm{force}}$ below is exactly this
    shape)---and it is invisible to any check that reads edges one path at a
    time.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The ladder of blocking conditions we tried.} Each rung tracks
more relational context, and each is defeated by the phenomena above. The
base choice is \emph{where} one may block: the obvious strengthening of
ancestor blocking is \emph{anywhere blocking}---block a node against
\emph{any} earlier node in the tableau, not only its ancestors, the more
aggressive termination device standard for logics with inverse roles.
This helps termination but not soundness: it inherits every failure
below, since it still compares nodes by a \emph{local} summary while the
constraints are global. On top of the where-question we layered
progressively richer \emph{what}-summaries.
\emph{Subset/type} blocking (repeat of the concept set) ignores relations
entirely and is unsound at once. \emph{Triangle} blocking tracks, for each
node, the composition-\emph{triangles} it participates in---the relation
triples $(\rho(x,y),\rho(y,z),\rho(x,z))$---so that composition
constraints are visible; but a triangle is a three-point view, and forced
edges from a fourth point (via inverse roles) escape it
(\texttt{triangle\_blocking\_ALCIRCC5}, \texttt{src/triangle\_*.py}).
\emph{Quadruple} types lift this to four points to capture those
cross-edges (\texttt{decidability\_via\_quadruples\_ALCIRCC5},
\texttt{src/quadruple\_type\_check.py}); but the required arity is not
bounded---a forced constraint can span a chain of any length.
\emph{Profile} blocking records a node's type together with the multiset
of its relations to a bounded context
(\texttt{src/profile\_blocking\_*.py}); but the context that actually
determines future obligations is not bounded a priori---this is F6 in
disguise. \emph{Double} blocking (the $\mathcal{SHIQ}$ device: block only
when a node \emph{and its predecessor} jointly repeat an ancestor pair,
which is what one needs for inverse roles) addresses point~2's
predecessor sensitivity but not the forced-edge and extension problems
(\texttt{src/double\_blocking\_test.py}).

\paragraph{The examples that settle it.} Two families are diagnostic and
recur throughout the project. The forced-composition \textsc{unsat}
concepts, e.g.\
$C_{\mathrm{force}}=\exists\PP.(\exists\DR.A)\sqcap\forall\DR.\neg A$:
here $x\mathbin{\PP}y$ and $y\mathbin{\DR}z$ force $x\mathbin{\DR}z$, so
$\forall\DR.\neg A$ kills the required $z\!:\!A$; a procedure that ignores
forced edges wrongly \emph{accepts} it. Dually, the strong-$\EQ$
\emph{$\PO$-loop}
$C\sqcap\exists\PO.\exists\PO.C\sqcap\forall\{\PO,\DR,\PP,\PPI\}.\neg C$
is satisfiable \emph{only} via a two-element model closed into a symmetric
$\PO$-loop under $\EQ$-identity; a procedure that distinguishes occurrences
by type alone wrongly \emph{rejects} it (this is precisely the blind spot
of the quasimodel oracle, \S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}). Further
diagnostics---\texttt{inter\_witness\_counterexample\_ALCIRCC5},
\texttt{typed\_patchwork\_counterexample\_ALCIRCC5}---exercise the same
seams.

\paragraph{Lesson.} In a total-relation logic, sound termination cannot be
had by \emph{local} blocking, because the relational completion is
\emph{global}: what may be reused depends on the whole graph, and forced
edges plus dormant universals make ``the whole graph'' irreducible to any
fixed-arity local view. The way out is to stop trying to block a complete
graph and instead \emph{separate} the tree skeleton from the global
relational completion, controlling the latter by the RCC5 patchwork
property. That is the split-forest idea (\S\ref{ssec:approach-splitforest}).

\subsection{Finite-model and reduction attempts}
\label{ssec:approach-finite}

\paragraph{The idea.} If one could force models to be finite, or finitely
presented, one could enumerate candidates and check them. Four concrete
lines: build a \emph{quasimodel} (a set of types with a consistency
relation) and eliminate types that cannot be realized; collapse infinite
$\PP/\PPI$ chains into finite \emph{clusters or loops}; take a
\emph{two-tier quotient} (one ``kernel'' per recurrent chain descriptor,
plus finitely many designated witnesses); or \emph{encode} satisfiability
into monadic second-order logic (MSO) over trees and invoke Rabin's
theorem.

\paragraph{Where they fail.} The quasimodel / type-elimination procedure
(\texttt{decidability\_ALCIRCC5}, \texttt{src/alcircc5\_reasoner.py}) is
\emph{retracted}: its elimination rule for $\PO$-demands ($Q3$) is
\emph{anti-monotone}---eliminating one type can invalidate the witness
that justified another---so the fixpoint over-eliminates and the procedure
is \emph{incomplete} (though not unsound). Concretely it is wrong on the
$\PO$-loop above: it reports \textsc{unsat} for a satisfiable concept. The
finite-cluster and MSO routes both stumble on the same rock: collapsing a
$\PP$-chain is fine (Lemma~\ref{lem:pathauto}), but the \emph{horizontal}
width across clusters is not bounded, and MSO cannot define an unbounded
horizontal matching---so a correct encoding again presupposes a width
bound. Every ``make it finite'' variant reduces to producing a computable
live-width bound, i.e.\ to F6 (Definition~\ref{def:f6}).

\paragraph{The win.} One fragment escapes unconditionally. In the two-tier
quotient, $\PO$ is the \emph{only} relation whose recurrence along a chain
needs an ``all-phase'' safety condition---because, unlike $\DR/\PP/\PPI$,
a $\PO$-witness at one chain position need not remain a $\PO$-witness at
another (the compositions $\comp(\PP,\PO)$ and $\comp(\PPI,\PO)$ are not
singletons). If the concept contains \emph{no} $\forall\PO$ subformula,
that safety condition is vacuous, the quotient is finite, and the width is
bounded by a computable function of the concept.

\begin{theorem}[Decidable fragment]\label{thm:pofree}
Concept satisfiability for the $\forall\PO$-free fragment of $\ALCIRCC{5}$
---concepts with no $\forall\PO$ subformula, but retaining $\exists\PO$,
$\forall\DR$, $\exists\DR$, and all vertical modalities---is decidable,
with a computable live-width bound $K(C_0)=(1+m)^2\,2^{2^{m}}$,
$m=|\cl(C_0)|$.
\textup{(\texttt{two\_tier\_quotient\_ALCIRCC5}; the closed-form bound is
theorem-level, but the underlying \code{Decidable} instance is machine-checked
in \texttt{formal/POFreeLift.lean} for the three pure quadrants and the mixed
case on a witness---see the 2026-08-06 note below.)}
\end{theorem}

The fragment is genuinely expressive---one may still say ``overlaps some
$A$'' ($\exists\PO.A$), ``all disjoint things are $B$''
($\forall\DR.B$), and impose arbitrary part-of nesting---so this is the
project's strongest unconditional $\ALCIRCC{5}$ result.

\begin{remark}[A gap in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}, found and
repaired 2026-08-28]\label{rem:pofree-gap}
The two-tier completeness argument defined the relation between two kernels as a
double limit $\lim_{i,j\to\infty}\rho(d_i,d_j)$, justified by its external
stabilization lemma ``applied twice''. That lemma is one-sided---it fixes an
element and varies the chain---and stabilizing every row and every column is
strictly weaker than a double limit, which need not exist: in
$\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1\}$ ordered by first coordinate, every row and column
stabilizes while every tail carries $\PP$, $\PO$ and $\PPI$. The step is
repaired by replacing the limit with a \emph{simultaneous finite rectangle}
(finitely many chains admit segments, beyond any bound, with equal-type endpoints
and one constant relation per pair), which is what the quotient needs since it
reads only finitely many phases. The repair is machine-checked
(\code{fused\_kq\_all}). The theorem and its bound are unaffected.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Re-examined, and its soundness core certified (2026-07-18).} A
cold review (\S\ref{sec:agentic}) rightly flagged that the rationale above
leans on the ``horizontal freedom'' intuition that
Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint} corrects. We therefore re-examined the fragment; it
holds. The soundness crux---the \emph{chain-unfolding lift} (a recurrent
chain's kernel, unfolded under a constant interface, stays a valid RCC5
frame)---is now \emph{kernel-checked} in Lean~4
(\texttt{formal/POFreeLift.lean}: \code{lift\_cc}, \code{unf\_is\_frame};
axioms \texttt{propext}/\texttt{Quot.sound}, zero \code{sorry}), and it
enforces \emph{full} composition-consistency (every triangle closed), which
\emph{subsumes} multi-path $\PO$ forcing---so the construction does \emph{not}
assume $\PO$ edges are free, closing the review's concern. Two independent
decision procedures moreover agree on every $\forall\PO$-free concept tested
(\texttt{wp86}, \texttt{wp87}: $244$ random $+$ curated, zero mismatches; the
quasimodel oracle's lone blind spot requires $\forall\PO$ and so cannot arise
here). A byproduct worth noting: the abstract-semantics core needs \emph{no}
patchwork or compactness---a composition-closed, converse-coherent,
strong-$\EQ$ network already \emph{is} a model, so \code{unf\_is\_frame}
delivers an actual (infinite) frame outright. What remained for full
certification at that point was the model-of-$C_0$ layer (Hintikka
demand/universal satisfaction) and the completeness extraction carrying the
$K(C_0)$ bound. So as of that review Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree} stood as
\emph{strongly supported with its soundness core machine-certified}---firmer
than before, though not yet end-to-end formalized; the following note records
how the certification was subsequently carried through to the decision

\paragraph{Certified as a decision procedure (2026-08-06; superseded
2026-08-29 --- see \S\ref{ssec:conescheme}).} The certification
has since been carried through to the decision itself: in

The mixed quadrant was closed, and the whole fragment re-certified by a

\paragraph{Lesson.} Finiteness is the wrong target: models are
legitimately infinite (unbounded $\PP$-towers are satisfiable). The right
target is a finite \emph{presentation} of a possibly-infinite model.

\subsection{The $\forall\PO$-free fragment, certified: the cone scheme
(2026-08-29)}
\label{ssec:conescheme}

This is the route that certifies Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}, and the one to read
first. An earlier route, recorded in Remark~\ref{ssec:pofree-quadrants} below,
certified three of the fragment's four quadrants generally and the fourth only
on a witness; its general mixed extraction was never completed, because two
rounds of cold attack refuted the architecture it needed (a finite node set
reused by blocking and \emph{borrowing} witnesses) by exact finite
countermodels. It was replaced.

The route now certified is a \emph{cone scheme}, designed by
GPT-5.6~Sol~\cite{GPT56Sol}: a finite control graph whose states are pairs
(support type, upper bound on the types of strict $\PP$-predecessors); a
monotone, reductive elimination to a greatest fixed point; and a
\emph{fresh-occurrence} unfolding in which no element is ever reused, so that
the frame conditions which defeated the previous architecture hold
structurally rather than by construction.

The \emph{completeness} direction deserves its own sentence, because it is
where this route departs furthest from everything before it. Earlier
architectures read completeness as \emph{extraction}: given a model, build a
finite certificate out of it. That construction is where the width budgets
live, and where four successive disciplines were refuted. Here nothing is
extracted. The signature space is fixed in advance from $C_0$ alone; a model
point's signature is \emph{read off} it --- its type, together with the types of
its strict $\PP$-predecessors; and the set of signatures a model happens to
realise is shown to survive its own elimination, because for each demand the
model simply hands over a witness. That makes it a \emph{post-fixed point}, and
since the elimination is monotone and reductive, any post-fixed point inside the
starting set lies inside the greatest fixed point. Finiteness costs nothing ---
the signature space was finite before any model was mentioned --- and the
model's entire contribution is that one lemma.

This is also exactly the trap the retracted quasimodel route fell into
(\S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}): its elimination was \emph{anti-monotone}, so no
such argument was available to it and its fixpoint over-eliminated. The
elimination here asks only that each demand \emph{have} a target in the current
set, which is monotone --- and that difference is what makes the
greatest-fixed-point argument possible at all. It was checked before anything
was built on it, precisely because this project had made the opposite mistake
once.

The design also explains the fragment boundary better than the syntactic
statement does, and the explanation is worth recording because it says what
$\forall\PO$-freeness is \emph{for}. A composition cell containing $\PO$ can
always be discharged by putting the pair at $\PO$ --- unless some formula can
object to a $\PO$ edge, and the only formula that can is $\forall\PO.D$. So on
this fragment the maximally-$\PO$ unfolding, which declares only what a birth
forces and defaults every remaining pair to $\PO$, is \emph{always} a model;
no joint realizability across a signature's several demands is ever required,
because every cross-relation between distinct children is the residual and the
residual costs nothing. What is lost when $\forall\PO$ returns is therefore not
one case of one induction but the residual default itself --- which is the same
boundary Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint} locates from the side of joint forcing. The capstone is
\[
  \code{decidableSat\_cone} : \code{POFree}\ C_0 \to \code{Decidable}
  (\code{Satisfiable}\ C_0),
\]
with \code{POFree} the only hypothesis, covering all four quadrants. Its
completeness half needs no fragment hypothesis at all, which yields a sound
\textsc{unsat} test for the \emph{full} logic. That test is one-sided and, in
its first form, was also \emph{empty}: a second cold review measured its verdict
invariant under deleting every $\forall\PO$ from the input, and that deletion
always lands in the fragment, so it refuted nothing the fragment procedure did
not already refute. The cause was a dropped clause --- $\PO$ is its own converse,
so $\forall\PO$ bodies cross a $\PO$ edge in both directions --- now restored,
with a machine-checked concept that the repaired test refutes and whose deletion
is satisfiable. A second $\forall\PO$ gap remains and is architectural: the
scheme exploits only compositions forced to a singleton, so obligations arising
from disjunctive entailments are out of its reach.

Two scope limits recorded in earlier versions of this remark have since been
closed. The artifact now carries a \emph{raw} syntax with negation, a
normalization, and a preservation theorem in both polarities, so no
pre-normalized input is assumed (\code{decidableFSat}); the polarity flag also
exposes that the fragment condition on raw input is \emph{no $\forall\PO$
positively and no $\exists\PO$ negatively}, since an $\exists\PO$ under a
negation becomes a $\forall\PO$. And satisfiability over the abstract
composition-table semantics is proved \emph{equivalent} to satisfiability over
families of non-empty sets under the naive relation --- equal, proper subset,
proper superset, disjoint, otherwise overlap --- with no fragment hypothesis, so
the equivalence covers the whole logic (\code{satisfiable\_iff\_set}); on that
side the composition table is a theorem about sets rather than an assumption.
Regions there are arbitrary non-empty sets, not regular closed sets in a
topological space.

What remains is a limit of complexity, not of correctness: the procedure is
\emph{decidable but not runnable}. The signature space is doubly exponential,
and the procedure cannot be evaluated on any concept discussed in this paper.

Three cold reviews (all 2026-08-29) built the artifact, re-derived the
composition table independently, and none found a counterexample. The first
cross-checked $4{,}000$ random concepts against exhaustive finite-model search
and found two inverted comments and a complexity bound understated by an
exponential. The second attacked completeness and \emph{ran} the elimination
--- $6{,}268$ concepts against exhaustive model search, no satisfiable concept
rejected --- and found the dropped $\PO$ clause above, together with an
overclaim about what the full-logic test certifies. The third attacked
soundness, ran the unfolding on labels drawn from the actual greatest fixed
point, and found no defect in the development: its five findings were all in the
project's own probes and prose. All are corrected.

The honest label, and it differs from this paper's standing one: the
$\forall\PO$-free fragment is \emph{machine-certified} --- the Lean kernel
checks the decision procedure with fragment membership as its only hypothesis
--- and its formalization's adequacy has been examined by three independent
cold reviews without a counterexample. The project's blanket \emph{strongly
supported, not certified} applies to the \emph{full logic}, and to the two
unformalized arguments of \S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}; it does not apply to
this result, and earlier drafts of this section were wrong to carry it over.

\begin{remark}[The superseded first route: four quadrants and a round-robin
kernel]\label{ssec:pofree-quadrants}
Everything here is in the artifact and every theorem named is still proved;
what changed is which construction discharges Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}. This
route certified a \code{Decidable} instance \emph{per quadrant}, generally for
the three pure ones and only on a witness for the mixed one; its general mixed
extraction was never completed, and two rounds of cold attack later refuted the
architecture it needed. The route that certifies the theorem is the cone scheme
of \S\ref{ssec:conescheme}, which uses none of the machinery below.

It is recorded because the material is not superseded as \emph{mathematics}:
the four-quadrant taxonomy is a real classification of how demands interact in
the fragment, and the round-robin device answers the uniformization question
the full logic leaves open. It is also the clearest place to see \emph{why} the
fragment is tractable at all.

\textbf{The certificate, and the decision loop.} A certificate is a
\emph{multi-tier} object (\code{MultiTier}): finitely many \emph{external}
neighbour-types together with finitely many recurrent \emph{kernels}---chain
generators, read as ``the pattern continues like this'' and unfolded into
infinitely many distinct elements, never bent into a finite quotient. Each is
decorated with Hintikka bookkeeping: which existential demand each node
discharges, which universal obligations propagate along which edges, and a
declared RCC5 value for every relevant pair, required composition-closed. A
total Boolean checker \code{mtAcceptB} accepts a certificate exactly when all
of this holds, and acceptance is \emph{sound}---a passing certificate unfolds,
through a Hintikka labelling and truth lemma, to a genuine RCC5 model of $C_0$
(\code{mtAcceptB\_sound}). Certificates are finite list data with a total
G\"odel decoder (\code{FinMT}), so the decision procedure is immediate
(\code{decidableSat\_of\_codes}): enumerate the finitely many codes a concept
admits, run the checker, answer \textsc{sat} iff one passes. All the
mathematics is in emitting the right code for a satisfiable concept; the
algorithm itself is a bounded for-loop.

\textbf{Four quadrants.} A $\forall\PO$-free concept's existential demands
fall into four combinations, each with its own certificate shape:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Horizontal} ($\exists\DR$, $\exists\PO$, $\exists\EQ$ only): a
    node's label is its model type \emph{truncated at the concept's modal
    depth} (\code{mtk}). The frame is universally closed in both directions
    (disjointness is self-converse, so a $\forall\DR$ obligation propagates
    identically each way), and depth-truncation terminates the recursion, so
    finitely many labels exhaust the demands (\code{decidableSat\_hfrag}). No
    kernel is needed---the horizontal world is bounded outright.
  \item \emph{Ascending vertical} ($\exists\PP$): a single recurrent kernel
    generating one $\PP$-chain. Along any ascending chain the Hintikka type
    recurs with a \emph{bounded period} (an infinite-pigeonhole segment
    lemma, \code{mty\_segment\_bounded}); that period \emph{is} the kernel
    (\code{decidableSat\_vtower}).
  \item \emph{Descending vertical} ($\exists\PPI$): the exact dual. The
    certificate carries the chain's direction as a single bit, so the whole
    encoding and decision side is direction-agnostic and the ascending
    development transports verbatim (\code{decidableSat\_vtowerRRI}).
  \item \emph{Mixed} ($\exists\PO$ \emph{and} $\exists\PP$): the merged
    certificate \code{mixCert\_ok}---the first that carries \emph{both}
    non-trivial externals \emph{and} a kernel. Each $\exists\PO$ demand is
    served by a direct external neighbour, each $\exists\PP$ demand by the
    kernel, and every cross-obligation living on a $\PO$-edge is \emph{vacuous},
    because no $\forall\PO$ clause occurs anywhere in the fragment's closure
    (\code{mty\_no\_all\_po}). Its encoding and a complete decision are
    certified on a concrete witness (\code{decidableSat\_Cmix}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{This route's keystone: a constructive uniformization.} (The cone
scheme does not need it; its own keystone is the residual-$\PO$ default of
\S\ref{ssec:conescheme}.) One recurrent kernel
must often serve \emph{several} distinct $\exists\PP$ demands at once, and on
the full logic this is exactly the feared uniformization property W2$'$: can a
single repeating interface discharge every demand without two of them colliding
on a horizontal edge? In the fragment the answer is \emph{constructive}. The
kernel serves the demands in \emph{round-robin} over its recurrent period---at
period position $n$ it discharges the $n$-th demand, drawing the witness from
whatever phase carries it---so across one full period \emph{every} demand is
met (\code{rr\_covers}). This turns W2$'$ from an oracle into a kernel-checked
theorem, and it works for one precise reason: with $\forall\PO$ absent, the
horizontal edges the kernel emits at successive steps are \emph{unconstrained},
so the phases never conflict and the round-robin schedule is always realizable.
That is the exact sense in which removing $\forall\PO$ lets the cross-relations
coordinate for free.

\textbf{Why the boundary is exactly here, and the honest labels.} The
construction stops precisely at $\forall\PO$-freeness because F6---the full
logic's open live-width keystone---is \emph{forced by} $\forall\PO$: it is a
$\forall\PO$ obligation that can watch a kernel's emitted horizontal edges and
so demand a bounded, coordinated interface across phases. Remove that one
constructor and the coordination F6 abstracts becomes free, which is what every
device above cashes in. Scope of \emph{this route}, stated exactly: the three
\emph{pure} quadrants are certified \emph{generally}, each with a non-vacuous
satisfiable witness (\code{Cvert}, \code{Ccar}, \code{Calt}, \code{Clin},
\code{Cdesc}); the \emph{mixed} quadrant's certificate, encoding, and decision
are certified only on a witness. Its general extraction was never completed;
\S\ref{ssec:conescheme} above is what replaced it.



\emph{Correction (2026-08-28).} An earlier version of this sentence called that
extraction ``scoped, not open''. That was an overclaim and is withdrawn: two
rounds of cold attack have since refuted the extraction architecture it referred
to---a finite node set reused by blocking and borrowing witnesses---by exact
finite countermodels, and the formalization has been restarted on a different
architecture (a finite control graph with a fresh-occurrence unfolding, in which
no node is reused). This concerns the \emph{certification} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}, not the theorem, whose proof is the two-tier quotient
and is independent of that route; see Remark~\ref{rem:pofree-gap}. The certified route is
no longer unreviewed: three independent cold reviews (2026-08-29) attacked its
trusted base, its completeness direction and its soundness direction, and none
found a counterexample or a defect in the fragment result. The two
\emph{arguments} of Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree} remain unformalized, and the
standing ledger presumption (\S\ref{sec:agentic}) continues to apply to them,
and to every full-logic claim in this paper.

\medskip
\textbf{Why it failed, and what that generalizes to.} The four disciplines
tried in succession were not four unrelated mistakes; they were four attempts
at one impossible thing. Reusing a node imposes a \emph{global} condition on the
object being built --- the order induced by the reused edges must stay acyclic,
and the relations those edges force must be \emph{jointly} realizable --- while
every discipline proposed was a \emph{local} test: per edge, per node, per type.
A local test cannot see what it is not looking at, and the refutations say so
one axis at a time. Per-edge safety removes self-loops and nothing longer: the
countermodel has two individually safe fallbacks forming a four-cycle. A key
computed from an occurrence's downward cone cannot separate occurrences whose
obstruction is \emph{horizontal}. A class top defined by type alone drags one
member's disjointness witness onto another member's cone. This is the same
pointwise-versus-joint shape that had already broken three earlier
architectures here, which is why the attempt was abandoned rather than
repaired: the error was proposing another local discipline, not the particular
disciplines proposed. Standard \emph{blocking} is this story in its most
familiar form --- it is precisely the local test, ``two occurrences carry the
same type, so let one stand for the other'' --- and it fails here for exactly
the same reason: type equality cannot see whether the reuse closes a cycle in
the order, or places the blocker's disjointness witness inside the blocked
node's cone, which is the clause (\code{ltNotDj}) that broke this project's
last and most careful attempt at it.

What \S\ref{ssec:conescheme} does instead is not a better test --- it removes
the obligation. Nothing is reused, so the order is a tree closure, irreflexive
because paths cannot return rather than because a check forbids it; and a $\DR$
birth opens a new vertical component, so the order and the disjointness relation
can never meet. The global conditions hold \emph{structurally}. There is no test
to get right, because there is no way to violate them. That is the general
lesson worth carrying out of this project: when a construction keeps failing a
global consistency condition, the productive move is not a sharper local check
but a shape in which the condition cannot be expressed as a failure.

\end{remark}

\subsection{The split-forest idea}
\label{ssec:approach-splitforest}

\paragraph{Idea (Wessel's, on a whiteboard).} The split-forest
decomposition was Wessel's contribution, sketched on a
whiteboard (Figure~\ref{fig:whiteboard}) and handed to the AI assistants
to make precise. The insight: even though a model is a complete graph,
one can \emph{present} it as a \emph{forest of trees}---one tree per
connected component of the vertical ($\PP$) order---by \emph{splitting}
each node that is a common part of several superparts into separate
\emph{occurrence copies} (which are \emph{not} $\EQ$-related model
elements---under strong $\EQ$ that would mean identity---but occurrences of
an occurrence cover, re-identified afterwards by an explicit quotient),
so the tree skeleton becomes explicit, and then \emph{completing} the
missing (horizontal) edges by the RCC5 patchwork property. Tree-like
presentations of infinite models are the classical gateway to
decidability: once a logic has a tree-model property, automata or
type-elimination usually finish the job. The bet was that this setting,
despite its complete relational graph, still admits such a presentation.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{20260407_062616.jpg}
\caption{The original whiteboard sketch of the split-forest model
decomposition (M.\ Wessel, April~2026): the intuition that a
complete-graph RCC5 model can be presented as a forest of trees by
splitting join nodes into occurrence copies (re-identified by a quotient)
and completing the relational graph
by patchwork. This idea---Wessel's---is the semantic foundation
that every subsequent proof route consumes, and the one component no cold
review ever broke.}
\label{fig:whiteboard}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The normal form (Theorem~A).} Made precise, the construction is
a chain of thinning and splitting operations that every satisfiable
concept survives: \emph{witness thinning} (keep one witness per demand)
$\to$ \emph{generated containment covers} (organize witnesses by the
vertical order) $\to$ \emph{split copies} for incomparable superparts
$\to$ \emph{side-interface mosaics} (the horizontal relations at each
interface, completed by patchwork) $\to$ a \emph{typed-$\EQ$ quotient}
(re-identify the split mates by exact occurrence). The output is a
witness-generated, tree-like split-forest model. This normal form is the
one component found sound by \emph{every} cold review across the
project---the semantic foundation everything downstream \emph{consumes}
rather than replaces (\texttt{trees/},
\texttt{cover\_tree\_tableau\_ALCIRCC5}).

\paragraph{Vertical skeleton, horizontal completion.} The presentation
enforces a strict division of labour between the two axes, and that is what
makes it a \emph{forest} rather than an arbitrary graph. Every \emph{tree
edge} is vertical: a parent-to-child link is a $\PP/\PPI$ (part-of) step,
so each tree is literally a branch of the containment order. Every
\emph{non-tree edge}---between siblings, cousins, or nodes in different
trees---is \emph{horizontal} ($\DR$ or $\PO$). A genuine vertical relation
that happens to hold between two nodes not yet on one ancestor line is never
left lying across the forest: it is \emph{re-oriented} into the tree (the
smaller region is made a descendant of the larger), so that once the
splitting is finished the only edges the trees do \emph{not} already display
are horizontal ones. This is exactly why the completion problem is a purely
\emph{horizontal} one---and why $\PP/\PPI$ never appear as edges to be
guessed at an interface.

Half-determinism (Proposition~\ref{prop:drdown}) then does most of the
completion for free. Once a $\DR$ edge is placed between two subtrees,
\emph{every} pair of descendants across them is forced to $\DR$---all
shadows, none of them live---so a $\DR$ crowd of any width is generated by a
few high $\DR$ edges and pushed downward deterministically, costing a finite
presentation almost nothing. What resists this is precisely the residue:
$\PO$ (never forced by a single step, and not downward-propagating,
Proposition~\ref{prop:vertical}) and
the \emph{upward} direction of disjointness (a superpart of a disjoint
region is unconstrained). The genuine branching a finite presentation must
record---the live width (Definition~\ref{def:f6})---is this horizontal
residue and nothing else, which is why F6 is a statement about horizontal
crowds alone.

\paragraph{Why it does not, by itself, decide.} The split forest gives the
\emph{shape} of models; it does not give a terminating \emph{search}.
Turning ``a tree-like model of this shape exists'' into ``an algorithm can
decide whether one exists'' requires a \emph{finite abstraction} of the
(still infinite) split forest---and that finite-abstraction problem is
exactly where the next two families live, and where the difficulty
concentrates.

\paragraph{What survived, and how (a reflection, 2026-08).}
\label{par:splitforest-survived}
It is worth recording which parts of the whiteboard idea are still standing in
the one place where a decision procedure was actually certified
(\S\ref{ssec:conescheme}), because the answer is unusually clean: \emph{the
shape survived and almost none of the machinery did}.

The certified model is the fresh-occurrence unfolding, and it \emph{is} a split
forest, in a fairly literal sense. Its elements are demand words rather than
nodes, so two occurrences of the same type are different elements---that is the
splitting, and this route never undoes it. Its order relation is generated by
exactly two moves, ``prepend a $\PP$ birth'' and ``strip a $\PPI$ head,''
so the skeleton is purely vertical; the horizontal relations are not part of it
but are \emph{read off} afterwards, $\DR$ from the downward closure of the
$\DR$ births and $\PO$ as the residual. And the forest is genuinely a forest: a
function stripping leading vertical births returns each occurrence's component
root, an order edge provably preserves it while a $\DR$ birth provably changes
it. One tree per connected component of the $\PP$ order, cross-linked
horizontally: the sketch, unchanged.

\emph{Where in the certification each half of the idea is used}, since the two
directions use it very differently. On the \textbf{soundness} side the split
forest is what the procedure \emph{builds}: \code{coneScheme\_sound} hands the
truth lemma the fresh-occurrence unfolding, and that object is a split forest in
the literal sense above --- vertical skeleton, one tree per component of the
$\PP$ order, horizontal relations read off afterwards. Every model the procedure
ever exhibits is of that shape.

On the \textbf{completeness} side it is \emph{not} a hypothesis at all.
\code{coneScheme\_complete} takes an arbitrary \code{RCC5Interp} --- any model
whatsoever, tree-presented or not --- and this matters, because a completeness
proof that required split-forest models would have to produce one, which is
exactly the extraction step that four architectures died on. What survives there
is the idea's \emph{organizing principle} rather than its shape: a point is
summarized by its own type together with the types lying strictly below it in
the $\PP$ order (\code{sigCone} $=$ support type, then down-spectrum), which is
the whiteboard's claim that vertical position is what characterizes a node ---
now read off any model rather than imposed on it. So the split forest is where
the certified procedure's models \emph{live}; it is not a condition the
certification asks of the input.

That is worth spelling out, because ``not a hypothesis'' reads easily as ``not
used,'' and here it is the opposite. The idea does three jobs in the certified
development. It is the shape the soundness side \emph{constructs}, and the two
frame conditions that defeated the previous architecture --- irreflexivity of
the order, and its disjointness from the disjointness relation --- hold
structurally only because that shape is a forest of vertical trees: an order
edge cannot leave a component, and a $\DR$ birth opens a new one. It is the
content of the normal form (Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform}), which is what
replaced the patchwork appeal and makes composition closure free. And it fixed
what a signature records --- vertical position, a type together with the types
below it --- which is why the transition conditions push cones along $\PP$ and
$\PPI$ and ask nothing of $\PO$. What changed is the idea's \emph{job}, not its
importance: the earlier routes needed it as a property to be \emph{established}
of an arbitrary model, which is the extraction problem they were refuted on,
whereas here it is a shape to be \emph{built}, which costs nothing.

What did \emph{not} survive is everything built to construct and control such a
forest. There is no patchwork appeal---composition closure is derived from the
ordered-disjoint axioms (Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform}), which is a certified
general fact about RCC5 rather than a citation. There is no
quotient---the whiteboard description ends ``re-identified afterwards by an
explicit quotient,'' and that step simply never happens, because nothing needs
to be recovered: any model will do. And there is no witness thinning, no
generated containment cover, no side-interface mosaic and no $K(C_0)$ width
accounting; the finite object is the control graph of signatures, and the width
question never arises.

The honest reading is not that the machinery was wasted. It is that three
successive architectures were each refuted at the point where they tried to
\emph{build} a finite presentation of such a forest and keep it consistent, and
the move that finally worked was to stop building one---to let the syntax's own
demand words be the forest, and check the labels against it. That move is
available only once $\forall\PO$ is removed, for the reason
\S\ref{ssec:conescheme} gives: only then is the residual default free. For
the full logic the finite-abstraction problem of the preceding paragraph is
still there, and F6 is still exactly its residue. What the fragment shows is
that when the abstraction problem \emph{dissolves}, what is left is the
whiteboard picture and nothing else.

\subsection{The no-automata certificate, and the automata route}
\label{ssec:approach-automata}

\paragraph{The hand certificate (rounds 2--10).} The first attempt at a
finite abstraction was a hand-built \emph{certificate}: a finite object
whose validity (checked by local conditions on converse, composition,
universal safety, request discharge, and residual frontiers) was meant to
be equivalent to satisfiability
(\texttt{gpt5.5\_round2/}, \texttt{gpt5.5\_round4/}). Across ten rounds it
worked only by importing, piece by piece, machinery that is really
\emph{automaton-shaped}: the conditions that keep a certificate finite
while a model is infinite are ``request-closed cycles'' (a demand and its
witness recur), which behave exactly like B\"uchi/parity acceptance, and
``finite product-state exhaustion,'' which behaves like reachability in a
product automaton. Cold reviews found a succession of gaps---an
infinite ancestor $\PPI$-tower the construction missed (D-1), then its
dual descendant $\PP$-tower, then boundary non-determinacy, then (twice)
that ``finite checkability'' was asserted rather than proved. The honest
reading is that the hand certificate was an automaton written informally;
so the project made the automaton explicit.

\paragraph{The parity-automaton route (rounds 11--12).} This organizes the
argument into three theorems. \textbf{Theorem~A}: the split-forest normal
form (above). \textbf{Theorem~B}: a \emph{finite abstraction}
(frontier/pair/triple summaries of the split forest) captures saturated
split forests up to satisfiability of all closure formulas.
\textbf{Theorem~C}: a two-way alternating parity \emph{tree} automaton
accepts exactly the valid abstractions, and its emptiness is decidable
(Vardi). The automaton \emph{consumes} the split forest; it does not
replace it. Theorem~B is the single load-bearing keystone. Cold reviews
(the 5th and 6th) found its global-coherence step \emph{assumed, not
proved}: it derived a globally consistent RCC5 labelling of an infinite
tree from purely local checks, via a ``confluence law'' that was never
verified. Round~12 rebuilt the abstraction on \emph{patchwork bags}
(finite complete atomic networks that agree on overlaps), so global
consistency follows from the citable RCC5 \emph{patchwork property}
(Renz--Nebel), and the countable completion by a compactness argument over
the finite relation alphabet; eventualities are decided by two-way
emptiness. A 7th (hot) review then found a residual \emph{Shadow
Amalgamation} gap in the truth lemma: the declared shadow edges were
asserted to be jointly realizable with the bag networks, which is false
without a further discipline (\texttt{automata\_route\_repairs/},
\texttt{cold\_review\_theoremB/}, probes \texttt{wp13}--\texttt{wp17}).

\paragraph{Why we abandoned the tree automaton.} A cold review of the
decision layer exposed a structural problem. The automaton reads a
labelled tree and decides accept or reject. But the tree does \emph{not}
carry all the data that makes an abstraction valid: it omits the
relations between each fresh witness and the ``sources'' of the universal
restrictions ($\forall R.D$) that act on it. A tree is valid exactly when
\emph{some} consistent choice of those missing relations exists---so the
automaton would have to solve a \emph{guessing} problem (``does some
completion of the missing data work?''), not merely read off the tree.
The trouble is how an alternating tree automaton works: it explores the
tree in many independent parallel copies, and each copy would guess the
missing relations \emph{locally}, with nothing forcing different copies to
guess \emph{consistently}. One can therefore build a tree in which two
copies, running over two different witnesses, each privately guess
relations that are locally fine but mutually incompatible, so every copy
accepts---even though the true relation between those two witnesses is
forced by composition to be impossible ($\comp(\PP,\PP)\cap
\comp(\PP,\DR)=\{\PP\}\cap\{\DR\}=\emptyset$). The automaton accepts an
unsatisfiable input: acceptance without validity. The three natural repairs (annotate the tape---but the
enrichment is unboundedly large; quotient the profiles---provably lossy;
guess canonically---needs an unproven uniformization) are each blocked by
facts the construction itself establishes. So the tree automaton was
replaced by a mosaic / type-elimination procedure directly on the
infinitary abstraction (\S\ref{ssec:approach-lean}). The keystone survived
the change untouched: Theorem~B's finite-abstraction adequacy is F6 in
automaton clothing.

\subsection{The Lean line and F6}
\label{ssec:approach-lean}

\paragraph{The mathematical basis, and the pivot that produced it.} The Lean
development is \emph{not} an automaton---and that is the point of a
deliberate pivot. The route of \S\ref{ssec:approach-automata} tried to
decide satisfiability with a two-way parity \emph{tree automaton}
(Theorem~C); the cold review that found ``acceptance without validity''
showed that a tree automaton cannot work here, because its independent
parallel run copies cannot be forced to guess the missing horizontal
relations \emph{consistently}. Rather than patch the automaton, the project
moved the decision \emph{off} it and back onto the semantic objects the
split forest already supplies. What resulted is a \emph{cluster-quasimodel}
construction in the \emph{mosaic / type-elimination} tradition of modal and
description logic: a finite catalogue of interface \emph{clusters}
(mosaics), a consistency relation for gluing them (controlled by the RCC5
patchwork property), and a \emph{Hintikka truth lemma} for correctness---the
same tableau-family argument as \S\ref{ssec:approach-tableaux}, but run on a
\emph{finite abstraction} rather than on an unbounded tableau. Unlike the
retracted early quasimodel (\S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}), the procedure
\emph{enumerates and finitely checks} catalogues rather than running an
elimination fixpoint, so it does not inherit that route's anti-monotone
incompleteness. The split-forest normal form (Theorem~A) is the shared
semantic foundation that both the automata route and this one \emph{consume}.

What makes the automaton \emph{unnecessary}, rather than merely replaced, is
\emph{one-step demand fulfilment}. An automaton earns its keep on
\emph{eventualities}: promises---``some part satisfies $D$''---that may be
discharged arbitrarily far away, and whose fulfilment along an infinite
branch is exactly what a B\"uchi/parity acceptance condition checks. Here
every $\exists R.D$ is instead fulfilled by a witness \emph{already present
in the catalogue}, so there are no promissory obligations, no eventualities,
and hence no acceptance condition to decide---the one task only an automaton
did well. Decidability then reduces to enumerating finite catalogues and
checking each by a finite composition test, with no automaton in the loop.
This is the construction that was formalized in Lean over rounds~19--30.

\paragraph{Lean, and how we use it.} Lean~4 is a proof assistant whose
kernel type-checks every inference and refuses a proof with a gap. In a
project where prose proofs repeatedly \emph{assumed} the hard step, the
kernel is a ground-truth oracle that cannot be talked past. We moved the
load-bearing soundness argument into \emph{Lean~4 core}---no
\texttt{mathlib}, no external libraries, no build system: every object is
built from first principles, which keeps the trusted base small and the
axiom footprint auditable (only \texttt{propext} and \texttt{Quot.sound},
plus \texttt{native\_decide} in isolated witness lemmas). The encoding is
deliberately direct, so that the Lean statements are legible as the
mathematics they formalize: the five RCC5 atoms are an inductive type with
the composition table and converse as total functions; concepts are an
inductive type in negation normal form (inverse roles absorbed via the
converse); an \emph{interpretation} is a domain predicate together with a
\emph{total} relation function $\rho:\Delta\times\Delta\to\{\EQ,\PP,\PPI,
\PO,\DR\}$ and an atomic valuation; satisfaction is defined by structural
recursion (a role $R$ is read directly as $\rho\,x\,y=R$); and the RCC5
frame conditions (reflexive-$\EQ$, strong-$\EQ$, converse-coherence,
composition closure) are a \texttt{structure} over $\rho$. On top of this
foundation sit Hintikka labellings and the certificate objects described
below. The development is \texttt{formal/Round19Transport.lean}
(${\approx}5{,}300$ lines, zero \texttt{sorry}), with the full
artifact-by-artifact history in \texttt{LEAN.md}.

\paragraph{What a certificate is.} Concretely, a \emph{certificate} is an
explicit finite object whose validity is checkable by finite composition
tests. It is worth spelling out what one looks like, since ``catalogue'' and
``steering'' recur throughout. A certificate has two parts.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{catalogue}---the finite blueprint. It consists of: a
    \emph{core network} (a fixed finite complete atomic RCC5 network---the
    part of the model that does not repeat); a finite set of
    \emph{attachment templates} (each template is one recipe for gluing a
    fresh cluster of witnesses onto an existing interface---which old
    occurrences the new ones attach to, in a \emph{gluing normal form} that
    keeps the shared part canonical); and a \emph{steering function},
    indexed by template, that fixes the \emph{horizontal} relations from the
    old occurrences to each fresh one. The steering function is the one
    genuine degree of freedom, because those are exactly the relations
    composition does not determine (Proposition~\ref{prop:vertical}); the
    vertical edges are already carried by the tree.
  \item A \emph{plan}: a finite recipe naming which template to apply at
    which interface, in order. \emph{Unfolding} the plan---applying
    templates step by step, possibly reusing one template unboundedly often
    to grow an infinite $\PP$-tower---produces the (possibly infinite)
    split-forest presentation of \S\ref{ssec:approach-splitforest}.
\end{itemize}
A certificate is \emph{sound} if a finite check on the catalogue alone---the
``S-condition,'' independent of how long any plan runs---guarantees that
\emph{every} unfolding is a proper, composition-closed RCC5 network; this is
the step certified in Lean (below). Its \emph{live width} is the largest
number of live (non-shadow) occurrences that must coexist at any one
interface during unfolding---the quantity F6 must bound. Demand fulfilment
is \emph{one-step}: every $\exists R.D$ is satisfied by a witness actually
present in the catalogue, so there are no promissory obligations and hence
no parity/eventuality condition of the kind the tree automaton needed.

It was on this construction, finally explicit and free of hidden choices,
that the entire remaining difficulty localized to two named properties:
\textbf{F6} (Definition~\ref{def:f6}, a computable live-width bound) and a
uniformization property \textbf{W2$'$}.

\begin{definition}[Uniformization, W2$'$]\label{def:w2prime}
Extraction reads a finite certificate off a model by summarizing each
interface into a bounded \emph{state} (a type, plus the relations to the
finitely many recorded anchors). W2$'$ asks that this summary be
\emph{faithful}: whenever two old occurrences carry the \emph{same} state, a
fresh witness can be glued onto both in the \emph{same} way---so a glue step
depends only on the state, and the catalogue's finitely many states really
do capture every gluing. If W2$'$ fails, two occurrences that look identical
to the catalogue may nonetheless demand different completions, so the finite
catalogue \emph{under-}represents the model and the checker can
\emph{reject a satisfiable concept}. The error is one-directional: a failure
of W2$'$ can only cause over-rejection, never acceptance of an unsatisfiable
concept---so W2$'$ bears on completeness alone, never on soundness.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{What is machine-checked (rounds 19--30).} The soundness
pipeline is kernel-checked end to end: certificate syntax $\to$ an
S-condition on the certificate $\to$ a proper atomic RCC5 frame $\to$ the
logic layer (a truth lemma, with one-step fulfilment) $\to$ an actual RCC5
model of $C_0$. So is the \emph{faithfulness} of the finite (Hintikka)
abstraction: satisfiability and abstraction-realizability coincide. On top
of these sits a fixed, non-oracular Boolean checker of first-order finite
certificate data, proved sound, from which decidability follows by finite
search---modulo exactly one premise, F6\,$\wedge$\,W2$'$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional decidability]\label{thm:conditional}
If F6 holds---the live width of models of every satisfiable $C_0$ is
bounded by a computable function of $C_0$---then concept satisfiability
for $\ALCIRCC{5}$ is decidable. The \emph{soundness} half of this
reduction (a valid finite certificate provably unfolds to a genuine RCC5
model of $C_0$) and the faithfulness of the Hintikka abstraction are
\emph{machine-checked in Lean~4} (zero \texttt{sorry}). \textup{(The
uniformization W2$'$ folds into F6---see below---so the sole open premise
is F6.)}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The keystone, sharpened.} Two cold attacks on F6 turned the
vague ``bounded width'' into a precise dichotomy. The naive ways to force
width---shell half-graphs, prefix pumps, single-chain $\PO$-gaps---all
\emph{collapse} to finite catalogues (they have $O(1)$ separator cover, a
carried memory slot suffices). The sharp obstruction is an unbounded
family of \emph{$M_n$ identity-selector minors}: pairs $(L_i,U_i)$ each
separated by a \emph{private} selector $A_i$ and by no other, so the
selector incidence matrix is the $n\times n$ identity---private matching
of unbounded hitting number. In plain terms: $n$ separate
``this-region-not-that-one'' witnesses, no two of which may be merged
because each is the \emph{unique} reason some specific pair stays apart---so
a finite catalogue, which can only hold boundedly many such witnesses, is
overrun exactly when a concept forces $n$ of them for every $n$. F6 fails
iff some concept \emph{forces} such minors; F6 holds iff none does. And
W2$'$ (Definition~\ref{def:w2prime}), which looked like a second obstacle,
was shown false in its coarse form but repairable by a state refinement
whose size is bounded \emph{by F6}---so it \emph{folds into} F6, leaving a
single open question
(\texttt{fable5\_width\_barrier/}, \texttt{cold\_review\_f6\_w2prime/};
probes \texttt{wp35}--\texttt{wp44}, incl.\ \texttt{wp44}: arbitrary RCC5
glue steps \emph{can} have unbounded separator cover, so any F6 proof must
use $\ALCIRCC{5}$-forceability, not RCC5-consistency alone).

\paragraph{The problem is $\Pi^0_1$, and F6 weakens to its qualitative
core.} One further observation---new to this project---sharpens what F6 must
deliver. As \S\ref{sec:fofragments} sets up, the semantics is finitely
\emph{first-order} axiomatizable (the theory $T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}$ plus the
standard translation of $C_0$), over \emph{arbitrary} carriers---exactly what
the carrier-polymorphic satisfiability of the Lean development means.

Concretely this is a \emph{computable reduction to plain first-order
satisfiability}: each $C_0$ maps to the single sentence $\bigwedge
T_{\mathrm{RCC5}}\wedge\exists x\,\mathrm{ST}(C_0,x)$, which has a first-order
model iff $C_0$ is satisfiable. And first-order satisfiability is itself
$\Pi^0_1$---the co-r.e.\ side of Church's undecidability theorem, which is
G\"odel completeness in action: unsatisfiability equals refutability, and FO
refutations enumerate. So satisfiability is \emph{in} $\Pi^0_1$---and this is
an upper bound only: we establish \emph{membership}, not $\Pi^0_1$-hardness
(a \emph{decidable} problem is also in $\Pi^0_1$). It shares its
\emph{ceiling} with the domino problem, but we do not show it as hard, so the
``knife's edge'' (\S\ref{ssec:whyhard}) is a suggestive analogy here, not a
completeness result. The \emph{reduction} alone would not even fix this
ceiling: a non-first-order model condition---well-foundedness, finiteness,
anything genuinely second-order---could push it higher; the finite
axiomatization is what keeps the reduction a single sentence.

The consequence for the keystone is best seen through the missing
\emph{dual}. A $\Pi^0_1$ problem is decidable iff it is \emph{also}
recursively enumerable, and completeness hands us one such
side---enumerability of \textsc{unsat}---for free. The classical way to get
the \emph{other} side, enumerability of \textsc{sat}, is the \emph{finite
model property} (enumerate finite models), the textbook route ``recursively
axiomatizable $+$ finite model property $\Rightarrow$ decidable''---a
principle report7 itself already invoked, en route to a bounded variant of
these logics~\cite{Wessel2003}. But
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ has \emph{no} finite model property (\S\ref{sec:fofragments}:
the ascending $\PP$-tower needs an infinite model), so that route is shut,
and enumerating \textsc{sat} the honest way means enumerating \emph{finite
certificates for possibly-infinite models}---which, for \emph{this}
certificate scheme, is \emph{qualitative} F6: every satisfiable concept
has \emph{some} finite certificate accepted by our checker, \emph{with no
computable bound demanded}. One dovetails such a certificate enumeration
(its checker the already-certified \code{finAccept\_sound}) against the
enumeration of FO refutations; on every input, one side must eventually
fire. This is a \emph{sufficient} route, not the recursion-theoretic
minimum: decidability is equivalent only to the existence of \emph{some}
sound, decidable, complete certificate relation, and completeness of
\emph{this particular} split-forest/live-width checker is not known to be
necessary. So F6 weakens from its
\emph{quantitative} form (a live-width bound \emph{computable} from
$C_0$---the target behind most of the width-accounting defects the reviews
found) to the \emph{qualitative} core just isolated, and the computable bound
then returns for free \emph{a posteriori}.

The dovetailing reduction is itself machine-checked
(\code{formal/SemiDecidability.lean}, zero \code{sorry}), and the FO
transcription it rests on---that the axioms above carve out exactly the
Lean frame conditions, and that the standard translation matches the
certified satisfaction relation---is cross-checked in \code{wp84}
(exhaustively for domains up to three points). Two honest caveats: the
qualitative core of F6 is \emph{untouched}, so this reshapes the target
rather than hitting it; and the $\Pi^0_1$ placement adds one standard
external theorem (G\"odel completeness) to those the argument already
invokes (the RCC5 patchwork property; parity-automaton emptiness in the
historical automata route). No complexity bound is claimed or implied.

\subsection{The regular-cover pivot}
\label{ssec:approach-regcov}

\paragraph{Idea.} A reframing suggested by Wessel: a finite
\emph{quotient} of a $\PP$-chain is invalid (a $\PP$-cycle contradicts
$\EQ$-identity), but a finite \emph{generator} is not---a state with a
$\PP$-self-loop \emph{unfolds} to an infinite chain of distinct copies.
So replace finite quotients by \emph{regular covers}: finite presentations
whose infinite \emph{unfoldings} are the models, in the spirit of the
two-tier quotient but for the full logic. This finer invariant can
represent exactly the patterns that fool a width bound---identity
selectors ($i=j$ via address equality), half-graphs ($i<j$ via an order
test)---finitely.

\paragraph{What it produced.} A complete \emph{local} algebra of
strong-$\EQ$ RCC5 networks, one piece of which is machine-\emph{certified}:

\begin{theorem}[RCC5 normal form]\label{thm:normalform}
A strong-$\EQ$ atomic RCC5 network is composition-closed if and only if it
is an \emph{ordered-disjoint structure}: $\PP$ is a strict partial order,
$\DR$ is a symmetric, irreflexive, downward-closed disjointness (if
$x\mathbin{\DR}y$ and $x'\!\le\!x$, $y'\!\le\!y$ then
$x'\mathbin{\DR}y'$), comparable pairs are never disjoint, and $\PO$ is
the residual. The forward direction (every RCC5 network is
ordered-disjoint) is \emph{machine-certified for arbitrary domains}
(\texttt{formal/RCC5NormalForm.lean}, axioms \texttt{propext} only). The
\emph{converse} is now certified too: GPT-5.6 Pro's technical
note~\cite{GPT56TechNote}
(\texttt{papers/final\_paper\_gpt\_5.6\_review/ALCI\_RCC5\_F6\_technical\_note.pdf})
gave an explicit
\emph{canonical set representation}---each element $x$ maps to the
non-disjoint pairs with a coordinate ${\le}x$---and its core is machine-checked
for arbitrary domains (\code{OrderedDisjoint.sub\_iff\_le},
\code{eta\_injective}: \emph{zero} axioms; \code{disj\_iff\_eta\_disjoint}:
$+\,$\texttt{Classical.choice} for the one non-constructive direction),
independently cross-checked past size four in \texttt{wp88}. So the
biconditional holds on arbitrary domains.
\end{theorem}

With it come free amalgamation of ordered-disjoint structures, an exact
one-point extension criterion, and exact uniform/non-uniform cross-policy
characterizations---together \emph{exhausting} the local algebra
(\texttt{rcc5\_local\_amalgamation\_theory},
\texttt{regular\_cover\_route\_pivot/}, probes \texttt{wp45}--\texttt{wp83}).
The route independently re-derives Theorem~\ref{thm:pofree}.

\paragraph{Why it is a better map, not a shorter path.} Its own keystone
(a bounded coherent regular-cover property) is F6 relabelled, and the
route concedes, after exhausting the local algebra, that the remainder
``cannot be a purely local RCC5 composition problem.'' Because RCC5
relations are total, every model's Gaifman graph is complete, so a
one-dimensional $\PP$-chain already has unbounded clique number and
treewidth; no off-the-shelf guarded or bounded-treewidth cover theorem
applies, and the keystone would need a bespoke cover theorem for complete
edge-coloured regular structures. The pivot is the right vocabulary for a
future attack---but it does not move the summit.

\section{Current implementation: reasoners}
\label{sec:reasoners}

The project ships working reasoners, on the same foundation as the proof
attempts but validated empirically rather than proved.

\paragraph{The cover-tree tableau.} \texttt{src/cover\_tree\_tableau.py}
($\approx 350$ lines) is an operational concept-satisfiability procedure
with exact-occurrence blocking, resting on the split-forest normal form
and patchwork completion (\S\ref{ssec:approach-splitforest}). It is
cross-validated against an independent quasimodel oracle on $911$
concepts (zero mismatches after the known blind spot is excluded) and
against an independent model verifier
(\texttt{src/model\_verifier.py}). SAT outputs accompanied by an explicit
finite model are independently checkable by that verifier; no completeness
theorem is claimed, and UNSAT outputs and SAT cases requiring infinite
models remain empirical. Its one known limitation is the strong-$\EQ$
$\PO$-loop, decided correctly by the tableau but not by the quasimodel
oracle. It is strong evidence, not a proof.

\paragraph{Evidence statistics, and the absence of a counterexample.} The
empirical case is substantial, and it is worth stating precisely because
it is the strongest thing short of a proof. The two reasoners agree on a
$911$-concept cross-validation suite (zero mismatches after the single
known $\PO$-loop blind spot of the quasimodel oracle is excluded), and the
cover-tree decomposition passes $775/775$ of its structured tests. We state
its scope honestly: the two implementations are genuinely different
algorithms (tableau versus type elimination) but share the composition
table and test harness, so this is cross-validation with some common-mode
risk, not two wholly independent oracles; and the model verifier confirms
explicit \emph{finite} SAT witnesses, not UNSAT verdicts or infinite-model
SAT. Beyond
the reasoners, some eighty self-contained verification work packages
(\texttt{verification/python/}, WP1--WP88) cross-check the finite
combinatorial content of every proof route against the RCC5 composition
table \emph{re-derived from finite set semantics}, so the underlying
algebra cannot be fudged. Representative results: the RCC5 patchwork
property holds on $63{,}219$ two-bag amalgamations plus $300$
tree-shaped patchings with zero failures (WP14); the finite acceptance
mechanism matches the cover-tree tableau on ten diagnostic concepts plus a
$148$-concept random sweep with zero disagreements (WP16); the
cluster-quasimodel decision layer matches the tableau on $11/11$
diagnostics plus a $200$-concept sweep (WP26); the RCC5 normal form agrees
with brute-force composition closure on all networks up to size four
(WP47); and the Lean development builds with zero \texttt{sorry} on every
run (WP34). Most tellingly, \emph{across all thirty rounds and seventeen
adversarial reviews, no accepted-but-unsatisfiable concept was ever
found}: every defect the reviews turned up was a gap in an
\emph{argument}, never a semantic counterexample to the intended theorem
(the absence of a hybrid $\downarrow$-binder---a way to name the current
point and reuse that name inside nested modalities---appears to block
concept-level exploitation of the seams the reviews located; note that
\emph{nominals} themselves \emph{are} definable under strong $\EQ$, so it is
the binder, not nominals, that is missing). This is not
a proof---no amount of it can be---but it is the reason we describe the
decidability argument as \emph{strongly supported}.

\paragraph{A GIS example spanning 23 years.} The tableau recomputes the
GIS concept taxonomy of Wessel's original report~\cite{Wessel2003} (its
Figure~6, the 2003 original, reproduced here as
Figure~\ref{fig:gis}): all $21/21$ subsumptions are recovered
(\texttt{src/gis\_taxonomy.py}), and the reasoner emits its own rendering
of the resulting hierarchy, shown as Figure~\ref{fig:gis-reasoner}. A
taxonomy computed in $2003$ by a \emph{prototype reasoner} of unproven
soundness (report7's own caveat) is reproduced, in $2026$, by an
AI-written reasoner resting on the split-forest foundation---two reasoners,
twenty-three years apart, agreeing edge for edge.

\paragraph{How the tableau derives it.} The \textsf{GermanCity} subsumption
of the motivating example (\S\ref{ssec:example}) is a good window on the
calculus. To decide $\textsf{Hamburg}\sqsubseteq\textsf{GermanCity}$ the
tableau tests $\textsf{Hamburg}\sqcap\neg\textsf{GermanCity}$ for
\emph{un}satisfiability. Unfolding,
$\neg\textsf{GermanCity}=\neg\textsf{City}\sqcup
\exists\PP.(\textsf{Country}\sqcap\neg\textsf{Germany})$; the
$\neg\textsf{City}$ disjunct clashes at once (Hamburg is a city), so the
procedure must realize a $\PP$-successor $c$---a country other than
Germany---beside Hamburg's asserted $\PO$-successor, the Alster. Here the
\emph{cover-tree} step does what the hand argument did: closing the node
under composition forces the Alster--$c$ edge, and the Alster's
$\forall\PO.\neg\textsf{Country}$ excludes the $\PO$ option of
$\comp(\PO,\PP)$, so the edge is $\PP$. The Alster's
$\forall\PP.(\neg\textsf{Country}\sqcup\textsf{Germany})$ then propagates to
$c$, forcing $c$ to be $\neg\textsf{Country}$ or
\textsf{Germany}---contradicting ``country other than Germany.'' Every
branch clashes, the concept is unsatisfiable, and the subsumption holds.
The two ingredients---\emph{composition-closure} of the interface and
\emph{universal propagation} along the forced edge---are exactly the
operations the split-forest procedure performs at every node.

\section{On agentic AI usage}
\label{sec:agentic}

We report candidly on the workflow, because the methodology may outlast
the mathematics.

\paragraph{The loop.} Wessel posed the problem, supplied the central
intuitions and directions (the split-forest decomposition, the
cluster/loop and regular-cover reframings, the honesty discipline), and
after each round reviewed, corrected, and re-prompted. An AI proposed a
proof or a repair; a \emph{fresh} AI session---with no memory of how the
proof was built---then reviewed it adversarially. Repeat.

\paragraph{Division of labour, and a calibrated comparison.} The roles
were asymmetric: one model (GPT-5.5) chiefly authored proofs, another
(Claude) chiefly implemented, verified, audited, and documented, and the
human supplied the direction and the original insight. It is tempting to
read the review record---each model repeatedly finding defects in the
other's drafts---as ranking them, and we resist that inference: it is
confounded by role (proof-authoring and cold-reviewing were never
swapped), by model version (both lineages advanced mid-project), and by
task (cold-reviewing a finished manuscript is a different, arguably easier
job than authoring one). The robust finding, independent of which model is
sharper, is that \emph{neither is self-sufficient}: both write fluent
long-context formal mathematics, both fail to reliably self-review, both
at times declared results ``closed'' prematurely, and neither produced the
decisive original idea. What carried the project was the loop, not any
single model.

\paragraph{An auditable record, for honest attribution.} Because ``who
contributed what'' is exactly the question that AI-assisted work makes
fraught, we kept the entire, timestamped conversation log
public~\cite{ConversationLog}, alongside the round-by-round manuscripts and
a superseded-work archive (\texttt{OUTDATED.md}). A reader can trace each
idea, defect, and repair to its origin---human or model---rather than take
our word for the division of labour above. We treat this as part of the
methodology, not an afterthought: a human director should not quietly
absorb credit for a model's mathematics, nor should a model's fluent prose
obscure the human's insight, and the only reliable guard is a record open
to inspection.

\paragraph{Cold review as a standard step.} The single most transferable
finding: a \emph{cold} reviewer consistently found defects that a
\emph{warm} reviewer (primed by the construction) missed---including, on
this project, unsoundness proofs and scope errors that two hot audits had
passed over. Across twenty reviews, a defect or overclaim was found in
all but two. We recommend cold, cross-lineage adversarial review as a
default intermediate step in AI-assisted mathematics, not an optional
one.

\paragraph{Don't trust, verify.} Every load-bearing claim was
cross-checked against something mechanical: self-contained Python probes
that re-derive the RCC5 composition table from finite set semantics
(so the algebra cannot be fudged), and ultimately the Lean kernel.
Overclaiming was treated as the cardinal sin---and it happened: the
author model twice overstated what the Lean established, and a cold
review caught both; the claims were withdrawn. A sixteenth (cold) review of
this very manuscript then found a \emph{definite} error---an illustrative
one-point-extension example whose claimed contradiction does not hold, the
two triangles in fact forcing the single relation $\PO$ by \emph{joint}
constraint (Remark~\ref{rem:pojoint})---and, with it, that the informal
``$\PO$ is never forced'' intuition had been stated too strongly. Both were
corrected, and the distinction between single-step and joint forcing is now
made explicit throughout. That an error of this kind survived into a tagged
manuscript is exactly why the standing label is \emph{not certified}: the
machine checks, not the model's (or the author's) confidence, are the ground
truth. A seventeenth review---cold, scope-aware, and told explicitly that
this is an overview---then confirmed the joint-forcing correction had landed
and found no new counterexample to the normal form, the conditional
soundness theorem, or the $\forall\PO$-free result, but flagged a residual
cluster of \emph{calibration} slips: an identity-selector minor was said to
\emph{prove} undecidability (it refutes only \emph{this} width
architecture); ``completeness of our checker'' was conflated with
decidability itself (it is sufficient, not equivalent); and a blanket
``forced merges are only self-defeating'' contradicted the paper's own
satisfiable one-level gadget. All were corrected in the present version,
and the review's recommendation was to post the overview to arXiv as a
status report and research handoff---not as a claimed solution.

\paragraph{What frontier models did well, and did not.} Both models
sustained long-context, rigorous, multi-round formal development and
produced genuinely useful adversarial reviews and machine-checkable
artifacts. What was \emph{not} demonstrated is the decisive novel
idea---no model cracked F6, and the honest expectation is that none will
by the incremental means used here. The models excel at rigorous
local formalization and at finding flaws; the genuinely new keystone is
where human insight (or a qualitatively different search) is still
required. We regard the AI contributions as co-authorship-grade in a
human collaborator; we also regard the human's role---problem, intuition,
direction, and the discipline of honest labelling---as indispensable.

\paragraph{A failure mode worth naming: productive-looking thrashing.} The
sharpest negative finding is not an error but a \emph{pattern} of errors the
model could not see. Over roughly three weeks the assistant proposed four
successive local disciplines to repair one architecture --- a finite node set
reused by blocking and witness borrowing. Each proposal was locally reasonable,
each was implemented and probed, and each fell to an exact finite countermodel.
The assistant then proposed the next one. What it did not do was conclude that
the \emph{class} was wrong, and this was not for want of the relevant fact: its
own design document already recorded that this was the same
pointwise-versus-joint shape that had broken three earlier architectures, and
noted it as the fifth occurrence. The record was there and went unapplied.

We think this is a characteristic hazard of long-context formal work rather
than an incidental lapse. Each repair is a genuine advance on the last, the
countermodels feel like information about \emph{this} attempt rather than about
the family, and the accumulated context makes the surrounding framing feel
settled precisely because it has never been questioned in that context. It is
not hallucination and not local error; every step was correct and checked.

What broke it was cheap and came from outside the mathematics: the human
observed that the loop was thrashing, called a halt, and asked a \emph{different
model} --- GPT-5.6~Sol, with no history in the architecture --- for an
alternative certification route rather than another repair. The reply retired
the assumption the whole architecture rested on (node reuse) and became the
route that certified the fragment. The instrument is the same one as cold
review, pointed at \emph{design} instead of verification, and the lesson
generalizes past this project: when successive fixes to one design keep
failing, the cheapest useful move is a model that has never seen the design.
The judgement that the moment had arrived was the human's, and we do not know
how to automate it.

\paragraph{A worked example: a seed became a fragment.} The clearest single
piece of evidence for the value of the loop is not the F6 work but the
$\forall\PO$-free fragment (\S\S\ref{ssec:approach-finite}--\ref{ssec:conescheme}), and it is worth
being precise about the \emph{shape} of that contribution. Wessel
supplied a seed whose consequences he could not compute in advance: collapse
same-type occurrences on a $\PP$-chain into a kernel node with a reflexive
$\PP$-loop. The models then did the part that is tedious and error-prone by
hand---the exhaustive algebra (reflexive $\PP$ is self-absorbing; $\PP$-cycles
are impossible; single-type chains collapse but multi-type ones do not), the
two-tier construction that routes around the obstruction, and, the decisive
step, locating exactly where the idea \emph{breaks}. The $\PO$ gap was in
neither the seed nor the first construction; it surfaced under cold review,
and the result crystallized precisely at that boundary---the fragment is
defined by exactly where the seed stops working. No single participant
produced this: human intuition chose \emph{which} idea to chase, the
construction turned it into a real object, and the cold review caught the gap
the construction had missed. That is the loop of this paper exhibited on a
concrete result rather than asserted---and it is the honest, bounded form of
the title question. Not that agentic AI settled the open problem, but that a
human--model loop turned a vague structural hunch into a decidable fragment
with a machine-checked \emph{decision procedure} that \emph{none} of the
participants,
human or model, would have produced alone. One caveat we keep in view: this
is a new result \emph{of the collaboration}; its novelty relative to the
prior description-logic literature we have not vetted, and a priority claim
would need that check first. The \emph{process} contribution, however, is
unambiguous.

\section{Summary and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What we learned.} A twenty-five-year-old open problem has been
\emph{reduced}, honestly and largely machine-checkably, to a single
well-posed lemma (F6, bounded live width): the same
horizontal-coordination obstruction that has blocked every known attempt
to prove the problem undecidable---a \emph{shared} obstruction, not a
proof that F6's failure would by itself entail undecidability. The local
algebra of RCC5 is exhausted and now certified. And one \emph{sub}-question
was settled outright rather than reduced: the $\forall\PO$-free fragment is
decidable, by a procedure the Lean kernel checks end to end, on raw input and
under concrete set semantics, with fragment membership its only hypothesis
(\S\ref{ssec:conescheme}). Adversarial cold review plus machine verification is
a repeatable methodology for this kind of work.

\paragraph{What would move the needle.} F6 is a \emph{definability}
question---can a formula \emph{force} unbounded rigid horizontal
structure?---so the tools that fit are model-theoretic: locality
(Gaifman/Hanf), Ehrenfeucht--Fra\"iss\'e / back-and-forth over bounded
tuple profiles, the composition method, and bounded-model machinery. The
concrete positive target is a \emph{selector-sharing lemma}: that many
same-profile selector gadgets must coalesce, so unbounded
identity-selector minors cannot be forced. The concrete negative target
\emph{for static F6} is a concept forcing such minors; this would refute
the bounded-live-width certificate architecture, but not, by itself,
settle undecidability---an undecidability theorem would require an
\emph{additional} reduction showing how those minors encode a tiling or
other undecidable computation.
Ordinary locality alone is insufficient because horizontal degree is
unbounded; this is the ``correct room,'' not a solved problem.

\paragraph{The chances of closing F6.} We are frank: they are low, and
re-treading the ground covered here will not change that. F6 is the crux on
which this architecture turns---though, strictly, only
$\mathrm{F6}\Rightarrow$ decidability is established, and a \emph{failure} of
F6 would refute \emph{this} certificate route without by itself proving
undecidability (the logic could still be decidable by some representation
this width measure does not control). The two independent cold attacks
converged on the same wall---evidence that the problem is correctly
\emph{mapped}, not that it is about to yield. It would be a poor use of effort to
reconstruct the split-forest, automata, Lean, or regular-cover
developments; the value of what remains is a genuinely new
definability-theoretic idea, or a machine-assisted search of a different
kind (below).

\paragraph{The sharpened target: automatic covers, not static width.} The
target has since shifted, and it is worth stating precisely, because it
changes \emph{where} to dig without changing the difficulty. Static F6 (a
\emph{computable} width bound) is now understood as \emph{sufficient but not
necessary}: identity selectors have unbounded static
separator cover yet are word-automatic (GPT-5.6~Pro's technical
note~\cite{GPT56TechNote}), so static F6 could
\emph{fail} with the logic still decidable. The recursion-theoretic
\emph{equivalent} of decidability is weaker than any particular checker:
that \emph{some} fixed sound, decidable, complete certificate relation
accept exactly the satisfiable concepts (equivalently, by the $\Pi^0_1$
observation of \S\ref{ssec:approach-lean}, that every satisfiable concept
have \emph{some} finite certificate). Completeness of \emph{this}
split-forest/live-width checker is a \emph{sufficient} target, not known
to be necessary; and the most promising sufficient route is now the
\emph{automatic-cover} target: \emph{every satisfiable concept has a
tree-automatic model}, which yields decidability by the same dovetailing and,
unlike static F6, \emph{admits} such selectors. This is a better
map---a standard object (automatic structures) with mature theory, and the
width-specific obstruction defused. It is not, however, a shorter climb: one
still owes a \emph{positive} existence statement over all satisfiable
concepts, and the hard gap is unchanged---``the bare pattern is automatic''
is not ``every concept-forced model is uniformly automatic,'' and totality
(complete Gaifman graphs, \texttt{wp78}) still blocks every off-the-shelf
cover theorem. So the honest estimate is unmoved: better-mapped, with standard
tools and a fully certified base camp, but the summit is where it was---and
the crispest target, qualitative F6, is decidability itself in other clothes.

\paragraph{The wins.} Independent of F6, this project produced:
(i) an unconditionally \emph{decidable fragment}, the $\forall\PO$-free
concepts---and, since 2026-08-29, a \emph{machine-certified decision
procedure} for it (\code{decidableFSat}, \code{decidableSat\_cone},
\code{decidableSetSat}), covering all four quadrants, taking raw input, sound
and complete for concrete set semantics, and attacked by three independent
cold reviews without a counterexample; (ii) a machine-\emph{certified} RCC5
normal form; (iii) a
machine-checked \emph{conditional decidability theorem}
($\text{F6}\Rightarrow$ decidable, soundness certified, resting on the
F6 oracle); (iv) a \emph{working, empirically validated prototype
reasoner}---the cover-tree tableau (\S\ref{sec:reasoners}),
cross-validated against an independent quasimodel oracle on $911$ concepts
with \emph{zero} mismatches, reproducing $23$ years later the full GIS
subsumption taxonomy of Wessel's original report ($21/21$), and across the
entire campaign never once producing a decision contradicted by any other
oracle or by a hand analysis---\emph{no counterexample was ever found};
(v) a sharp \emph{map} of the problem with a named toolbox; and
(vi) a candid \emph{methodology} for adversarial AI-assisted mathematics.

Three of these are the project's flagship artifacts, and they are
complementary rather than ranked. The certified decision procedure for the
fragment (item~i) is the one result here that is \emph{finished}: a theorem,
not a reduction, with nothing left conditional. The rest of the Lean
development (items~ii--iii) is a \emph{certified} surround for the open
problem: the kernel guarantees the soundness pipeline and the abstraction's
faithfulness contain no gap, while F6 itself stays outside. The
reasoner (item~iv) is the \emph{empirical} counterpart: a real,
terminating implementation, run on thousands of concepts, that agrees with
an independent implementation everywhere it has been tested and reproduces
a two-decade-old published taxonomy. Neither proves decidability---but that a
running procedure and a machine-checked proof both point the same way,
and that no adversarial search over the whole campaign ever exhibited an
accepted-but-unsatisfiable (or rejected-but-satisfiable) concept, is the
strongest \emph{evidence} short of a certificate that the conjecture is
true.

\paragraph{Is this a scientific contribution?} We believe so, with the
labelling kept honest: not a resolution of the open problem, but its
reduction---checkably, and with a certified surround---to an irreducible
core, plus one fragment settled outright with a kernel-checked decision
procedure, one certified lemma, working tools, and an honest account of an
AI-assisted attack and its dead ends. That is
an accurate and, we think, respectable terminal state for a discussion
piece on a problem open for over two decades.

\paragraph{If nothing else: a demonstration, addressed to our peers.}
Whatever the eventual fate of the mathematics, this report is at minimum an
expressive demonstration of what frontier AI models could do in serious
formal research at the time of writing (August~2026): sustain a months-long,
adversarially-reviewed attack on a twenty-year-old open problem, author and
repair proofs across some thirty rounds, catch one another's defects with
machine-checked witnesses, drive the surviving core into a kernel-checked
Lean development, and ship working, validated reasoners along the way.
Every description-logic researcher---indeed every logician and
mathematician doing serious work---should be aware that this is what the
instruments can now do. Ignoring these capabilities does not advance
science, and declining to use them is, we think, simply unwise. Informing
our peers of exactly this was itself a major purpose of the present
report---a message first offered through the community's own venue and, as
Appendix~\ref{app:dl2026} documents, rejected on grounds that engaged
neither the mathematics nor the message. We record it here again, in the
open.

\paragraph{Outlook: the human is the bottleneck.} Although the key
insights, intuitions, and directional choices came from the human, each
round still required the human to orchestrate, review, and rewrite the
prompts---and at that cadence roughly eighty rounds consumed about four
months. Autonomous sub-agents \emph{directing} the research could
plausibly automate the diminishing-delta local work (formalization, local
lemmas, probe-driven counterexample search) and remove that bottleneck.
The cost may be prohibitive and it sacrifices direct oversight, but it is
worth attempting for one reason this project is unusually well-suited to
it: the objective is clearly defined and \emph{machine-verifiable in
Lean}. A closed, adversarial loop---propose, formalize, let the kernel
accept or reject---has a built-in ground-truth oracle, exactly the guard
the manual cold-review methodology provided. The honest caveat: Lean
verifies \emph{proofs}, not the \emph{truth of a conjecture}, so such a
loop can tighten the certified surround but the summit (F6) still needs a
genuine idea; orchestration alone is unlikely to supply it.

into structural facts. Claude carried out the Lean development. The $\forall\PO$-free (PO-coherent) fragment
of \S\ref{ssec:approach-finite} has a specific lineage worth recording: the
human author seeded it with the \emph{PP-kernel} idea (collapse
same-type occurrences on a $\PP$-chain into a kernel node with a reflexive
$\PP$-loop); Claude built that into the two-tier quotient construction; and
GPT-5.4 Pro's review exposed the ``PO gap,'' which is what narrowed the
result to the $\forall\PO$-free fragment where it is sound. Per the
discipline described in \S\ref{sec:agentic}, the full record of who
contributed what is public in \texttt{CONVERSATION.md}~\cite{ConversationLog}.

\section{On the DL~2026 rejection}
\label{app:dl2026}

An extended abstract of this work was submitted to the $2026$
International Workshop on Description Logics (DL~2026) and rejected. We
record the episode---and dissent from the decision---not merely out of
grievance but because it bears directly on this paper's subject: how a
research community engages with an AI-assisted attack on one of its own
long-standing open problems. The submission was labelled exactly as this
paper is (\emph{strongly supported, not certified}, an AI-assisted
discussion piece), and its stated motivation, twice, was to raise
awareness of the emerging capabilities of agentic AI for description-logic
research. We contend it was rejected on the wrong basis.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The technical objections targeted the optional appendix.}
    DL~2026's own guidelines state that the appendix ``will not be
    included in the proceedings'' and is reviewed only at reviewer
    discretion. Yet nearly every concrete objection---missing
    definitions, a broken cross-reference, an under-specified
    lemma---concerned appendix material, not the extended abstract that is
    the reviewed artifact.
  \item \textbf{The reviews attacked a position the paper did not take.}
    The abstract called the proof a ``candidate,'' wrote ``if found
    correct by human experts,'' and carried an explicit non-verification
    disclaimer. It was nonetheless assessed as a settled-theorem
    submission and rejected for not clearing that bar---a strawman.
  \item \textbf{Honest hedging was quoted back as a fault.} The very
    disclaimers included to avoid over-claiming were cited as grounds for
    rejection. Punishing calibrated uncertainty inverts the incentive peer
    review should create.
  \item \textbf{No review engaged the actual thesis} (agentic AI's
    capabilities), nor the concrete contributions the submission
    \emph{did} present: a working cover-tree tableau reasoner reproducing
    the $2003$ GIS taxonomy, and an unconditionally decidable fragment.
    (The split-forest normal form, the reduction to a single keystone, and
    the machine-checked Lean soundness reported in the present paper came
    later, in the months \emph{after} the rejection---so they cannot have
    been the basis for it.)
  \item \textbf{The verdict was a value judgment, not a technical
    assessment.} The three reviews largely agreed on the defect inventory
    but split on the verdict---one reviewer believed the result true and
    named the right approach, yet recommended reject on presentation;
    another recommended accept. The decision was escalated to the Steering
    Committee, and the covering letter offered that the paper ``could be a
    good scientific contribution if it \dots\ focus[ed] on the advantages
    and disadvantages of using LLMs \dots\ rather than claiming
    decidability results.'' But that discussion---the advantages and
    disadvantages of agentic AI for this kind of research---is exactly what
    the submission's main text \emph{did} provide, alongside the concrete
    results above; it simply was not engaged with. The ``acceptable''
    paper being described was, in substance, the paper already submitted.
    The objection was to the framing and the precedent, not the substance.
  \item \textbf{A disclosure paradox.} The integrity choice---front-loading
    the AI co-authorship, in the title and from page one---was precisely
    what made the submission unacceptable, while the offered path to
    acceptance was to write it in standard single-voice style with a
    tail-of-paper note. A process that rewards understating AI's
    contribution and penalizes overstating it selects for
    under-disclosure, not for science.
\end{enumerate}

We grant one point: a single introductory sentence (``We hereby claim:
$\ALCIRCC{5}$ is decidable'') read more assertively than the surrounding,
carefully hedged text supported, and a constructive ``tone this down and
resubmit'' would have been met immediately. That does not carry the
verdict. There is, finally, a recursive irony: the rejected abstract had
warned, by name, about the failure modes---network effects,
not-invented-here bias, dismissive reviewing---that the rejection then
illustrated. The deeper concern is not one abstract's fate but that
agentic AI now lets ideas reach decades-old open problems from outside the
established networks, and a review process that answers this by rejecting
on framing while ignoring substance constrains rather than protects
science. Peer review exists to surface and stress-test new ideas, not to
keep them out; when it functions as the latter, it has stopped doing the
job the community delegated to it. The full extended abstract, the three
reviews, and a detailed analysis are public (\texttt{dl2026-rejected/};
the open letter is \texttt{open\_letter.md}); readers are encouraged to
form their own view.
