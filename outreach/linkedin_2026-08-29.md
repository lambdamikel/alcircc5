**Update: one wall came down. The building is still standing.**

Four weeks ago I posted about directing frontier AI models at the decidability
of ALCI_RCC5 — a question I raised and left open in my 2002/2003 doctoral work.
The honest result then: we didn't solve it, but reduced it to a single
well-posed lemma (F6) and found a decidable fragment, argued on paper.

Since then the fragment stopped being an argument and became a theorem a machine
checks.

→ **A decision procedure for the ∀PO-free fragment, in Lean 4.** Fragment
membership is the *only* hypothesis — no unproved premise, no oracle, zero
`sorry`. It now runs on raw input (negation included, with the normalization and
its preservation theorem proved), and satisfiability under the abstract
composition-table semantics is proved *equivalent* to satisfiability over honest
families of sets. So the caveats I'd been listing are gone.

→ **Three independent "cold" reviews, each attacking a different seam** — the
definitions, the completeness direction, the soundness direction. **None found a
counterexample, and none found a defect in the fragment result itself.** They
were not idle: the second caught a genuinely missing clause, which turned out
vacuous on the fragment but not on a full-logic side result I had overstated —
so I withdrew the overstatement and repaired the clause. The third read the
soundness proof adversarially and found nothing in the development at all; its
five findings were all in my own test harnesses and prose. Twenty adversarial
reviews across the project now, a defect or overclaim in all but two, which is
exactly why I trust this as far as I claim it and no further.

→ **The full problem is exactly as open as it was.** F6 is untouched. And the
procedure is decidable but *not runnable* — the search space is doubly
exponential. Decidability and usability are different claims and I'm only making
the first.

**The part I didn't expect to be the story.** For three weeks the AI proposed
four successive fixes to the same architecture. Each one fell to an exact
counterexample. What broke the deadlock wasn't a smarter model — it was noticing
we were thrashing and asking a *different* model (GPT-5.6 Sol) for a different
architecture altogether. Its answer was to stop reusing nodes: keep the finite
structure as a control graph only, and let every existential spawn a fresh
occurrence. Five obligations that had cost four refuted attempts became
structural facts — and the reason it works is that in this fragment, nothing can
ever object to the lazy choice of relation, so the lazy model is always correct.
Put the missing construct back and you lose that default entirely. That is the
boundary, in one sentence.

Still weighing arXiv as a status report and research handoff, not a claimed
solution.
