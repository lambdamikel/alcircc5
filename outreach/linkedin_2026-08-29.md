**Update: one wall came down. The building is still standing.**

Four weeks ago I posted about directing frontier AI models at the decidability
of ALCI_RCC5 — a question I raised and left open in my 2002/2003 doctoral work.
The honest result then: we didn't solve it, but reduced it to a single
well-posed lemma (F6) and found a decidable fragment, argued on paper.

Since then the fragment stopped being an argument and became a theorem a machine
checks.

→ **A decision procedure for the ∀PO-free fragment, in Lean 4.** Fragment
membership is the *only* hypothesis — no unproved premise, no oracle, zero
`sorry`. It now runs on raw input (negation included, with the normalization and
its preservation theorem proved), and satisfiability under the abstract
composition-table semantics is proved *equivalent* to satisfiability over honest
families of sets. So the caveats we'd been listing are gone — all but one, and
it is about cost rather than correctness: the procedure is **decidable but not
runnable**, the search space being doubly exponential. Decidability and
usability are different claims and we are only making the first.

→ **Three independent "cold" reviews, each attacking a different way the
decidability claim could be wrong** — do the definitions actually say
ALCI_RCC5; can the procedure *reject* something satisfiable; can it *accept*
something unsatisfiable. **None found a counterexample, and none found a defect
in the fragment result itself.**
All three found *other* things — which is the pattern here: twenty AI-generated
adversarial reviews across the project now, and a defect or overclaim in all but
two of them.

→ **The full problem is exactly as open as it was.** F6 is untouched, and
nothing here bears on it: the keystone is *forced by* the very construct the
fragment removes.

**The part that turned out to be the story.** For three weeks the AI proposed
four successive fixes to the same architecture; each fell to an exact
counterexample. What broke the deadlock wasn't a smarter model — it was my
calling a halt and asking a *different* one (GPT-5.6 Sol) for a different
architecture altogether. Its answer was to stop reusing nodes at all. Five
obligations that had cost four refuted attempts became structural facts.

Everything is in the repo — paper, slides, Lean, probes, and the full audit
trail including the dead ends: 🔗 [link]

Still weighing arXiv as a status report and research handoff, not a claimed
solution.
