Dear DL Committee,

thank you for engaging with this material - I know, it is difficult, but I do think that you rejected the Extended Abstract on the wrong basis, even violating your own reviewing guidelines (as all "technical" objections are about the Appendix which is completely optional). My intent was sincere - to raise awareness for the emerging capabilities of Agentic AI. The Extended Abstract made this clear, but the reviewers chose to deflect using nitpicking strategies.

Unfortunately, the reviews did disappoint me, and these are my main points:

**1. The reviewers' "technical" objections concern the Appendix, which your own guidelines explicitly exclude.** The DL 2026 submission guidelines state, verbatim, that *"the appendix is optional and will be reviewed at the discretion of the reviewers, but it will not be included in the proceedings."* Yet almost every concrete objection in all three reviews — missing definitions, unclear cover-tree formalisation, "safety for a pair of types," broken references in A.6.4 — concerns appendix content that is not part of the proceedings and is not mandatory review material. Weighting these as rejection grounds is at odds with the rules you set for what is being reviewed.

**2. The reviewers attacked positions the paper did not take.** The Extended Abstract called the proof a "candidate" twice, said "if found correct by human experts," and included an explicit disclaimer that the results are not verified and should not be taken as established. Reviewer A nonetheless treated the submission as a settled theorem submission and rejected it for not meeting that bar. That is a strawman — and a convenient one.

**3. The reviewers then quoted my own epistemic disclaimers back as evidence of irresponsibility.** The transparency hedges I included precisely so as not to over-claim — "candidate," "seems to have succeeded," "if found correct," "should not be taken as established" — were quoted verbatim and used as rejection grounds. Punishing honesty about uncertainty is the opposite of the incentive peer review should create.

**4. No reviewer engaged with the actual thesis.** The Extended Abstract states twice — once in the abstract proper, again in §3 — that the main motivation was to raise awareness of agentic AI's emerging capabilities for DL research. Not one of the three reviews responds to this argument. Instead they reframe the paper as a "call for community validation" of a proof — which it explicitly was not — and reject on the basis of that reframing.

**5. The three reviews disagree on the verdict but not on the defects.** Reviewer C explicitly believes the result is true and names the right approach, then recommends Reject on presentational grounds. Reviewer A admits to having only skimmed the appendix, then rejects on LLM co-authorship rather than technical content. Reviewer B sees essentially the same defects as the other two and recommends Accept. The defect inventory is roughly constant across the three reviews; the verdict difference is a value judgment about AI-assisted work, not a technical assessment.

**6. The decision was escalated to the DL Steering Committee.** Steering Committees do not adjudicate typos or commit-hash pinning. They are called when a submission raises a policy question — here, whether DL is willing to platform an openly AI-generated decidability claim. Your covering letter makes this explicit when it offers to entertain the same content under a re-framed "higher-level perspective on LLMs for formal proofs." The objection was to the framing and the precedent, not the substance.

**7. §4 of the rejected paper warned, in advance and by name, about the three failure modes the rejection now exhibits — network effects, not-invented-here bias, and dismissive reviewing.** The recursive irony is hard to ignore: the rejection is itself a small case study in exactly the phenomena the paper was trying to draw the community's attention to.

The deeper concern is not the fate of one Extended Abstract — that is a small matter. The deeper concern is that this kind of peer-review process — closed-minded gatekeeping, reframing of claims into attackable strawmen, surface-level nitpicking in place of substantive technical engagement — endangers exactly the scientific progress the DL community exists to advance. Agentic AI is now making it possible for ideas and attempted solutions to come from outside the established networks, on open problems that have resisted attack for decades. A review process that responds to that by rejecting submissions on framing while ignoring substance is not protecting science; it is constraining it.

The full Extended Abstract (with post-submission typo corrections), the three reviews, and a detailed analysis are publicly available at https://github.com/lambdamikel/alcircc5 — I encourage interested colleagues to read the material and form their own view.

I remain open to continued dialogue. These questions will not go away with one rejection, and the community will have to grapple with them sooner or later. I would much rather we did so constructively.

With respect,
Michael Wessel
